## The Story

### Chapter 16 — the national big-box retailer (RC)

**KEVEN:** The first anchor account in the Sellers Guide is *the illustrative national big-box retailer.* This is what the guide uses as the worked reference deployment — Store 100 — across the entire RC Practice. Four hundred stores. Conventional-retail margin pressure. Heavy on Microsoft already.

**APEX thesis** — section sixteen point three. The retailer has spent two years on AI pilots, most of them lab demos, none of them in production at store level. The APEX thesis is: *one Service, one store, one quarter, measurable shrink or labour outcome.* That's Wave One.

**Microsoft pillar lead** — section sixteen point four — *Fabric.* Because the retailer's data is fragmented across legacy POS, ERP, WMS, e-commerce. Fabric consolidates first; agents come second.

**The opening demo** — section sixteen point eight — is the *Store 100 reference deployment.* A day in the shift, narrated. The seller walks the buyer through what a store associate sees, what the agent sees, and what the regional ops dashboard sees, all in real time.

**Commercial envelope** — section sixteen point nine — Wave One around 2.8 million dollars Deloitte services, 600K dollars Microsoft consumption.

**JORDAN:** Two-minute observation — *this account is the template.* The big-box retailer chapter is the most worked-through anchor account playbook in the guide. If you've never read an anchor account chapter, read this one first. Every other anchor account chapter borrows its structure.

### Chapter 17 — the global athletic brand (RC)

**KEVEN:** Chapter Seventeen. *A global athletic brand — direct-to-consumer, app-driven.* The buyer profile here is dramatically different from the big-box. This is a high-design, high-margin, brand-led organisation. Snowflake-heavy in their current data state, by the way — which is its own pursuit dynamic.

**APEX thesis** — section seventeen point three. The brand has built world-class customer-facing AI for personalisation and discovery. They have *not* solved the supply-chain-to-store agentic question. The APEX thesis is: *bring agentic AI to the supply-loop and product-availability conversation — leave the customer-facing AI to the brand's internal team.*

**Microsoft pillar lead** — section seventeen point four — *Foundry plus Fabric*, with explicit Snowflake-on-Azure co-existence patterns. We do not displace Snowflake. We compose with it.

**The opening demo** — section seventeen point eight. The demo is *product availability at peak* — a global drop, twelve markets, ninety minutes of demand spike. Can the supply-loop agent reroute inventory across markets in real time?

**Commercial envelope** — section seventeen point nine — Wave One typically 3 to 4 million dollars services. Microsoft consumption is moderate because the brand already has significant Azure footprint.

**JORDAN:** Observation — *this is a deal where the client's Microsoft relationship is already deep.* They have an active Azure footprint, an active Microsoft account team, and their own platform conversations underway. Your job is to enter on Deloitte's merits — the framework, the Practices, the Independence posture — not to insert yourself into their existing Microsoft conversation.

### Chapter 18 — the heritage spirits and beverages manufacturer (RC)

**KEVEN:** Chapter Eighteen. *A heritage spirits and beverages manufacturer.* This is a fascinating account because the business is centuries old, the brand book is sacred, and the AI conversation cannot threaten the brand mystique.

**APEX thesis** — section eighteen point three. The opportunity is *operations and channel intelligence* — distribution, on-premise/off-premise channel dynamics, regulatory compliance across U.S. state-by-state alcohol law. Wave One is *channel intelligence with agentic decision support.*

**Microsoft pillar lead** — section eighteen point four — *Fabric and Purview.* Purview lands hard because alcohol distribution is state-regulated and audit-heavy.

**The opening demo** — section eighteen point eight — *on-premise channel optimisation.* The agent surfaces which accounts in which states are over- or under-performing on a specific SKU, recommends sales-rep visit prioritisation, and emits the regulatory paper trail automatically.

**Commercial envelope** — section eighteen point nine — Wave One 2.5 to 3.5 million dollars services.

**JORDAN:** Observation — *this is a relationship account.* The buyer here will not move fast. Eighteen-month sales cycle is realistic. Don't push for a four-quarter close.

### Chapter 19 — the global health-products conglomerate (HLS / RC)

**KEVEN:** Chapter Nineteen. *A global health-products conglomerate.* And this one is structurally complex — the business spans pharma, medical devices, and consumer health, which means three different Deloitte teams, three different Microsoft AEs, and two potential APEX Practices applying — HLS for pharma and devices, RC for consumer health.

**APEX thesis** — section nineteen point three — *do not lead with a horizontal pitch. Pick one segment. Win Wave One there. Expand laterally in Wave Two.* The recommended entry is *consumer health supply-and-channel*, because it's the lowest-regulator-friction starting point.

**Microsoft pillar lead** — section nineteen point four — depends on segment. For consumer health entry — *Fabric*. For pharma extension — *Purview-first*. Always.

**The opening demo** — section nineteen point eight — *consumer health new-product-launch readiness.* Cross-channel inventory, retailer commitment tracking, claim-substantiation governance, all agentic.

**Commercial envelope** — section nineteen point nine — Wave One 3 to 4 million dollars services. Wave Two could expand to 8-plus million if pharma extension lands.

**JORDAN:** Observation — *this is the most likely lateral-expansion deal.* If you win Wave One in one segment, the path to Wave Two is real. Plan the second-Wave conversation while still in the first.

### Chapter 20 — the global automotive OEM (AXLE)

**KEVEN:** Chapter Twenty. *A global automotive OEM, North America headquartered.* And this is the AXLE flagship account. The buyer here is a CIO with a quality-and-warranty pressure profile and an explicit board mandate to do something material with AI by FY27.

**APEX thesis** — section twenty point three — *the Zero Day Warranty agentic scenario.* The same scenario we built for the Toyota outreach in the Toyota account materials. Connected-vehicle warranty claims joined to factory build records, identifying the cohort-station-tool interaction that drives a warranty cluster. Eight-to-twelve-week investigation becomes minutes-long agent run.

**Microsoft pillar lead** — section twenty point four — *Foundry, with NVIDIA composition.* This is one of the two anchor accounts where NVIDIA is in the architecture conversation from week one — Metropolis at the station for inline vision, RAPIDS for the supplier-cohort analytics. Section twelve point seven and the NVIDIA composition tab in the Architecture document.

**The opening demo** — section twenty point eight — *a single VIN walk-back.* The agent takes a warranty claim, walks back to the build station, identifies the operator cohort and supplier lot, and produces the chargeback evidence package — three minutes end-to-end on stage.

**Commercial envelope** — section twenty point nine — Wave One typically 4 to 6 million dollars Deloitte services. With NVIDIA-co-architecture this can land higher. Three-year value is in the eight-figure dollars range with disciplined value-share construction.

**JORDAN:** Observation — *this is the deal where the four-pillar plus NVIDIA composition is the architecture conversation.* You will be asked about NVIDIA. You should know the NVIDIA stack — Metropolis, DeepStream, RAPIDS, Triton, NIM, Omniverse — at the level of *which one fits which problem,* not just the names.

### Chapter 21 — the global tire and rubber-products manufacturer (AXLE / RC)

**KEVEN:** Chapter Twenty-One. *A global tire and rubber-products manufacturer.* This account sits across AXLE — for the supply-into-OEM relationships and the manufacturing depth — and RC — for the consumer-facing replacement-tire retail-and-service business.

**APEX thesis** — section twenty-one point three — *dual-domain entry.* Pick *supplier-side traceability* with the OEMs *or* *consumer-side service experience* in the retail channels. Don't pitch both in Wave One.

**Microsoft pillar lead** — section twenty-one point four — *Fabric for supplier-side, Copilot for consumer-side.* Different entry, different pillar lead.

**The opening demo** — section twenty-one point eight — depends on which entry. For supplier — *supplier-quality root cause* — the agent walks a defective lot back through the manufacturing process. For consumer — *service-bay diagnostics* — the agent gives the technician a recommendation in twelve seconds.

**Commercial envelope** — section twenty-one point nine — Wave One typically 2.5 to 4 million dollars services. Mid-range for AXLE.

**JORDAN:** Observation — *don't try to be everything to this account.* They will respect a focused Wave One pitch over an ambitious cross-business pitch. The lateral expansion comes in Wave Two — same pattern as the health-products conglomerate.

### Chapter 22 — the global heavy-equipment manufacturer (ICE)

**KEVEN:** Chapter Twenty-Two. *A global heavy-equipment manufacturer.* And this is the ICE flagship anchor account — Industrial Commercial Equipment. The customer here isn't a consumer — it's mining companies, construction firms, and farmers. Aftermarket-economics shape — make the equipment, monetise the parts, service, and refurbishment.

**APEX thesis** — section twenty-two point three — *dealer-network performance acceleration.* The manufacturer has a four-tier dealer structure across roughly fifty countries. The dealer-performance agent diagnoses underperforming dealers, recommends interventions, and feeds back into commercial planning.

**Microsoft pillar lead** — section twenty-two point four — *Fabric.* Because the dealer-data foundation is the bottleneck. Until it's unified, agents don't help.

**The opening demo** — section twenty-two point eight — *the dealer performance walk* — the agent identifies the bottom-decile dealers in a region, diagnoses why, and recommends regional sales actions. The buyer can see their actual dealer landscape on the screen and immediately pattern-match.

**Commercial envelope** — section twenty-two point nine — Wave One typically 3 to 4.5 million dollars services. The dealer-network data foundation is heavy lifting in Wave One.

**JORDAN:** Observation — *this account values the data foundation as much as the agent.* That's because they know they can't sustain agents on the data state they have. Lead with Fabric. Earn the agent conversation.

### Cross-account observations

**JORDAN:** OK. Seven accounts, three patterns I noticed.

Pattern One — **the pillar lead varies, but Fabric is the most common.** Three of seven lead with Fabric. Why? Because anchor-account-scale deals usually have fragmented data state. Foundation work is unavoidable.

Pattern Two — **lateral expansion plans live in Wave Two — but you have to plant the seed in Wave One.** Multi-business clients are most of the anchor accounts. The Wave Two conversation belongs in the Wave One closing meeting.

Pattern Three — **the opening demo varies, but it's always a *specific narrative*.** A day in the store. A drop at peak. A VIN walk-back. A bottom-decile dealer walk. *Never* "let me show you the platform." The Sellers Guide is consistent on this — section X point eight in every chapter has a specific scenario, not a product demo.

**KEVEN:** And one more — *the discovery agenda for every anchor account is between two and three days, structured workshop format.* Section X point seven. The buyer wants the rigor. Bring the rigor.

## Adopt / Hold

**KEVEN:** Adopt versus Hold on the anchor accounts. Jordan, you take Adopt.

**JORDAN:** Adopt — **read the chapter for any anchor account you have any pursuit thesis on, before any other prep.** The twelve-section structure is the most efficient pursuit-prep document I have ever seen in a consulting firm. Strategic context to scenarios in twelve sections, three to four pages each. A pursuit team reading the chapter together in a one-hour kickoff is a two-month head start.

**KEVEN:** Hold. When are the anchor-account chapters *not* the right input?

**JORDAN:** Two cases.

Case one — *if you're pursuing an account that's not on the anchor list.* Then the chapter doesn't apply. The Practice chapter — chapter nine through fifteen — is your input, not Part Four. Don't try to retrofit Big Box Store onto a Mid-Market specialty retailer.

Case two — *if you're more than two quarters out from the opening meeting.* The "extended account intelligence" sections — section X point eleven of each chapter — were written at a point in time. Public signals shift. Decision-maker maps shift. Don't treat the chapter as current intel if it's been a year. Use the framework — refresh the signals.

**KEVEN:** Synthesis?

**JORDAN:** The structure is timeless. The specifics are time-stamped. Trust the structure. Re-verify the specifics.

## Lessons

**KEVEN:** Monday-morning lessons.

One — **identify the anchor account you most likely have a thesis on.** Read its chapter in full this week. Two hours. Worth it.

Two — **build your decision-maker map first.** Section X point ten. The 30-60-90 plan. Knowing *who* you're going to talk to in the first ninety days is more valuable than knowing *what* you're going to pitch.

Three — **for each anchor account you touch, build the opening demo narrative.** Two pages. Section X point eight is the template. Practice it aloud.

Four — **for the AXLE OEM anchor specifically — get fluent on the NVIDIA stack.** Metropolis, RAPIDS, Triton, NIM, Omniverse. Not the marketing — the *fit-for-purpose* mapping. The Sellers Guide deep-dive plus the Toyota outreach Architecture document are the right combo.

Five — **read both Wave One *and* Wave Two scenarios from every anchor account chapter.** The lateral-expansion plan is the difference between a Wave One win and a multi-Wave relationship.
