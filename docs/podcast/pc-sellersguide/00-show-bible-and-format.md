# Show Bible · The APEX Sellers Podcast

This document defines the format, the voices, and the house style for the seven-episode series. Read it before producing or narrating any episode.

---

## 1. The reference

*The Acquired Podcast* (Ben Gilbert & David Rosenthal). What we are borrowing:

- **Two hosts, both expert, not interchangeable.** They have distinct angles. One walks through the mechanics; one steps back to ask "but why does this work *as a business*?"
- **Narrative arc per episode.** Cold open → tension → resolution. Not a list of bullet points read aloud.
- **Recurring segments.** Listeners learn the rhythm. The format is the trust signal.
- **Specificity beats abstraction.** Real numbers, real timestamps, real names. "$4.2M in supplier-lot chargeback recovery" beats "significant warranty savings."
- **Productive disagreement.** The hosts argue. They don't both agree with every claim. When the framework over-promises, somebody calls it.
- **Carve outs.** Personal recommendations at the end. We adapt this to "what to read next from the Sellers Guide."

What we are **not** borrowing:
- The 3–4 hour episode length. Ours are 30 minutes.
- The pure business-history vibe. APEX is operational selling content, not a story about a founder.
- The fundraising rounds / cap table emphasis. Not relevant.

## 2. The voices

### Keven · the practitioner

- VP, Deloitte's Microsoft Technology & Services Practice
- 22+ years on the Microsoft platform; Industrial Products & Services background
- Has been in the room for these deals
- Vocabulary: specific, concrete, slightly informal. "The CIO leaned back and said…"
- Default move: tells the story of *what actually happened* in a pursuit
- Tic: "Here's the thing —"
- Avoids: corporate-speak, "leverage," "synergies," "journey"

### Jordan · the strategist · skeptic

- Strategy partner; background in industrial M&A and platform economics
- Has not lived inside APEX day-to-day — comes at it from "is this actually the right answer for this CFO?"
- Vocabulary: precise, pulls back to first principles, comfortable with finance terms
- Default move: "OK, but a CFO is going to ask…" or "wait, back up — define that"
- Tic: "Let me push on this —"
- Avoids: pretending to know the implementation details Keven knows

### How they interact

- **They use first names.** "Keven, the way you just said that —"
- **They interrupt each other.** Naturally, briefly.
- **They disagree on tactics, agree on strategy.** They both believe APEX works; they argue about *when* and *how* to position it.
- **They don't read scripts at each other.** Even when the dialogue is scripted, it's written to *sound* unscripted.

## 3. The format · every episode

### Section A · Cold Open (90 sec – 2 min)

A scene. One of these patterns:
- **The Meeting** — "Three weeks ago I was sitting in a CIO's office in [city], and the question I got asked was…"
- **The Pager** — "Imagine you're the head of warranty at a global OEM, and at 6:47am on a Tuesday, your dashboard lights up red…"
- **The Deal That Almost Wasn't** — "Last quarter we lost a deal at the discovery stage, and the reason we lost it is the entire reason we're doing this episode…"

Then drop into: "I'm Keven Markham." / "I'm Jordan." / "And this is the APEX Sellers Podcast."

### Section B · Theme Statement (30 sec)

One paragraph. "Today we're going deep on Part [X] of the Sellers Guide — [Title] — and the reason this matters is…"

### Section C · The Story (18 – 22 min)

The body of the episode. Walks the source-guide content as a conversation. Hosts trade off who's leading.

- Keven introduces a concept → Jordan interrogates it → Keven gives the worked example → Jordan reframes for the buyer's perspective.
- Long passages of monologue are broken with "wait, can I jump in — " or "hold on, you just said X — does that really mean…"
- Use real numbers from the Sellers Guide. Never round to "millions" if the source says "$2.8M."
- Anchor every abstract claim to a concrete scenario.

### Section D · APEX Facts (90 sec)

Rapid-fire. The hosts trade quick definitions, numbers, and the one-sentence summaries from the chapter. The audio equivalent of a card stack.

Format:
> **Keven:** APEX Fact #1 — there are four Microsoft pillars. Name them.
> **Jordan:** Fabric, Foundry, Copilot, Purview.
> **Keven:** Two.
> **Jordan:** Two — Wave 1 is six to nine months and lands the first agent in production.
> **Keven:** Three.
> **Jordan:** Three — DCIF, value-share, direct funding. Never ECIF.

Six to ten facts per episode.

### Section E · Adopt / Hold (3 – 4 min)

The Acquired "Bull / Bear" equivalent, adapted. Hosts argue:
- **Adopt case:** When does this part of APEX actually work? Who buys it? Why now?
- **Hold case:** When is this the wrong pitch? What's the qualify-out signal?

Each host takes one side; they don't have to *believe* the side they're arguing — they argue it for the listener's benefit. Then they land on the honest synthesis.

### Section F · Lessons (2 min)

What does a seller actually *do* with this on Monday morning? Three to five concrete actions.

- "Open the Sellers Guide to chapter X."
- "On your next discovery call, ask this question first."
- "When you hear [signal], you're in the right deal. When you hear [other signal], qualify out."

### Section G · Carve Outs (30 – 60 sec)

What to read next.

> **Keven:** My carve out — go read the canonical-schema appendix. Chapter 1.6. It's the kernel of the whole framework.
> **Jordan:** Mine is the procurement defense script in 2.7. Memorise it. You will need it within six pursuits.

### Section H · Sign-off (15 sec)

> **Keven:** That's it for this one. Next episode — [tease].
> **Jordan:** See you there.

## 4. House style

### Numbers and citations

- Every number in audio matches the Sellers Guide exactly. If the source says **440 hours**, the audio says "four hundred and forty hours."
- Currency: spoken as "four point two million dollars" not "four million two hundred thousand."
- When citing a chapter: "section one point six A" (read the numerals normally, not as "one-point-six-A as one word").

### Independence language

The Sellers Guide has a bright-line list of things sellers cannot say. Audio inherits it:

- ❌ Never: "audit," "attest," "audit-grade," "Deloitte will audit"
- ❌ Never: "ECIF" as a positive funding path
- ✅ Always: "audit-ready *evidence package*" (artefacts the client's auditor can use)
- ✅ Always: "DCIF, value-share, or direct funding"

If the script ever drifts into forbidden language, Jordan is the one who catches it — that's part of his skeptic role, and it doubles as an Independence reminder to the listener.

### Anonymisation

Anchor accounts (Big Box Store, Nike, etc.) are referred to by archetype in audio. Example:

- Source: "Nike — global athletic brand"
- Audio: "A global athletic brand — direct-to-consumer, app-driven, Snowflake-heavy in their current state"

Listeners can map back to the named account via the Sellers Guide. This keeps the audio safe for broad distribution without losing the substance.

### Stage directions

Used sparingly. Only when delivery matters.

```
[pause]                ← natural beat
[slight laugh]         ← when one host catches the other in something
[overlapping]          ← brief interruption, both voices for one beat
[reading]              ← when a host reads from a document on the call
```

### Length discipline

30 minutes ≈ 4,800 – 5,200 words at conversational pace (about 165 wpm). The scripts in this folder target that window. If a script runs long, cut the Cold Open or trim APEX Facts — *never* gut the Story section.

## 5. What to do if the source guide updates

The Sellers Guide is a living document. When a chapter materially changes:

1. Identify which episode covers that chapter (see README table).
2. Update the affected segment(s) in the script.
3. Update the "Last updated" timestamp at the top of the script file.
4. If numbers changed (ROI math, capacity sizings), update APEX Facts too.
5. Re-record only the affected segments — the rest of the episode is stable.

---

## 6. A note on tone

This is **not** a marketing podcast. It is a *practitioner* podcast. The implied listener is a Deloitte seller who has to walk into a CIO's office on Tuesday morning and explain APEX without notes, on the technical and commercial merits, independently of any partner-channel positioning. The audio is their preparation.

So: be honest about what doesn't work. Be specific about what does. Sound like two people who have been in the chair and are talking to a third person who's about to be in the chair.

The reason *The Acquired* works is that Ben and David sound like they genuinely care whether the listener understands the business. Carry that ethic.
