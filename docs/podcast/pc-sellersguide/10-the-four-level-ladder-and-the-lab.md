## The conversation

### Picking up from where the weave left us

### Rung one — Level 1 · The Power BI Opportunity Heatmap

**KEVEN:** Start at the bottom. L1. *The Power BI Opportunity Heatmap.*

What it is — *a free gift* the seller offers to a Deloitte industry practice leader. The seller says — *"I'll build you a Power BI Opportunity Heatmap for your portfolio. Five days. Free. You own the .pbix."* The leader almost always says yes. Free gift. Five days. No catch. They own the artefact.

What's in it — *the heatmap shows, for the practice's portfolio of accounts, every place where APEX would compress delivery.* Account by account. Industry by industry. Service by Service from the catalog. Each cell scored on three dimensions — *opportunity size, Microsoft attach potential, Wave 1 readiness.* The leader sees, on a single page, where the highest-value places to start are.

What it costs — *twenty-five to seventy-five thousand dollars* of services revenue at the wedge. Wedge revenue isn't the point. The point is the *conversation it unlocks.*

**JORDAN:** And the deliverables —

**KEVEN:** Two artefacts. The .pbix file — they own it, they can open it whenever. *And* — this is critical — a *one-page summary* identifying the top three Wave 1 candidates from their portfolio. *That summary is the seller's bridge to L2.* It's the thing the seller points to in the next conversation when they say — *"Want to act on the top three?"*

### Rung two — Level 2 · Wave 1 Agentic Services

**KEVEN:** Up to L2. *Wave 1 Agentic Services.* The first real SOW.

What it is — *one use case, one agent, one HITL gate, ninety days, BVA-backed.* The seller has converted the L1 wedge into a specific Wave 1 deal — picked from the top-three list the heatmap surfaced.

What it costs — *five hundred thousand to two million dollars* of services revenue. *Plus* the client's Microsoft consumption — Fabric capacity, Foundry consumption, Copilot licenses — which the client procures directly from Microsoft under their separate enterprise agreement.

What the bundle looks like — *Discover, Design, Build, Deploy.* The Practice Service Bundle. Each engagement has a defined shape — ten weeks of design through deployment, then a thirty-day pilot period before transition to operate. The KPI envelope is set in the BVA. The pilot's success is measurable.

**JORDAN:** And the platforms attaching from L2 —

**KEVEN:** *All four pillars plus Entra.* Fabric for the data plane. Foundry for the agent runtime. Copilot for the user surface. Purview for the trust plane. Entra for the identity plane. *Five of the six Growth Platforms attaching from L2 onward.* Dynamics 365 attaches later as the B2C motion opens.

### The unlock from L2 to L3

**JORDAN:** And the transition from L2 to L3 —

**KEVEN:** *"Wave 1 returned X in ninety days against Y of services spend. Want to scale this pattern across the practice?"* That's the unlock sentence. The math is concrete. The practice leader's option is binary — *do Wave 2 in a different use case, or stop at Wave 1.* Most leaders, having seen Wave 1 land, want Wave 2. The seller has earned the next conversation.

### Rung three — Level 3 · B2B Practice-Wide

**KEVEN:** L3. *B2B Practice-Wide.*

What it is — the practice's *first Agentic Services Center of Excellence.* The seller is no longer pitching individual use cases. The seller is pitching *a multi-Service fleet that the practice operates over multi-year.* Supplier agents. Partner agents. Channel agents. Contract agents. Whatever the B2B portfolio of the practice covers.

What it costs — *five to twenty million dollars* of services revenue annually. This is *expansion revenue* — the same client, growing footprint, predictable cadence.

What attaches — Fabric and Foundry expand in capacity. Plus *Azure Logic Apps* for non-agent integration steps, *Azure AI Agent Service* for the production runtime at scale, *MCP servers* hosted in the client's tenant for the multi-Service tool catalog. The framework material in Episodes Three and Eight applies. The architecture *composes* at L3.

**JORDAN:** And the practice leader at L3 —

**KEVEN:** The practice leader at L3 isn't the same buyer as at L1. At L1 you have a single practice partner. At L3 you have a practice steering committee that includes the CIO, the CCO, the Chief Operating Officer of the practice, the relationship leadership, and the engagement leadership. *The deal is bigger. The committee is wider. The cadence is steering-committee-driven.*

### The unlock from L3 to L4

**JORDAN:** L3 to L4 unlock —

**KEVEN:** *"Your B2B is humming. When do we go to your clients' consumers?"* That's the consumer unlock. The B2B work has earned the trust. The framework has demonstrated it can govern. The natural next conversation is — *the consumer side of the practice's business.*

### Rung four — Level 4 · B2C Transformation

**KEVEN:** L4. *B2C Transformation.* The strategic / displacement deal.

What it is — *consumer-facing agents.* Loyalty agents that personalise winbacks. Service agents that handle the customer interaction at scale. Pricing agents that respond to demand in real time. Returns and complaint agents that resolve in moments. *The L4 portfolio is the practice's consumer-experience future-state.*

What it costs — *twenty to fifty million dollars and up* annually. *Strategic / displacement revenue* — the client is choosing APEX over alternatives that include the major customer-experience platforms.

What attaches — *Dynamics 365 becomes central.* Customer Insights. Sales Copilot. Service Copilot. *M365 Copilot* in customer-facing context. *Fabric Real-Time Intelligence* for the streaming consumer signal. All six Growth Platforms now densely composed.

**JORDAN:** And the buyer at L4 —

**KEVEN:** At L4 the buyer is *the CEO and the board.* The investment is strategic. The timeline is multi-year. The competitor isn't another consulting firm — *the competitor is doing-it-yourself or doing-it-with-a-platform-vendor.* The seller's pitch at L4 isn't *"we can do this."* It's — *"you've seen us do B2B at scale; we're the firm to do B2C with you."* Trust earned from L1 through L3 is what makes L4 the *displacement* deal it is.

### The substrate — the GSSC Lab

**KEVEN:** OK. Underneath all four rungs — *the GSSC Lab.* I want to spend real time on this because it's a structural advantage.

What it is — *a fully provisioned Microsoft-native environment* in Deloitte's tenant. *Always on. Pre-built. Live today.* Fabric F64-plus capacity. Foundry workspace. Copilot Studio. Purview catalog. Entra tenant. Dynamics 365 sandbox. *All running, all wired together, all available to demonstrate.*

The Microsoft Agent 365 framework is wired and active inside the Lab. *Agent 365* is Microsoft's umbrella agent runtime — the integration of Agent Framework, Foundry Agent Service, and the surrounding governance. The Lab demonstrates Agent 365 in production-shaped configuration.

The APEX fleets are installed. Today — *thirty-four RC agents running, thirty TH agents running.* HLS, ER, TMT, and ICE fleets are staged for activation as engagements light them up. *Two Practice catalogs are demonstrable today. The other five are demonstrable within days.*

The *APEX Scenario Matcher* — a decision engine running on Foundry — takes account context and returns matched scenarios from the framework's catalog. *The Matcher is what makes the L1 heatmap and the L2 discovery conversation feel concrete in real time during the demo.*

**JORDAN:** Why this matters operationally —

**KEVEN:** Three operational advantages.

One — *L1 demos in person.* The seller walks into the practice leader's office with a tablet — not a deck — opens the Lab, pre-loaded with the leader's account context, and shows live agents. *Real Agent 365 sessions. Real audit rows in Purview. Real Microsoft consumption profile.* The leader can see what they're being asked to license.

Two — *Wave 1 starts thirty days from yes.* Because the platform substrate already exists, the engagement doesn't spend six to eight weeks on environment provisioning. The Lab serves as the development substrate during Discover and Design phases; the production substrate is promoted into the client's tenant for Build and Deploy.

Three — *the Lab is the Independence-safe demo path.* The Lab runs on *Deloitte tenant with synthetic data.* No client data ever enters the Lab. Promotion to the client's tenant is an L2 motion, with the client's own Microsoft licensing covering the production substrate. *That clean separation* — Deloitte's lab versus client's production — *is what makes the demo posture Independence-clean.*

**JORDAN:** And the seller's say-not-say discipline at the Lab —

**KEVEN:** Three things to say. *"This is Deloitte's lab — pre-built, working today." "We promote from here into your tenant for production." "You're looking at Agent 365 running an APEX edition agent."*

Three things *not* to say. *"This is your environment."* It's Deloitte's. *"This is what you'll license."* The services are licensed from Deloitte; the platform is licensed from Microsoft separately. *"Microsoft built this for us."* The Lab is Deloitte's intellectual property running on Microsoft platforms — never frame the relationship in a way that suggests Microsoft funded or built it for us.

### The Six-Phase Seller Journey

**JORDAN:** OK. The journey across all four rungs — the six-phase model from the Handbook.

**KEVEN:** Right. The seller motion has six phases, mapped to the rungs.

Phase one — *Ready.* The seller's prep before the first meeting. Target practice identified. Top three accounts named. Heatmap candidate scenarios assembled. *No client conversation yet.*

Phase two — *Meet.* The first conversation with the practice leader. The L1 wedge offered. The leader opts in. The GSSC Lab slot booked.

Phase three — *Wedge.* The L1 build. Five days. Power BI heatmap delivered. .pbix handed over. Top-three Wave 1 candidates surfaced.

Phase four — *Sell.* The L1-to-L2 transition. The 30-Minute Functional Discovery conversations. The BVA constructed. The Wave 1 SOW signed.

Phase five — *Scale.* The L2-to-L3 transition. Wave 1 proven. B2B Practice-Wide CoE established. Multi-Service expansion across the practice.

Phase six — *Annuity.* The L3-to-L4 transition. The consumer side opens. The practice becomes a multi-wave annuity. *The Dream realised.*

Each phase has a defined entry and exit. Each phase produces an artefact. The artefacts compound — the L1 heatmap becomes the L2 input. The L2 BVA becomes the L3 baseline. The L3 fleet portfolio becomes the L4 reference set.

### The operational plays that make the journey repeatable

**JORDAN:** And the Handbook has *plays* that make the journey teachable. The 10-Minute Practice Conversation. Objections and Recoveries. BVA in 5 Bullets. The Independence Cheat Sheet.

**KEVEN:** Let me hit each one briefly.

*The 10-Minute Practice Conversation.* This is the seller's *first-touch script* with a practice leader. The seller has ten minutes. Maybe at a partner offsite. Maybe over coffee. Maybe at a conference. The script — *open with the L1 wedge offer, name a specific account or two from their portfolio, mention the five-day timeline, drop the GSSC Lab as the substrate, close with a date request.* Ten minutes. Done.

*Objections and Recoveries.* Thirty common objections, each with a one-paragraph recovery. *"We already have an AI strategy."* Recovery — *"Great — APEX is the framework for rationalising AI portfolios with production governance. Want to see how your existing pilots fit?"* *"This sounds expensive."* Recovery — *"The wedge is five days for twenty-five to seventy-five thousand. The first Wave is BVA-backed — you commit to Wave 2 only if Wave 1 returned."* The Handbook has them all.

*The BVA in 5 Bullets.* A Business Value Assessment compressed to *one slide, five bullets.* The five — *(1) the metric we'll move, (2) the gap today, (3) the agent that closes the gap, (4) the Wave 1 timeline and cost, (5) the return math.* If the BVA needs more than five bullets, the deal isn't ready. The discipline of the five bullets is what produces clear deals.

*The Independence Cheat Sheet.* A one-pager — *say this, don't say that* — for every Independence-sensitive conversation. ECIF is prohibited; DCIF and direct client funding are valid. *"Deloitte's Microsoft Technology and Services Practice"* — yes. *"Microsoft partner-channel co-sell"* — never. Microsoft software contracts directly with the client; Deloitte services contract directly with the client; *Deloitte is not in the software margin.* The cheat sheet is the seller's bright-line guard against Independence breaches.

*One-Page Practice Pitch Template.* The deliverable the seller leaves with the practice leader. One page. The practice's portfolio. The top three Wave 1 candidates. The BVA shape for the top one. The kickoff timeline. *That one page is the artefact the practice leader carries into their own internal conversation.*

### One final disagreement

**JORDAN:** Pushback time.

**KEVEN:** Go.

**JORDAN:** The Sellers Handbook's ladder model assumes *the seller carries the relationship from L1 to L4.* In practice, *the seller who opens L1 is often not the seller who closes L4.* The L1 wedge might be a relationship seller. The L2 Wave 1 might be the engagement partner. The L3 might be the industry practice leader. The L4 might be a CEO-level relationship led by a senior executive partner.

The ladder model is *idealised.* In real life it's *team-relayed.* The handoffs between sellers across the rungs are where the most deals are lost.

**KEVEN:** I agree with this completely. And the *honest* version of the ladder is — *the practice climbs the ladder, the seller team carries the climb.* The L1 seller may not be the L4 seller. But the *practice relationship* persists, and the framework's commercial promise is to that relationship, not to any single seller.

The discipline this requires — *clean hand-offs at each transition.* The artefacts at each phase — the heatmap, the BVA, the L3 charter — are the seller team's hand-off documents. If those artefacts are clean, the next seller picks up cleanly. If they're not, the next seller restarts.

**JORDAN:** And the Sellers Handbook should be more explicit about this.

**KEVEN:** Agree. *The ladder is one practice's climb. The carrier of the climb is the seller team across phases. The hand-offs are real work.*
