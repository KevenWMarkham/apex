# Episode 01 · The pursuit · DMTSP Denver kickoff (M0)

**Builds on:** the design doc and show bible for the LA28 Telco-Agentic Anchor Podcast.

## Cold Open

[soft room-tone, a hotel HVAC unit cycling, a laptop fan]

Early June 2026. The TMT VP is in her hotel room in Denver the night before the DMTSP all-hands kickoff. The Hyatt at 17th and California. A room-service plate she barely touched. The single-page charter the M&P Leader will see in the morning is on her laptop, the cursor blinking at the end of the sequencing paragraph she has rewritten four times. The list of nine Practice Principals she'll brief one-by-one over the next two weeks is in her notebook, in her own hand, in the order she means to call them. TMT first, because TMT is leading. It is a top-down commitment that travels down the org chart faster than she could carry it herself. *"Do we go after this together or not."*

Sequencing rationale that explains why AT&T is the Wave-1 commit and the rest of the Telco field is Wave-2 soft pre-warming. She knows the M&P Leader will read the sequencing paragraph first. ## The conversation

### The Microsoft platform engagement framework

**KEVEN:** Picture the DMTSP Microsoft Platform Lead at her desk the Monday morning after Denver, second coffee, a yellow legal pad open next to her laptop. She is not drafting a commercial sketch. She is drafting the Independence language. The exact phrasing — the canonical sentence — that will travel the next twenty-four months across every MOU, every Principal brief, every anchor conversation, every tracker entry. The phrase she keeps writing and crossing out at the top of the page is the one the whole campaign has to inherit. That pad, that morning, is where the Independence-language discipline for the whole twenty-four months gets set. I want to be slow here, because if we get it wrong at M0 — if she lands on a phrase that drifts even one degree — we are paying for it at M7.

**RASHMI:** Walk it.

**KEVEN:** The rule is one phrase, used the same way every time, on every page, in every brief, in every external conversation. *Microsoft platform engagement.* That is the canonical phrase. The Microsoft platform is the platform Deloitte recommends on the merits — Azure, Foundry, the architecture-center reference patterns, the CAF capacity-planning discipline — and the recommendation stands on technical and architectural grounds. The DMTSP Microsoft Platform Lead's job at M0 is to set the language so that anyone reading a Deloitte page two years from now sees *Microsoft platform engagement* and nothing else. No commercial-instrument language. No funding-vehicle language. No phrasing that implies anything beyond the platform-on-the-merits recommendation. That is the discipline. That is the whole sub-section.

**RASHMI:** Stress test. Why does that phrase matter so much? What happens if it slips?

**KEVEN:** Because Deloitte audits Microsoft. That is the underlying fact, and the auditor-independent posture is what protects every other thing the firm does with Microsoft as a platform vendor. So when we describe what we are doing here, we describe it in language that respects that posture. We do not use language that implies a commercial bundling between Deloitte and Microsoft. We do not use language that implies a coordinated funding play. We do not use language that implies a designated-reseller posture or any kind of commercial alignment. We use *Microsoft platform engagement* because it describes the actual relationship — Deloitte engages clients on the Microsoft platform, recommended on the merits — and nothing more. If the phrase slips — if a draft slides into language that sounds like a coordinated commercial motion — we have created an Independence problem we did not have to create. The National Office hears about it. The Risk Advisory Partner has to walk it back. The anchor's counsel sees the inconsistency and asks why the language changed. None of which we want. So the phrase holds, the same way, every time.

The auditor-independent posture means we stay on the platform-engagement-on-the-merits side of the page. Microsoft's commercial conversation is Microsoft's commercial conversation. The Risk Advisory overlay reviews every Deloitte-authored document before it goes external to confirm the language hygiene held — no drift, no implication of a coordinated commercial motion, nothing that looks like alignment beyond *Microsoft is the platform Deloitte recommends on the merits.*

### The capacity commitments

**RASHMI:** Picture the ML Engineering lead on a Thursday-night call with the AI Institute lead and the D&A Practice lead, the three of them on Teams, screen-share up, and what is on the screen is a napkin sketch — literally a phone photo of a napkin — with A2A Swarm on the left, LangFuse in the middle, the Registry on the right, and a question mark drawn over the top in red ink. The question mark is the inference-cost forecast. None of the three of them have it. None of the three of them can size a bench commitment until they do. AI Institute, D&A Practice, ML Engineering. Why is this the make-or-break sub-section of M0?

**KEVEN:** Because the pursuit doesn't ship without those three. The Microsoft platform engagement gives us the platform headroom. The capacity commitments give us the people who build on top of the platform. And the people problem is harder than the platform problem at M0, because the people problem requires three separate Practice leaders to commit bench capacity for a twenty-five-month window against an anchor that does not yet exist on paper.

**RASHMI:** So the framework. What does the M0 brief to the AI Institute, D&A, and ML Engineering joint conversation actually contain?

**KEVEN:** Four pieces. First, a capacity-planning framework — how many ML engineers, how many D&A architects, how many AI Institute research seats over the twenty-five-month runway, sized by milestone. Second, an inference-cost model — how the runtime costs scale as we move from shadow mode through the first plug-in Channels through Games-day live operations.

**RASHMI:** What's the inference-cost forecast at peak?

**KEVEN:** That's the model's whole job to answer, and the answer drives the next two. Third, an LLM-tier mix recommendation — which tiers of model handle which runtime workloads, because the cost curve flips depending on the tier mix. Fourth, a runtime stack commitment — A2A Swarm for the agent-to-agent coordination layer, LangFuse for the observability and trace surface, the Registry for the agent and capability catalog. All three of those are platform-native to the Microsoft platform engagement frame, all three of them carry inference costs that have to be modeled, and all three of them carry capacity requirements on the people side.

**RASHMI:** Let me name the consequence. If those three commitments don't land at M0 — if AI Institute, D&A, and ML Engineering don't agree to the capacity framework in the next thirty days — what happens in M3?

**KEVEN:** M3 is the first-plug-in milestone. Travel plus Retail plus Media commit. If the capacity framework hasn't landed, M3 doesn't ship on time. Because three Channels commit and we don't have the ML engineers to build the three Channel runtimes against the platform. So the anchor commits in M1, the Home Channel ships in shadow in M2, and then in M3 we have signed plug-in anchors with no build team. That is the failure mode.

**RASHMI:** And the way to avoid it is to land the capacity framework at M0.

**KEVEN:** The way to avoid it is to land the *commitment* at M0, with the framework, on paper, signed by the three Practice leaders. Not a verbal yes. A capacity commitment on paper. The inference-cost model gives them something concrete to commit against — because they can see what a Channel runtime costs to operate, they can size the bench commitment against the cost model, and the commitment becomes a budget line in their own Practice plan.

**RASHMI:** And the LLM-tier mix — because I want to make sure listeners hear this — that is not a tactical decision the engineering team makes later. That is a capacity-planning input at M0.

**KEVEN:** Right. The tier mix determines the inference-cost curve, which determines the runtime budget, which determines how many engineers we can afford to staff against the runtime, which determines the bench commitment. So the tier-mix recommendation is upstream of the capacity ask. You cannot ask for the capacity until you have the tier mix. And the tier-mix recommendation lives in the AI Institute brief, not in the D&A brief.

**RASHMI:** And A2A Swarm plus LangFuse plus the Registry. That is the runtime stack we are committing to for the production runtime?

**KEVEN:** That is the runtime stack we are committing to architect against for the production runtime. A2A Swarm handles the agent-to-agent coordination — the way the Home Channel agent talks to the Travel Channel agent talks to the Retail Channel agent. LangFuse handles the observability — every trace, every span, every prompt, every completion, surfaced so the Risk Advisory overlay can audit what the runtime actually did.

**RASHMI:** And what does an audited trace actually cost AT&T per Adaptive Card?

**KEVEN:** That is the question the inference-cost model has to answer before the production-runtime envelope gets sized — and we'll come back to it. The Registry is the catalog — every agent, every capability, every tool, every Adaptive Card surface, listed and versioned. **RASHMI:** Monday morning. What does the seller do.

**KEVEN:** Monday morning, the AI Institute, D&A Practice, and ML Engineering joint-capacity brief is on the calendar. Two-week target. Same window as the 1:1s. Same window as the platform-engagement coordination conversation with the DMTSP Microsoft Platform Lead. M0 has three motions running in parallel in those two weeks. They all have to land.

### Risk Advisory pre-clear

**RASHMI:** This is the sub-section where I am going to push hardest, because the sequencing argument here is the one most pursuit teams get backwards.

**KEVEN:** Walk it.

**RASHMI:** The instinct — and I have watched this fail — is to treat the Risk Advisory per-anchor Independence pre-clear as a thing you do *after* the BD motion. You go after the anchor. You get the verbal commit. Then you take it to Risk Advisory and ask for the pre-clear. That is backwards. The pre-clear motion runs *concurrently* with the BD motion, not sequentially. It starts at M0. It runs the whole twenty-five months. Every anchor on the list — every named brand on the per-Practice anchor identification — goes onto the Independence pre-clear list at M0 and gets submitted to the National Office in the M0 window.

**KEVEN:** Why does that ordering matter?

**RASHMI:** Two reasons. First, an Independence event during a BD conversation is the most expensive kind of Independence event. If we are halfway through a BD conversation with an anchor and the National Office comes back and says *there is an Independence issue we need to work through,* we have either lost time we cannot get back or — worse — we have created a situation where Deloitte's posture in the conversation gets complicated. Neither of those is good. So we want the pre-clear *upstream* of the BD touch. Second — and this is the part the field underestimates — the National Office moves on its own clock. The pre-clear takes time. If we start the BD motion thinking the pre-clear is a one-week sign-off, we are going to be unhappy. So we submit at M0. We hold the BD motion until the pre-clear lands. We do not flip those two.

**KEVEN:** What would an Independence event cost, in concrete terms?

**RASHMI:** It would cost the anchor. It could cost the whole Channel. It would definitely cost the pursuit-team's standing with the M&P Leader, because the M&P Leader endorsed the campaign in part on the assumption that the Independence posture was clean from the start. So when we run the M0 sub-section that submits the pre-clear list, what we are really doing is protecting the endorsement we just got in the kickoff.

**KEVEN:** And the per-anchor list at M0 — how broad does it go?

**RASHMI:** Every named brand. AT&T at the top, because AT&T is Wave-1. Then the Wave-2 Telco field — T-Mobile, Verizon, Charter, Lumen — because we know they're coming. Then every Channel anchor across all eleven Channels. Walmart, Target, Costco in Retail. American Airlines, Marriott, Expedia, Airbnb in Travel. Toyota, AutoNation, Cox in Automobile. Progressive in Mobility. Sazerac, CVS in CPG and Health. PG&E, SCE in Energy. Kaiser, UnitedHealth in Health. JPMC, Visa, Mastercard in Finance. NBC, Comcast in Media on the ride-along scope. Every name goes on the list at M0, even the ones we will not touch until M3 or M4 or M5. Because the National Office's clock is slower than ours and the field-level cost of a late pre-clear is a Channel we cannot ship.

**KEVEN:** And NBCUniversal specifically. The Olympic-broadcast piece.

**RASHMI:** NBC is the highest-risk door. NBC has exclusive U.S. broadcast rights for the Games. The Media Channel sits ride-along — watch-party orchestration, Peacock cross-promo with disclosure, second-screen Adaptive Cards — and never competitive distribution. The scope-boundary memo for NBC has to be authored at M0, before any external touch. Because if we get the NBC scope wrong — if the Media Channel design strays anywhere near competitive distribution — the entire LA28 motion gets a problem we cannot fix. So the NBC ride-along scope memo is its own M0 deliverable. The TMT VP authors it. The Risk Advisory Partner reviews it. The National Office sees it as part of the pre-clear submission.

**KEVEN:** What does that memo say in two sentences?

**RASHMI:** It says the Media Channel orchestrates the viewer's experience *around* the broadcast — watch parties, social, second screen, cross-promo with disclosure — and never *in place of* the broadcast. NBC's distribution rights are absolute and the Media Channel is additive. That is the memo, in two sentences. The actual memo is longer and the Risk Advisory Partner writes the careful version. But the spine is those two sentences.

**KEVEN:** Monday morning, the Risk Advisory Partner has the pre-clear list and the NBC scope memo on her desk.

**RASHMI:** Monday morning of the M0 window, yes. Submitted to the National Office the same week. The clock starts then.
