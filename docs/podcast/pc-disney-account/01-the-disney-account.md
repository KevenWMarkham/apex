## The conversation

### Why this podcast exists

**KEVEN:** Set up where this podcast sits.

**RILEY:** The Trilogy podcasts cover the framework — Sellers, Services, Deployment. Every Account Team member should have listened to at least the Sellers Podcast. This podcast is *on top of that.* It assumes you know what APEX is, you know the four-level ladder, you know the six Microsoft growth platforms. *We're going account-specific.* Disney as an account.

The reason an account-specific podcast matters for Disney is — *Disney isn't an account where the framework just gets applied generically.* Disney is an account where the framework gets *curated.* TMT Practice has done that curation. Sixteen scenarios. Out of the 723 in the full APEX scenario library. Those sixteen are what we'll walk in this series.

**KEVEN:** And the audience for this podcast specifically —

**RILEY:** The Deloitte Account Team for Disney. Partners. Senior managers. Managers. Senior consultants. Anyone who's going to walk into Burbank, Lake Buena Vista, or one of the other Disney properties on behalf of the account. *Five episodes, two-and-a-half hours, this is your preparation.*

### A century of context

**KEVEN:** OK. Let me try to set up Disney as a *company* for the listener who hasn't been on this account before. Because — even if you've heard the brand your whole life — *the company itself has structural features that aren't obvious from the outside.*

**RILEY:** Take your time.

**KEVEN:** Disney was founded in 1923. Hundred years old. Three years from now will be the actual centennial of the Mickey Mouse character. *Disney is an old company by every measure that matters.* That matters because — *old companies have institutional muscle memory.* They've been through the transition from silent film to talking film. They've been through the transition from theatrical to broadcast. They've been through the transition from broadcast to cable. They've been through the transition from cable to streaming. *Each transition reshaped the company.* The current transition — to agentic AI — is *one of those transitions.* And Disney's institutional memory of *how to transition* is one of the most valuable assets the company has.

The seller who walks in with a *novel-technology-from-Silicon-Valley* posture is going to be met politely and ignored. The seller who walks in understanding that *Disney has been here before, and APEX is a framework for *this* transition specifically* — that seller earns a different conversation.

### The three segments — name them correctly

**RILEY:** And the company structure today.

**KEVEN:** Three segments — reorganised in 2023. *Entertainment. Sports. Experiences.* Anyone on the Account Team should be able to name them in their sleep.

*Entertainment* contains — Walt Disney Studios, Pixar, Marvel, Lucasfilm, 20th Century, Searchlight. The networks — ABC, FX, Nat Geo. The direct-to-consumer streaming — Disney+ and Hulu. *That's roughly half the company by revenue.*

*Sports* contains — ESPN linear, ESPN+, and the forthcoming ESPN-direct-to-consumer launch. Smaller revenue but disproportionately attention-grabbing and culturally important.

*Experiences* contains — the parks (Disneyland, Walt Disney World, Disneyland Paris, Tokyo, Hong Kong, Shanghai), the Cruise Line, Consumer Products, Disney Vacation Club. *Experiences is the largest and most profitable segment.*

**RILEY:** And the implication for the seller —

**KEVEN:** The implication — *every Disney conversation is segment-specific.* You don't pitch *"AI for Disney."* You pitch *"AI for Experiences ops"* or *"AI for Disney+ subscriber retention"* or *"AI for ESPN content production."* Each segment has its own P&L owner, its own technology leadership, its own KPI envelope. The four-level ladder we covered in the Sellers Podcast — *that ladder is per-segment, not per-company.* You might be at L1 in Experiences and L3 in Streaming at the same time. They climb independently.

### The strategic pressure today

**RILEY:** Pressure points. Walk me through what the leadership is actually managing in 2026.

**KEVEN:** OK. Six pressure points that matter for the APEX conversation. I want to be specific because the Account Team will encounter each of these in conversations.

*Pressure one — streaming profitability.* Disney+ launched in November 2019. Aggressive subscriber growth followed. Subscriber growth came at the cost of profitability. Bob Iger returned as CEO in late 2022 and made *consolidated DTC profitability* a central commitment. *That commitment hit in late 2024 with the first profitable quarter for the direct-to-consumer business.* The commitment continues — sustained profitability is the goal, not one quarter. *This is the pressure that motivates the streaming-churn and personalisation scenarios.*

*Pressure two — linear TV decline.* ABC, FX, ESPN linear all face cord-cutting. Annual cord-cutting rates in the US have been five to eight percent of households for the past several years. *Linear revenue is structurally declining.* The transition to DTC is mandatory. The pressure for *every* linear network is — extract maximum value from the linear business while it lasts; transition viewers and economics to DTC at the same time. *This is the context behind ESPN-DTC and the ESPN auto-highlight scenario.*

*Pressure three — content cost inflation.* Marvel, Star Wars, and Pixar production costs have escalated through the 2010s and 2020s. *Per-title ROI is under scrutiny.* The era of "every Marvel film is a hit" has given way to *some hits and some misses.* The economics of expensive content are forcing harder decisions on what gets greenlit and what gets shelved. *This is the context behind the cold-start scenarios — making sure the launches you do invest in succeed.*

*Pressure four — Parks operational complexity.* Experiences is the most profitable segment but operationally enormous. Six parks worldwide. Cruise Line. Multi-day, multi-property, multi-park guest journeys. *Operational efficiency at this scale is a continuous optimisation problem.* IoT-instrumented rides, guest-flow optimisation, F&B operations, hotel operations — each one is an operations agent opportunity. *This is the context behind the Parks IoT scenarios.*

*Pressure five — password sharing and paid-sharing.* Netflix demonstrated in 2023-2024 that converting password-sharers into paid accounts is *highly accretive to revenue.* Disney has begun implementing similar measures. *This is the context behind the password-sharing-detection scenario.*

*Pressure six — successor planning.* Bob Iger's eventual successor is the most-watched executive succession in media. The strategic posture for the next decade depends on who that successor is and what they bring. *The Account Team should be attuned to the succession conversation, not because we should comment on it, but because every long-term commitment Disney makes will be evaluated through the lens of "what will the successor inherit."*

### Where APEX fits in this picture

**RILEY:** And the framework's role —

**KEVEN:** APEX is — *the framework that lets Disney execute against these pressures using agentic AI, with governance Disney's compliance organisation will accept, on a Microsoft platform Disney already substantially uses.* That sentence is the pitch. Let me unpack each clause.

*The framework that lets Disney execute* — *not* the framework that promises to transform Disney. Execute. Specific scenarios, specific KPIs, specific outcomes. Disney's leadership doesn't need consultant promises. They need execution.

*Against these pressures* — the six pressures we just walked. APEX scenarios map specifically to them.

*Using agentic AI* — not BI. Not predictive ML. Not Copilot deployment. *Agentic AI* — agents that compose data, reason, recommend, and act under governance.

*With governance Disney's compliance organisation will accept* — Disney's CCO and legal organisations are sophisticated. Purview-based audit, hash-chained lineage, HITL gating — these are the architectural commitments that make agentic AI deployable inside Disney. We covered this in the Services Podcast.

*On a Microsoft platform Disney already substantially uses* — Disney has been on Microsoft for productivity (Microsoft 365) for years, has Azure for portions of its infrastructure, and has been deepening Microsoft engagement. APEX rides that existing investment. We are not asking Disney to *adopt* a new platform. We are asking Disney to *compose* the platform they already have.

### The 16 scenarios — a preview

**RILEY:** Preview the 16 scenarios. We'll walk them in detail in Episodes 2-4, but set them up here.

**KEVEN:** TMT Practice curated 16 Disney-fit scenarios from the 723-scenario APEX library. The curation criteria — *(a) the scenario maps to a known Disney pressure point. (b) The scenario is Wave-1-deliverable in the next 12 months. (c) The scenario has cross-scenario reuse potential with other curated scenarios. (d) The scenario respects Disney's compliance posture from day one.* Sixteen scenarios passed those criteria.

The 16 fall into four groups.

*Customer Experience — 6 scenarios.* Across Disney+, Hulu, ESPN+, and Parks. Streaming-churn prediction. Personalised content recommendations and rails. Cold-start boost for Marvel, Pixar, Lucasfilm launches. Password-sharing detection plus paid-sharing playbook. ESPN auto-highlight detection and clipping. Auto-dub and subtitle for international Disney+ markets. *We'll go deep on these in Episode 2.*

*Network and Infrastructure — 4 scenarios.* Streaming delivery operations. Parks IoT. *We'll go deep on these in Episode 3.*

*Engineering and R&D, Internal IT, Cyber — 4 scenarios.* Internal IT operations augmentation. Cyber operations. *Episode 3 also.*

*Engineering and Build / Deployment — 2 scenarios.* The two meta-scenarios that augment Disney's own engineering organisation's build and deployment workflows. *Episode 4 covers these alongside the ladder discussion.*

**RILEY:** And the strict practice boundary —

**KEVEN:** TMT Practice owns the 16. Where Disney's operations cross into theme-park ops or consumer-products retail — those scenarios pull from the RC and TH libraries, but they're handled through cross-Practice composition with TMT as the lead. The Account Team should understand that *boundary management is part of the engagement.*

### One disagreement

**KEVEN:** Pushback.

**RILEY:** OK. Let me push on the *positioning sequence.* The framework says *segment first.* Start with Entertainment, or start with Experiences, or start with Sports. I want to push on whether — for the *opening conversation* with Disney leadership — we should *deliberately not* pick a segment yet.

**KEVEN:** Talk through it.

**RILEY:** The reason — Disney's *cross-segment* opportunity is enormous and underappreciated. The guest who visits Walt Disney World is *probably* a Disney+ subscriber. The Disney+ subscriber is *probably* in the consumer-products purchase funnel. The ESPN+ subscriber overlaps significantly with the parks guest base. *The cross-segment value of Disney's data estate is what makes Disney unique as an APEX account.* If we pick a segment too early, we cut off the cross-segment narrative.

**KEVEN:** So you'd suggest —

**RILEY:** I'd suggest — *open with the cross-segment view in the first conversation with senior leadership.* Then, in the second meeting with a segment leader, pick the segment that owns the pain. The opening conversation is about *what Deloitte uniquely sees* — the cross-segment value of a composed canonical data layer. The second conversation is about *the specific Wave 1.*

**KEVEN:** I think you're right. And it's specifically counter to the typical engagement pattern, which is — pick the segment first, get to Wave 1 fastest. The Disney-specific play is — *establish cross-segment credibility first.* The lower-velocity opening pays off in longer-term commitment.

**RILEY:** Yes.

### What to carry forward to Episode 2

**KEVEN:** Three things.

One — *Disney has three segments — Entertainment, Sports, Experiences. Every conversation is segment-aware. The four-level ladder climbs per-segment, not per-company.*

Two — *six pressure points — streaming profitability, linear TV decline, content cost inflation, Parks operational complexity, password-sharing, succession.* These motivate every scenario the Account Team will discuss.

Three — *the cross-segment data estate is Disney's structural advantage and our positioning hook.* Open with cross-segment; narrow to segment in the second conversation.

**RILEY:** Next episode — *The Customer Experience Six.* Six scenarios spanning Disney+, Hulu, ESPN+, and Parks. Each one with a specific Disney pressure point, a specific KPI signal, and a specific seller's path.
