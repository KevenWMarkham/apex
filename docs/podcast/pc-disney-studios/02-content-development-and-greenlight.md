## The conversation

### Why content development is where the studios bets get sized

**KEVEN:** Set up the framing.

**EDEN:** The studios business is a portfolio of bets. *Each bet is sized at greenlight.* Once a film is greenlit, the budget, the marketing spend, the talent commitments, and the slate-positioning are largely set. *Errors at greenlight propagate downstream.* A bad greenlight produces a budget overrun, an oversized marketing spend, an under-performing release, and a talent relationship that needs to be managed afterward.

*So the leverage point is greenlight.* Earlier and better signal into the greenlight decision — without displacing the creative judgement of the decision-makers — is the highest-leverage agentic play at the studios.

The four plays this episode all support the greenlight decision and its adjacent decisions (development, casting, audience-science) without crossing into creative-authorship territory.

### Play one — Greenlight Decision Support Agent

**EDEN:** Play one. The flagship.

**KEVEN:** Greenlight Decision Support Agent. The play that composes data across comparable-title performance, genre trends, talent availability, production-cost benchmarks, marketing economics, and awards positioning *for the executive committee that decides greenlight.*

The Studios context. Greenlight at the tentpole level requires alignment across twelve-plus stakeholders. Each stakeholder has their own data view. Some data lives in financial systems, some in distribution analytics, some in marketing-research databases, some in third-party industry-research subscriptions, some in internal historical-performance archives. *No single executive has the full picture.*

The pain point. *The cycle time and the variance in decision quality.* Greenlight cycles run weeks-to-months for tentpole projects. Within that cycle, individual stakeholders form views based on incomplete data. The executive committee then debates those views. The debate is *as much about reconciling differing data interpretations* as it is about creative-and-strategic judgement.

The agent strategy. The greenlight-support agent composes the data into a unified per-project view. Comparable-title performance (theatrical, streaming, international). Talent-availability calendar. Production-cost benchmark. Marketing-spend comparable. Awards-positioning analysis. Genre-trend signal. International market readiness. *All composed continuously for any project currently in development.* The executive committee sees the same composed view — disagreements happen over interpretation and strategic judgement, not over which data exists.

*Critically — the agent does not recommend greenlight or no-greenlight.* The executive committee decides. The agent composes the substrate of the decision.

The architectural shape. Cross-system canonical at Silver — title performance, talent profiles, production cost benchmarks, distribution windowing data, awards calendar. Gold mart shapes per-project composite-decision view. MCP tools span the data sources with read-only access.

The KPI signal. *Greenlight cycle time compression 30-50 percent on tentpole projects.* *Decision-quality variance reduction* — measured as alignment-time across stakeholders. *Post-greenlight surprise reduction* — fewer cases where a stakeholder's late objection delays production start.

**KEVEN:** And the buyer —

**EDEN:** *Studio President plus Chief Creative Officer plus Head of Business Affairs.* This is a senior-executive play. The buyer is multi-person from day one. *The Account Team's relationship work is significant before the SOW conversation.*

### Play two — Audience Science & Sentiment Agent

**EDEN:** Play two. Audience science.

**KEVEN:** Audience science is the rigorous practice of *testing how audiences will respond* before a film locks. Preview screenings (paid audience research). Trailer testing. Online sentiment tracking once campaigns launch. Test-and-learn cycles on creative decisions.

The Studios context. Every major studio invests heavily in audience science. The work is conducted by internal research teams plus external research vendors. *The data is rich.* What's been partial is *agentic-AI augmentation of the data composition.*

The pain point. Audience-science data arrives in batches — *preview screening results in twelve hours, trailer test results in 48 hours, social-sentiment analysis in days.* Decisions on cut and marketing have to happen on the same compressed cadence as the audience-science data. *The composition of multiple data streams* — preview + trailer test + social signal + competitive positioning — is manual today.

The agent strategy. Audience-science composition agent reads the streams continuously, composes per-project sentiment-and-response view, recommends focal areas for creative-team review. *The creative team — director, editor, producer — decides what to change.* The agent surfaces the signal; humans interpret and act.

The architectural shape. Cross-system canonical for audience-research data. Real-Time Intelligence for the social-signal streaming side. Gold per-project composite-sentiment view. MCP tools span research-vendor APIs, social-listening platforms, and internal preview-screening systems.

The KPI signal. *Time-to-composed-audience-insight compression 60-70 percent.* *Decision-velocity on cut-and-marketing adjustments improved.* *Creative-team feedback cycle accelerated.*

### Play three — IP & Franchise Opportunity Scoring Agent

**EDEN:** Play three. IP scoring.

**KEVEN:** Disney's IP portfolio is enormous. Marvel characters and IP, Star Wars IP, Pixar original IP, Walt Disney character library, the 20th Century library acquired through the Fox transaction, Searchlight prestige catalog. *Each IP has potential for franchise extension, sequels, spin-offs, theme-park integration, consumer-products licensing.*

The pain point. *Identifying the highest-opportunity IP for next-cycle development is largely judgement-based today.* Senior creative-and-business-strategy leaders maintain their own lists. The portfolio view across the entire IP estate is *not continuously composed.* Opportunities are surfaced episodically.

The agent strategy. IP-opportunity-scoring agent reads continuously across IP-performance signal (theatrical, streaming, consumer products), audience-affinity data, social-signal, competitive landscape, talent-attachment-availability. Surfaces *emerging opportunity signals* — an underutilised IP gaining audience momentum, a character whose franchise-extension potential is rising, a story-world whose adjacent properties have unexplored synergy.

*Studio development leadership reviews and prioritises.* The agent surfaces; the creative-and-business leaders pick what to develop.

The architectural shape. Cross-segment canonical — IP performance, audience signal, competitive intelligence. Gold per-IP composite-opportunity view. MCP tools span Disney's internal IP systems and external industry-research subscriptions.

The KPI signal. *Underutilised-IP-identification rate.* *Time-to-opportunity-surfaced.* *Development-pipeline coverage of high-opportunity IP.* Long-term — *hit rate of agent-surfaced opportunities versus baseline pipeline.*

**KEVEN:** And the strategic frame —

**EDEN:** *This play is the strategic-CCO conversation.* The Chief Creative Officer of a studio wants to know — *what should we be developing next?* The agent's contribution is *systematic surfacing* of opportunities that the human judgement might not notice across the breadth of the IP estate. *Augmentation of strategic creative judgement, not replacement.*

### Play four — Talent & Casting Intelligence Agent

**EDEN:** Play four. Talent intelligence.

**KEVEN:** Casting decisions affect *every dimension of a production* — budget, schedule, marketing positioning, awards potential, audience appeal. Studio leadership casting decisions are made in coordination with the director, producer, casting director, and the talent's representation. *Each casting choice composes implications across many dimensions.*

The pain point. *Composing the implications of a casting choice is manual today.* Comparable-performance data for candidate talent. Recent box-office and streaming-performance signal. Audience-affinity scores. Awards-track-record. Compensation comparables. Calendar availability. Existing talent commitments. *These exist; they're composed by the head of casting plus the studio executive in conversation, not by an agent.*

The agent strategy. Casting-intelligence agent composes the dimensions for any candidate talent or candidate ensemble. Surfaces composite-fit view *for the role and the production economics.* The casting director, director, and studio executive review and decide. Agent does not recommend specific casting choices — agent composes the data.

*Critically — and the Account Team must be explicit about this with studio leadership* — the agent does not generate or simulate performances, does not use likenesses for casting visualization without explicit talent consent, and does not displace casting directors. The play is *operational composition* of casting-decision data. The creative casting judgement stays with the casting director and studio executive.

The architectural shape. Cross-system canonical for talent performance, availability, compensation, awards-track-record. Gold per-role composite-fit view. MCP tools span talent-database integrations and audience-research data.

The KPI signal. *Casting decision cycle compression.* *Coverage of full candidate-set across roles* (the agent surfaces candidates the casting director might not have surfaced manually). *Casting-decision-stakeholder alignment time reduction.*

### Cross-cutting observation

**KEVEN:** Step back. What does the Account Team take away across the four plays?

**EDEN:** Three things.

One — *all four plays support the same underlying decision rhythm* — development through greenlight. The same Silver canonical foundation supports all four. *Wave 1 in one play substantially pays for Wave 2 in adjacent plays.* Compounding asset thesis at work.

Two — *every play is explicit about not displacing creative authorship.* The agent composes the data; the human creative-and-strategic leader decides. The Account Team's framing must be unambiguous on this. *Studios leadership is sensitive to the post-strike AI-policy landscape* — the Account Team's posture must respect it.

Three — *the buyer for these plays is the executive committee of a specific studio, not the corporate Disney level.* Marvel's greenlight committee. Pixar's development leadership. Lucasfilm's IP-strategy team. *The Account Team picks the studio with the strongest current relationship and lands Wave 1 there.* Other studios follow once the first studio has reference value.

### One disagreement

**KEVEN:** Pushback.

**EDEN:** Let me push on the *casting-intelligence play.* Talent and casting is the most politically sensitive area in studios. I think the Account Team should *not lead with casting intelligence* in initial conversations. Lead with greenlight decision support and audience science — the operational plays where the AI-talent boundary is clearer. *Earn the relationship before opening the casting-intelligence conversation.*
