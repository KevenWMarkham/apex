## The conversation

### Why we needed a curated list beyond the 16

**KEVEN:** Set this up. The 16 TMT-MED scenarios are real. Why do we need a different list?

**RILEY:** Three reasons.

One — *Disney's leadership is structurally cross-functional.* Even a senior content executive at Disney also operates a back office. Their P&L includes finance, HR, vendor, technology costs. When the Account Team has a meeting with that executive, *they're not just a content buyer.* They're an enterprise-account buyer. *Back-office plays land in that conversation too.* The 16 TMT-MED scenarios are silent on it.

Two — *streaming-experience-centric plays deserve their own focused list.* The 16 TMT-MED scenarios include six CX scenarios. *Within streaming specifically*, there are *additional* plays that round out the experience surface — quality-of-experience triage, ad-targeting for AVOD, subscriber lifecycle agents — that didn't make the original 16 because the original 16 had broader operational and infrastructure cuts to make. *The Account Team needs the experience-centric streaming subset called out clearly.*

Three — *Wave 1 conversations at Disney often start with a back-office leader.* The CFO. The CIO. The Chief Audit Executive. These leaders have *clear, measurable, public commitments* — close cycle, audit posture, IT productivity. Back-office plays are *easy starting points* for the Account Team's first meeting at a senior leadership level, *especially* when the meeting is with a CIO or CFO rather than a content leader.

The twenty plays — ten back-office, ten streaming — fill those gaps.

*Sheet three — How To Use.* Account-team-specific guidance. How to use the list in pre-meeting prep, quarterly account planning, BVA construction, and Microsoft-attach modelling.

**KEVEN:** And how the Account Team actually uses it —

**RILEY:** Three primary workflows.

### The ten back-office plays

**KEVEN:** Walk the ten back-office plays. Take time on each.

**RILEY:** OK. I'll be brief per play but specific.

*Play one — Continuous-Close Agent.* CFO conversation. The classic close-cycle compression play. Disney's close cycle today is in the high single digits of days. The agent compresses to three to five days by drafting journal entries, reconciling inter-company, generating consolidation packages. *Controller approves.* KPI signal — thirty to fifty percent cycle compression. Wave-1 range one point zero to one point eight million dollars. High priority.

*Play two — AP Invoice-Triage Agent.* AP director conversation. Disney processes very large invoice volumes. Agent reads invoices, extracts line items, matches PO and contract, codes GL accounts. *AP clerk approves exceptions.* KPI — AP cycle time down fifty percent, exception rate down thirty percent. Wave-1 range zero point eight to one point four million. High priority.

*Play three — Procurement Intake and Spend-Categorisation Agent.* CPO conversation. Disney's procurement intake is high-volume and manual. Spend categorisation drift creates analytics errors. Agent classifies intake, recommends suppliers, surfaces consolidation. *Procurement approves.* KPI — intake cycle down forty percent, spend data quality plus thirty points. Wave 2. Medium priority.

*Play four — Contract Review Agent.* General Counsel conversation. Disney's legal organisation reviews enormous volumes of contracts — talent, distribution, vendor, partnership. Agent flags non-standard clauses, compares against precedent library, drafts redlines. *Attorney approves.* KPI — review cycle down sixty percent. Wave 2. Medium priority.

*Play five — Audit Evidence Readiness Agent.* Chief Audit Executive conversation. Annual SOX and operational audits require evidence compilation across many systems. Agent continuously gathers control evidence. KPI — evidence preparation down seventy percent. Wave 1. *High priority — this play is one of the best opening plays for the Disney audit and compliance conversation.*

*Play six — IT Service-Desk Agent-Assist.* CIO conversation. Two hundred thousand-plus Disney employees globally. Agent assists human IT support tech with cross-system context. *Human approves.* KPI — average handle time down thirty percent, first-call resolution up fifteen points. Wave 2. High priority. *Note the Disney specific framing — IT service-desk is outsourced, so the value is internal NPS and contract leverage, not headcount reduction.*

*Play seven — HR Onboarding and Lifecycle Agent.* CHRO conversation. New-hire onboarding across 200,000 employees is workflow-heavy. Agent orchestrates onboarding across HR systems, IT provisioning, training, and policy acknowledgement. KPI — time-to-productivity down forty percent. Wave 2. Medium priority.

*Play eight — T&E Compliance and Audit Agent.* Controller / T&E head conversation. Travel and expense compliance audits are sample-based. Agent reviews claims continuously against policy. KPI — 100% claim coverage, violation detection up forty percent. Wave 2. Medium priority.

*Play nine — Continuous Controls and Risk Monitoring Agent.* Chief Risk Officer and CCO conversation. Disney's risk register requires continuous monitoring. Agent monitors control evidence streams continuously, escalates emerging risk. KPI — mean-time-to-detect emerging risk down sixty percent. Wave 2. High priority.

*Play ten — Third-Party Risk and Supply-Chain Security Monitoring Agent.* CISO and head of vendor risk. Disney's third-party ecosystem creates indirect exposure. Agent monitors third-party security posture from public signals continuously. KPI — third-party incident rate down thirty percent. Wave 3. Medium priority.

*Play eleven — CTO Portfolio and Decision Intelligence Agent.* *This is the strategic-CTO play, and I want to slow down on it.*

**KEVEN:** Take time here.

**RILEY:** OK. The Disney CTO oversees hundreds of technology investments across the three segments. Platform modernisation initiatives. AI portfolio. Engineering productivity programs. Build-versus-buy decisions across the studios and the streaming and the parks. Cyber posture coordination with the CISO. Talent strategy. *Manual portfolio reviews happen quarterly. The portfolio moves faster than that.* Stranded pilots accumulate between reviews. Investment ROI is opaque. The CTO is making weighty allocation decisions on quarterly-stale data.

The agent reads the CTO's technology investment portfolio *continuously.* It pulls from the CapEx tracking, the project status systems, the performance telemetry on what's been deployed, the cost actuals, the alignment to business KPIs. It produces a *continuous portfolio-health view.* It identifies *stranded pilots* — projects that have been running for months without crossing milestones — and at-risk investments. It drafts *portfolio recommendations* — sustain, accelerate, sunset, consolidate.

The CTO and the technology steering committee approve the recommendations. *The agent doesn't decide. The agent surfaces the work the CTO already needs to do, with the data composed.*

**KEVEN:** And the KPI signal —

**RILEY:** *Stranded-pilot rate down forty percent.* That's the visible win. The deeper win — *investment-cycle compression by fifty percent.* The portfolio-review cadence goes from *quarterly to continuous.* The CTO is making allocation calls on current data, not stale data.

The buyer — *the CTO, with the CIO as joint owner depending on how Disney's tech-org is structured this year.* The CFO is a critical adjacent buyer because the play touches CapEx and OpEx investment discipline.

Microsoft attach — *Fabric for the portfolio data foundation, Foundry for the agent, Power BI for the CTO's continuous portfolio dashboard, Purview for the audit trail of every allocation recommendation, Power Platform for the operator workflows around approval and tracking.*

Wave 2 placement. Wave-1 range — *one point four to two million dollars* in services revenue. High priority.

**KEVEN:** And why this is the *strategic* play for the CTO conversation —

**RILEY:** Three reasons.

One — *it positions APEX as the framework that governs Disney's AI portfolio.* Not just one Service among many — *the framework that holds the portfolio together.* The CTO's strategic question is — *how do I rationalise this AI investment.* APEX's answer through this play is — *we govern your portfolio with the same framework we use to deliver individual Services.*

Two — *it makes APEX defensible at the executive-committee level.* When the Disney executive committee asks the CTO — *how is the AI portfolio performing* — the CTO has an agent-driven answer with continuous visibility. *That's a credibility moment for both the CTO and for APEX inside Disney.*

Three — *it surfaces CFO-CTO alignment opportunities.* The portfolio agent shows *which investments are returning and which aren't.* That's exactly the conversation the CFO wants to have with the CTO. *APEX becomes the joint substrate for that conversation.*

**KEVEN:** And the seller's hook for this play —

**RILEY:** *"You have hundreds of technology investments across Disney. Quarterly portfolio reviews can't keep up. We can give you a continuous portfolio agent that surfaces stranded pilots, drafts your sustain-accelerate-sunset recommendations, and gives you and the CFO a shared view of where investment is paying off. Wave 2 from us — after your Wave 1 lands the canonical foundation."*

That's the strategic-CTO conversation.

*Play twelve — Engineering Headcount Optimisation and Productivity Agent.* *This is the joint CTO-CFO play, and it's the play that the Account Team needs to handle with deliberate framing.*

**KEVEN:** Go slowly on this one because the framing matters.

**RILEY:** Yes. Let me set up what this play actually is, and then how the Account Team should position it.

What it is. Disney's engineering organisation is *large.* Studios technology. DTC engineering for Disney+ and Hulu. Parks technology — MagicBand+, MyDisneyExperience, ride systems. ESPN production technology. Internal IT engineering. Corporate engineering. *Thousands of engineers in aggregate across the segments.* AI-augmented engineering produces measurable productivity lift in this kind of organisation. The platform is *a multi-agent engineering productivity capability* — code-generation agents for routine implementations, agentic code-review, design-doc and architecture-record drafting, automated test generation, technical-documentation maintenance, deployment-pipeline agent assist, internal developer Q&A agents.

The capability lifts productivity twenty to thirty-five percent on covered task types. Code-review cycle compression of forty to sixty percent. Time-to-PR-merge compression. Onboarding-time reduction for new engineers.

**KEVEN:** And the framing —

**RILEY:** Here's the framing. *The productivity lift creates an option set for management.* Four real options.

Option one — *ship more output with the same team.* Engineering capacity is reused; the velocity of new features and platforms increases.

Option two — *reduce engineering headcount for cost optimisation.* The CFO frames the play as a cost-savings opportunity. Productivity lift translated into reduced staffing.

Option three — *shift the hiring profile.* Same headcount, different mix — fewer junior implementers, more senior architects and product owners. The skill profile evolves.

Option four — *redirect capacity to higher-leverage work.* Senior engineers spend less time on routine implementations and more time on architectural and strategic work the engineering organisation hasn't had capacity for.

*Disney's leadership decides which option to take.* The platform enables the option. The CFO and CTO will have different preferences. The CHRO will be involved on the workforce-composition dimension. *The Account Team's job is to deliver the platform; the management decision is Disney's.*

**KEVEN:** And the buyer set —

**RILEY:** Joint. *CTO and CFO together.* Plus heads of engineering per business unit. Plus CHRO as the workforce-composition partner. *This is one of the few plays where the buyer is explicitly multi-executive from the outset* — because the option set crosses CFO, CTO, and CHRO authority.

The Microsoft attach. GitHub Copilot is central to the implementation. Plus Fabric for the engineering-productivity data foundation, Foundry for the multi-agent orchestration, M365 Copilot for the documentation and communication agents, Power Platform for the developer-experience workflows.

Wave 2-3 placement. Wave-1 range *one point five to two point five million dollars* in services revenue. High priority.

**KEVEN:** And why this is the strategic conversation —

**RILEY:** Three reasons.

One — *industry context.* The AI-augmented-engineering pattern is now industry-standard. Major technology companies have publicly committed to AI-driven engineering productivity. The question for Disney leadership isn't *whether* to adopt the pattern — it's *how to deploy it well and what to do with the productivity gain.* APEX is the framework for both.

Two — *honest CFO conversation.* The CFO wants the cost-savings option understood and quantified. *The play makes that quantification possible.* The CFO sees the headcount option even if the engineering organisation prefers a different option.

Three — *honest CHRO conversation.* The CHRO is concerned with workforce transition, retention of high-performing engineers, and brand-employer reputation. *The play makes that transition manageable.* If the option taken is reduction, it's done with planning. If the option taken is redirection, it's done with capability investment.

**KEVEN:** And the Account Team's seller hook for this play —

**RILEY:** Honest and direct. *"Your engineering organisation is sized for the pre-AI era. AI-augmented engineering will produce measurable productivity lift. You have four real options for what to do with that lift — more output, lower headcount, shifted hiring profile, or redirected capacity. The platform we deliver enables you to pursue any of them. Wave 2 — six months — pilot scope of two hundred engineers. Measure the lift. Decide which option."*

That's the conversation.

**KEVEN:** And one specific Independence note —

**RILEY:** *Deloitte recommends what the client should build. The client decides what to do with the workforce.* The Account Team's posture is unambiguous on this. *Deloitte does not recommend specific headcount actions to clients we audit.* The platform creates the option; the client's leadership decides; the client's leadership is accountable. Be explicit about that in any conversation where the headcount-reduction option is on the table.

*Play twenty — Subscriber Support Agent.* Head of customer service streaming. Disney+/Hulu/ESPN+ CS tickets span billing, playback, content, account. **KEVEN:** And the *priority sequence* the Account Team should use across the union —

**RILEY:** Three principles.
