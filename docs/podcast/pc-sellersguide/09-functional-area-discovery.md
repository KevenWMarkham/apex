## The conversation

### Where this fits in the Sellers Dream

**JORDAN:** Set this up in the Dream from Episode Eight.

**KEVEN:** The Sellers Dream — four rungs — L1 Power BI wedge, L2 Wave 1 agentic services, L3 B2B fleet, L4 B2C transformation. The transition from L1 to L2 — that's the moment Episode Eight named — *"Want to act on the top three?"* — is *what this episode operationalises.*

The practice leader has seen the L1 heatmap. They've said yes — let's talk about the top three. The seller now has to *take the conversation deeper into the client organisation* — typically to a functional leader, sometimes to multiple in parallel — to *find the specific Wave 1 deal.* That's where the 30-Minute Framework lives. *Five questions per leader. One index card per conversation. Three to five conversations across the practice to surface the right Wave 1.*

**JORDAN:** And the framework is the same five questions for every functional area —

**KEVEN:** Same five questions. Different *content* per functional area. Same *shape.* That's what makes it teachable. A seller who learns the framework once can run discovery with a CFO, a COO, a CMO, a CRO, or a CIO using the same scaffold.

### The five questions

**KEVEN:** Let me walk the five questions slowly. Because the *order* matters and the *posture* matters more than the words.

Question one — *open with the metric.* *"What's the top number you're held to this year — and how far off baseline are you tracking?"*

You're asking — *what's the thing your boss measures you on. What's the gap.* You're letting them name the pain. You're not naming it for them. The CFO answers in EBITDA terms. The COO answers in throughput or OEE. The CMO answers in CAC or LTV. The CRO answers in pipeline coverage or win rate. The CIO answers in modernisation milestones or security posture. *Each one self-identifies their own number.*

**JORDAN:** And the posture —

**KEVEN:** *You are a student, not a vendor.* That phrase is in the Handbook and it's worth repeating. You're learning their job in five minutes. You're not pitching.

Question two — *reverse the timeline.* *"What would have to be true in ninety days for you to feel ahead?"*

The reversal is the magic. Instead of asking *"what do you want long-term"* — which gets aspirational answers — you ask *"what's the win that puts you ahead by Q-end."* That's a *concrete* answer. They have a board meeting. They have an investor day. They have a budget review. The ninety-day mark is real to them.

Question three — *surface the constraint.* *"What's blocking you from getting there today — capacity, data, decision speed, or risk posture?"*

The four options aren't arbitrary. Those are the four constraint classes APEX addresses. *Capacity* — they need more throughput. *Data* — they can't see what they need. *Decision speed* — the cycle takes too long. *Risk posture* — auditors or regulators are constraining what they can do.

**JORDAN:** And typically they pick one.

**KEVEN:** Typically they pick one as dominant. Sometimes two. Almost never four.

Question four — *test the scenario.* *"If an agent could handle [name a concrete decision] overnight, would that move your number?"*

This is the highest-skill question. You have to name a *specific* decision they care about — not a concept. *"If an agent could compress your monthly close from eight days to three"* — that's a concrete decision. *"If we could leverage AI for finance"* — that's a vendor pitch. You score their reaction. Enthusiastic, curious, skeptical. If skeptical, swap to a different scenario before they cool to the whole conversation.

Question five — *confirm the bundle shape.* *"We have a Wave 1 pattern that runs in ninety days for roughly [a range]. Should I schedule the kickoff?"*

Specific number. Specific timeline. Specific ask. You're not asking them to *think about it.* You're asking them to *do the next thing.*

### The index card — what you walk out with

**JORDAN:** And the output of the conversation —

**KEVEN:** An index card. *Five slots.* Filled during the meeting.

*Slot one — Priority.* The number they're measured on. *"Forecast variance under five percent by Q4."*

*Slot two — Pain Signal.* The gap they admitted. *"We're at twelve percent today."*

*Slot three — Scenario.* The APEX agent that closes the gap. *"Forecast-Accuracy Agent."*

*Slot four — Bundle.* The Practice Service Bundle. *"Wave 1 — Finance Agentic Pilot, ninety days."*

*Slot five — Microsoft Attach.* The SKUs the client will license. *"Fabric F64, Foundry, Power BI."*

Five slots. One index card per leader. Three to five cards across the practice. *That's your raw material for the Wave 1 SOW.*

### Five functional areas — each with its own shape

**JORDAN:** OK. Five functional areas. Each one has its own characteristic priorities, pains, and APEX scenarios. Walk me through.

**KEVEN:** Take each one in turn. I won't pretend to be exhaustive — that's the Handbook's job — but I'll land the shape of each conversation.

**Finance and Risk — CFO, Controller, FP&A leader, CRO, Chief Compliance Officer, General Counsel.**

Measured on — EBITDA, cash, forecast accuracy, audit posture, risk register, attestations. Cycle — quarterly for the CFO, continuous for risk and compliance. Sells when — close is too long, forecast misses are public, auditors are pressing.

Top priorities — close compression, forecast accuracy, working capital, audit readiness, cost-to-serve.

Pain signals to listen for — *"Our close takes eight days, we want three." "Forecast variance is over ten percent." "Auditors are asking for evidence we don't have."*

Mapped APEX scenarios — Continuous-Close Agent (cross-edition, 30-50 percent close compression), Forecast-Accuracy Agent (RC, TH, ER), Working-Capital Agent (RC, ICE), Audit-Trail Agent (all editions), Cost-to-Serve Agent (RC, TH).

Microsoft attach — Fabric for data, Foundry for agents, Purview for audit labels, Power BI for the CFO surface, Entra for segregation of duties.

Practice Service Bundle — *Wave 1, Finance Agentic Pilot, ninety days, one to one point eight million dollars.*

**JORDAN:** And the discovery questions specific to the CFO —

**KEVEN:** Three. *"What's your forecast variance this quarter versus plan?" "Where do your auditors push back hardest right now?" "If close took three days instead of eight, what would you do with the time?"*

That last one is the conversion question. The CFO has *a real answer* to that. The answer reveals what they'd do with the recovered capacity — and that's the value you can quantify in the BVA.

**JORDAN:** OK. Operations next.

**KEVEN:** **Operations and Supply Chain — COO, CSCO, plant managers, logistics leaders.**

Measured on — OEE, fill rate, throughput, safety, on-time delivery, inventory turns. Cycle — continuous, daily standup-driven. Sells when — operations are throughput-constrained, supply-chain disruption is recent, safety incidents are visible.

Top priorities — throughput, equipment reliability, supply visibility, safety, sustainability.

Pain signals — *"We can't see our supply chain in real time." "OEE has been stuck at sixty percent for two years." "Demand forecasts miss by twenty percent on long-lead items."*

APEX scenarios — Predictive Maintenance Agent (AXLE, ICE), Cold-Chain Excursion Agent (RC), Refinery Yield Optimisation Agent (ER), Distribution Outage Triage Agent (ER), Field Service Dispatch Agent (ICE).

Microsoft attach — Fabric with Real-Time Intelligence, Foundry, Activator for triggers, Entra for OT identity.

Bundle — *Wave 1 Operations Agentic Pilot, ninety days, eight hundred K to one point five million dollars.*

CFO-like discovery questions for the COO — *"Where does your team spend the most time on coordination?" "What's the cost of your last unplanned downtime?" "If you could see the full supply chain in real time, what decision would change?"*

**JORDAN:** Marketing.

**KEVEN:** **Marketing and Customer Experience — CMO, CXO, Chief Loyalty Officer, brand leadership.**

Measured on — CAC, LTV, NPS/CSAT, retention, share of wallet, campaign attribution. Cycle — monthly with quarterly campaign reviews. Sells when — customer acquisition cost has spiraled, retention is leaking, personalisation is generic.

Top priorities — retention, personalisation, channel efficiency, brand consistency, customer-effort reduction.

Pain signals — *"Our CAC is up forty percent year over year." "Loyalty churn is killing margin." "We have data on every customer and use almost none of it."*

APEX scenarios — Loyalty Churn Prediction and Winback Agent (RC), Personalised Stay Experience Agent (TH), Customer Effort Score Acceleration Agent (TMT cross-segment), Returns Fraud Detection Agent (RC).

Microsoft attach — Fabric, Foundry, M365 Copilot for marketers, Dynamics 365 Customer Insights for the customer-data platform.

Bundle — *Wave 1 Marketing Agentic Pilot, ninety days, seven hundred K to one point three million dollars.*

Discovery — *"Which customer segment costs you most to acquire? "Where are you spending real money on retention that's not working?" "If you had real-time signal on churn risk, what would you do that you can't do today?"*

**JORDAN:** Sales / Revenue / Merchandising.

**KEVEN:** **Sales, Revenue and Merchandising — CRO, Chief Commercial Officer, Chief Merchandising Officer, buyers, category managers.**

Measured on — pipeline coverage, win rate, deal velocity, margin per SKU, sell-through, markdown depth. Cycle — weekly cadence for sales, monthly for merch. Sells when — revenue is missing plan, deals are slipping, margin is compressing.

Top priorities — pipeline acceleration, deal-cycle compression, margin optimisation, assortment intelligence, dynamic pricing.

Pain signals — *"Reps spend more time on CRM than selling." "We mark down too aggressively because we can't predict sell-through." "Our forecast and our pipeline don't agree."*

APEX scenarios — Markdown Optimisation Agent (RC), Demand-Response Orchestration Agent (ER customer-side), Dynamic Rebooking Agent (TH), Connected Vehicle Diagnostic Acceleration (AXLE).

Microsoft attach — Fabric, Foundry, Dynamics 365 Sales Copilot, Power BI for sales dashboards.

Bundle — *Wave 1 Commercial Agentic Pilot, ninety days, eight hundred K to one point six million dollars.*

Discovery — *"What's your win rate trending and what's driving it?" "Where are your reps losing the most cycle time?" "If pricing decisions could happen in seconds instead of days, what would change?"*

**JORDAN:** Last one.

**KEVEN:** **Technology, Data and Talent — CIO, CTO, CDO, CISO, CHRO.**

Measured on — modernisation milestones, platform consolidation, security posture, talent retention, AI-investment ROI. Cycle — quarterly for the CIO, continuous for the CISO. Sells when — the AI portfolio has stranded pilots, the data foundation is fragmented, the team is burning out.

Top priorities — AI portfolio rationalisation, platform consolidation, security posture, talent productivity, governance.

Pain signals — *"We have eleven AI pilots and zero in production." "Our auditors keep asking about AI governance and we don't have an answer." "We can't hire fast enough."*

APEX scenarios — every APEX Service is a candidate. The CIO conversation is *less about which Service* and more about *which framework rationalises our portfolio.*

Microsoft attach — all six platforms.

Bundle — *Wave 1 Platform Agentic Pilot, ninety days, one point two to two million dollars. Plus the bigger frame — APEX as the rationalisation framework for the next three years of AI investment.*

Discovery — *"How many AI pilots are running and how many are in production?" "Where is your auditor most concerned about AI today?" "If your team had a framework that turned pilots into deployable Services, what would change in your hiring plan?"*

### The Scenario → Service Mapping Catalog

**JORDAN:** And the Scenario → Service Mapping Catalog?

**KEVEN:** This is the *closing artefact* of the framework. The Handbook ships a catalog that maps each common functional-area scenario — *forecast accuracy, close compression, predictive maintenance, churn prediction, contact-center agent assist, prior auth, outage triage, et cetera* — to the specific APEX Service that delivers it. The seller's job after the discovery conversation is — *check the index card scenario against the catalog. Confirm the mapped Service. Confirm the Practice Service Bundle. Send the kickoff invite.*

The mapping catalog turns the index card into a *deployable proposal.* Without it, the seller has a wishlist. With it, the seller has a scope.

### A reading I want to do

**KEVEN:** I want to read a short passage from the Handbook on the 30-Minute Framework.

**JORDAN:** Read it.

**KEVEN:** [reading]

*"Sellers who run discovery before opening the Scenario Matcher get sharper matches. The Matcher is a tool, not a shortcut. Five questions in, you can already predict the top scenario it will return. The Matcher's job is to confirm — and to surface the bundle, the attach, and the BVA shape. The seller's job is to listen well enough that the Matcher can do its job."*

[pause]

**JORDAN:** *Listen well enough that the Matcher can do its job.* That's a great frame for the seller's posture.

**KEVEN:** Great frame. *The seller's value-add is the listening, not the tool.* The tools amplify what the seller already heard. They don't substitute for it.

### One disagreement

**JORDAN:** OK. Pushback.

**KEVEN:** Go.

**JORDAN:** The 30-Minute Framework is *beautifully clean* — five questions, one index card, repeatable. *In practice — most functional leaders won't give you thirty uninterrupted minutes.* You'll get fifteen if you're lucky. You'll get a phone call. You'll get a hallway conversation. The framework has to flex to *shorter, more fragmented* interactions.

**KEVEN:** Agree. And the way to flex is — *the five questions don't all have to fit one conversation.* You can do question one on a five-minute hallway. Question two on a follow-up call. Questions three and four in the actual thirty-minute meeting once they've conceded one is needed. Question five at the end of the meeting OR in a follow-up email. *The questions don't have to fit a single block of time. They have to all get asked.*

**JORDAN:** Right. *The framework is the question set, not the time slot.*

**KEVEN:** Yes.

### What to carry forward

**KEVEN:** Two things.

One — *the 30-Minute Framework is a five-question discovery scaffold that works across all five functional areas.* Open with the metric. Reverse the timeline. Surface the constraint. Test the scenario. Confirm the bundle. *One index card per leader.*

Two — *the five functional areas each have their characteristic priorities and APEX scenario matches.* Finance and Risk. Operations and Supply Chain. Marketing and CX. Sales/Revenue/Merchandising. Technology/Data/Talent. *Memorise the pain signals for each. They're how you know you've found the right Wave 1 deal.*
