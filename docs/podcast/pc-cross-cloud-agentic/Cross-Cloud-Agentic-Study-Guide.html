How to use this guide

The Cross-Cloud Agentic Podcast teaches a vendor-neutral architecture for agentic AI — the Acceleration Framework — and how a Microsoft Target Platform Seller positions it honestly across Microsoft, AWS, and Google Cloud. This guide turns the eight episodes into a study and reference companion.

What the series is

Eight episodes, roughly five hours, for Microsoft Target Platform Sellers — people who sell the Microsoft platform but meet clients running any of the three major clouds, often more than one. The series is generic (no specific client named) and Independence-minded: recommendations are made on technical and economic merits, never on partner-channel motion. It is honest about where AWS and GCP lead. Two hosts carry it: Keven, the Microsoft platform practitioner, and Reid, the cross-cloud principal architect who pushes back whenever a claim cannot be defended.

The Five Architectural Principles

The Acceleration Framework rests on five principles. They are vendor-neutral — they work on Microsoft, AWS, or GCP. The clouds differ only in how much of each principle is already productized versus assembled. Episode 1 names all five; every later episode goes deep on one or two.

1

Gold-Tier-First

Agents talk to a purpose-built Gold Tier shaped for reasoning — never directly to systems of record or the data warehouse.

2

Governance + Audit + Ledger as Trust Substrate

DSPM for AI policy, a hash-chained audit row per step, and replay-token validation — the substrate that lets agentic AI enter a regulated enterprise.

3

Identity Continuity

Agent, operator, source, and auditor identities stay distinct yet interlinked — propagated through every tool call with no translation gaps.

4

No Replication — Sources Stay Untouched

Compose via virtualization, mirroring, shortcuts, and federation. Systems of record keep serving OLTP; warehouses keep serving BI; streams stay live.

5

Model Portability

Agent design survives model-generation refreshes and provider swaps. Models are deployable artifacts; agents are durable assets.

Episode 01

The Agentic Stack and The Five Principles

⏱ 34:08 Introduces all Five Principles Foundation episode

Agentic AI is an architectural discontinuity, not a continuation of generative AI. The seller who masters a vendor-neutral framework — the four-layer stack and the Five Principles — turns a procurement conversation into a strategic relationship, while the productization gap window is still open.

1

Content Breakdown

section by section
CO

Cold Open

Keven fields a real seller's-inbox question — "Can you build an agent on top of our Snowflake data warehouse?" — and answers "I can. But you'll wish I hadn't." The exchange sets the series' move: refuse the easy face-value answer and unpack why one question is really three. The hosts introduce themselves — Keven (22 years on Microsoft) and Reid (cross-cloud principal architect, the honesty enforcer).

§1

Why "agentic" needs a definition before we start

The word "agent" is overloaded — stored procedures and ETL pipelines get relabelled as agents. The episode insists on a four-criterion test: reasoning, tool use, state, and an audit substrate — all four required. The audit substrate is the criterion most often skipped because it is hardest to build.

§2

The four-layer agentic stack — plus identity

Four layers: data foundation (where the Gold Tier lives), agent runtime, control plane, and model serving. Identity is the fifth, cross-cutting concern — it threads through all four. Reid notes the most painful production failures happen at identity "seams" between clouds.

§3

The Five Architectural Principles — named explicitly

All five principles of the Acceleration Framework are named: Gold-Tier-First; Governance + Audit + Ledger; Identity Continuity; No Replication; Model Portability. They are vendor-neutral — clouds differ only in productized-capability density.

§4

Why now — the 2024-2026 inflection

All three cloud runtimes (Foundry, Bedrock Agents, Vertex AI Agent Builder) crossed GA inside an 18-month window; governance substrates moved too (Purview DSPM for AI GA 2025); EU AI Act and NIST AI RMF are in force. Reid's tension: Microsoft's productization lead is real but finite — it narrows in 12-18 months.

§5

The Microsoft seller's lens — Independence-minded

The commercial posture: recommend on technical and economic merits; the Five Principles are vendor-neutral so the seller must be able to say "lead with AWS" when honest; Microsoft earns the recommendation on productized-capability density; two contracts (client–Microsoft, client–Deloitte), three when NVIDIA is in scope.

§6

What we'll cover across eight episodes & the reading

A roadmap of Episodes 2-8. Keven then paraphrases the industry-analyst register on the generative-to-agentic discontinuity — it adds persistent state, autonomous tool invocation, multi-step reasoning, and an audit burden; applying generative-era discipline accumulates technical debt at industrial scale.

⚔

The disagreement

Reid argues the strict four-criterion test will make sellers tell clients "that's not actually an agent," alienating them. Keven counters that going along with overloaded vocabulary leaves nothing to defend when the governance bar lands. They converge: keep the precision, deliver it as an upgrade frame ("let me show you what an agent adds"), never a correction.

★

What to carry forward

(1) The four-layer stack plus identity as the fifth concern. (2) The Five Principles as the Acceleration Framework's vendor-neutral commitments — principles first, cloud after. (3) Microsoft earns the recommendation on productized-capability density, not partner-channel motion.

Episode 02

Data Foundation and The No-Replication Principle

⏱ 39:14 Principle 4 · No Replication Principle 1 · Gold-Tier-First

Under agentic workloads, the data foundation should compose an agent-shaped Gold Tier from canonical Silver via federation, mirroring, and shortcuts — never bulk replication — so sources stay authoritative and the agent's audit trail points one hop back.

1

Content Breakdown

section by section
CO

Cold Open

The data architect's recurring nightmare: every project arrives at the same SOW paragraph — bulk-extract source data into a new lake. The pile of copies grows, lineage decays, governance scope explodes. The worst version puts the agent on top of the copies, so an auditor's "where did this come from?" gets a four-hop answer. The antidote is Principle 4 — No Replication.

§1

Why the medallion architecture is the right starting frame

Bronze / Silver / Gold is decades-old practice, not a 2024 invention. What is new under an agentic workload is the shape of Gold: agentic Gold is per-entity composed context, not BI aggregate cubes. The fix is a separate agent-shaped Gold Tier alongside the warehouse, both reading the same Silver.

§2

Silver is canonical, not Gold

The episode's hard rule. Silver is where cross-system reconciliation happens — Customer, VIN, Policyholder, Product, Account each defined once. Gold composes per-scenario from Silver. Getting it backwards breaks lineage and leaves nowhere clean to compose.

§3

Why agentic AI needs Gold-Tier-shaped views

The warehouse optimizes for aggregation — cubes, star schemas, slowly-changing dimensions. Agent reasoning is per-entity and unpredictable ("what does the world look like for this customer now?"). Pre-aggregation strips the granular signal the agent needs. Two consumers, two Gold Tiers, one Silver.

§4

Microsoft Fabric — Mirroring, Shortcuts, Eventstreams

Three Fabric capabilities express no-replication across batch, lakehouse, and streaming. Mirroring reflects external sources (Snowflake, Databricks, Cosmos DB, Azure SQL/Postgres) into OneLake without bulk copy. Shortcuts point at S3/ADLS/Dataverse. Eventstreams pass streaming through. Reid validates from the field: Mirroring on Snowflake shipped, single-digit-minute lag, Purview lineage threading back.

§5

GCP BigQuery Omni + BigLake

Reid leads: BigQuery Omni queries S3 and Azure Blob from one BigQuery surface, with compute running in the source cloud. The query travels, the data does not; egress is only the result. BigLake is the access-control overlay. Keven concedes: when the client's constraint is "data in three clouds, copy none," the right answer can be BigQuery Omni.

§6

AWS Athena Federated Query + Lake Formation

AWS can build the same Gold Tier with more assembly. Athena Federated Query uses per-source Lambda-backed connectors, each with its own version and security config — an operational tax. Lake Formation provides arguably the most granular productized access control. Seller posture: if the client is AWS-primary with depth here, compose with it.

§7

Per-entity joinability at Gold & the streaming + vector layers

The cloud-independent unlock: the agent asks "what does the world look like for this entity?" and Gold returns one composed view joined across every Silver domain — one read, one audit trail. Vector store choice (Azure AI Search / OpenSearch / Vertex Vector Search) is the cheapest decision to migrate later. Streaming sources stay live; persist to Bronze only when regulation requires replay.

⚔

The disagreement

Reid: BigQuery Omni is the strongest cross-cloud federation story — no productized Microsoft or AWS equivalent runs compute in the source cloud at the same maturity. Keven concedes the cross-cloud axis cleanly, then pivots: productization is multi-axis — Fabric Mirroring + Shortcuts + Eventstreams + Purview lineage is the broadest across batch, streaming, and lakehouse. Both true on different axes.

★

What to carry forward

(1) The medallion — Bronze landing, Silver canonical, Gold composed; warehouse and agent Gold are siblings. (2) Principle 4 in action — Fabric broadest productized, BigQuery Omni strongest cross-cloud, AWS most assembly. (3) Per-entity joinability at Gold is the cloud-independent unlock. Seller posture: name the axis the client has, then choose the cloud.

Episode 03

Agent Runtime: Talking to Gold, Not SORs

⏱ 37:20 Principle 1 · Gold-Tier-First in action MCP boundary

Agents must talk to the Gold Tier, never directly to systems of record. The productized agent runtime — Foundry, Bedrock, Vertex — is commoditizing fast, so durable differentiation lives in the architecture, control plane, and data foundation, not the runtime.

1

Content Breakdown

section by section
CO

Cold Open

A real on-call page: an agent is hammering the SAP production REST API at peak hour, breaching the rate limit and backing up three batch jobs. Root cause — the agent's tool definition pointed at SAP directly instead of a Gold view, because composing the Gold view was deferred to a sprint that never arrived. A Principle 1 violation in production; the war story becomes the curriculum.

§1

The MCP boundary discipline — Principle 1 in action

Agent tool calls land on Gold views — never on SORs, the warehouse, or source REST APIs. Four reasons: source systems were not designed for agent traffic; source-direct access explodes governance scope; it breaks audit posture; and a Gold view is a stable contract that absorbs schema drift. The discipline applies to every tool call — auditors grade the one that crossed.

§2

Microsoft Agent Framework + Azure AI Foundry Agent Service

Two pieces that get conflated. The Agent Framework is the open-source, model-agnostic SDK (Agent/Tool/Thread/Run abstractions). Foundry Agent Service is the managed hosting plane — persistent threads, managed state, native Azure AI Search RAG, and native Purview audit echo. GA and solid for single-agent; multi-agent orchestration is still maturing across all three clouds.

§3

AWS Bedrock Agents

Reid leads from production experience. Action Groups give agents tools, defined as an OpenAPI spec or a Lambda function. Bedrock Knowledge Bases is the managed RAG abstraction; Guardrails handles content safety. Claude is native and excellent on Bedrock — latest versions land first. Microsoft posture when Bedrock is the right runtime: compose, do not displace.

§4

GCP Vertex AI Agent Builder + Agent Engine

The youngest of the three runtimes. Agent Builder is the well-executed low-code authoring tool; Agent Engine is the managed hosting plane; Agent Garden offers templates. Tool definitions commonly point at BigQuery views. Workload Identity Federation is GCP's mature cross-cloud identity story; Gemini is native.

§5

Model availability — honest comparison

OpenAI/GPT native on Foundry; Claude native on Bedrock; Gemini native on Vertex; open-model breadth widest on Bedrock. Takeaway: when model selection dominates, pick the cloud where the model is native — but for most enterprise work, governance and data foundation matter more than a two-week recency delta.

§6

Orchestration patterns & HITL design

Three patterns — sequential chains, parallel decomposition, hierarchical sub-agents — all work on all three runtimes; the choice is architectural. HITL gates sit before an irreversible action or after a reasoning-chain composition; wrong placement causes rubber-stamping or lets irreversible actions slip. The operator interface must show the reasoning trace, not just the conclusion.

§7

RAG vs fine-tuning vs distillation

A cost spectrum: prompt engineering (cheapest, brittle), RAG (model unchanged, retrieves at inference — the default start), fine-tuning (weights adjusted, durable, costly), distillation (a frontier model trains a smaller one). Field reality: ~80% of workloads land on RAG plus prompt engineering, ~10% fine-tuning, ~1% distillation.

⚔

The disagreement

Reid: Anthropic Claude is materially better on Bedrock today — latest versions land first by weeks; for a client whose primary requirement is the best Claude experience, Bedrock is the honest answer. Keven concedes the Claude-experience axis, then pivots: for ~80-90% of enterprise work the productized control plane and Gold-tier discipline are foundational, while the model-version delta is "Wave-One negotiable."

Audit, Ledger, and Replay: The Trust Substrate

⏱ 42:17 Principle 2 · Audit + Ledger the trust substrate

In agentic AI the audit row is the product, not the by-product. Because a recommendation is a composite of dozens of intermediate steps, the reasoning chain itself must be captured as a tamper-evident, replayable, identity-stamped substrate — the trust foundation that lets agentic AI cross into the regulated enterprise.

1

Content Breakdown

section by section
CO

Cold Open

An External Audit Reviewer arrives at 7 a.m. with a phone-book-sized printout — not a financial reconciliation, but an agent's overnight reasoning chain. It is hash-chained and replay-token validated, letting her re-run it offline with the same model version, seed, and inputs. The reframe: the audit row is the product, the recommendation is one column.

§1

Why agentic AI requires a different audit pattern

A traditional ML prediction is atomic; an agentic decision is a composite of dozens of intermediate steps. Enterprises audit the model and the data but cannot audit the reasoning chain — that gap is the agentic-AI audit problem. Unfilled, recommendations are "unprovable" in the legal-evidentiary sense.

§2

"Audit row, not log line" — the discipline

A log line is free-form, mutable, debug-oriented. An audit row is a defined schema with validated fields, machine-readable for replay, cryptographically integrity-protected, governance-retained. Emission must be enforced at the architecture layer — the runtime emits rows by construction; developers cannot opt out.

§3

The hash-chained audit row pattern

Every decision lands as a structured row — decision context, agent identity, operator identity, model version, tool calls, data accessed, output, output hash, parent-row hash, replay token. Each row carries the prior row's hash; modifying any earlier row breaks every downstream match. Tamper-evidence is cryptographic, not policy-based. The pattern is the "ledger pattern" — lowercase, descriptive, never a brand.

§4

Replay-token validation — the offline replay

The killer feature: a reviewer can take any decision, take its chain, and replay the reasoning offline to get the same result. Four mechanics enable it — the replay token (seed plus sampling parameters), the model-version pin, the input pin, the state pin. Reid pushes back: deterministic replay is not free — it must be designed from the runtime up.

§5

HITL gates as audit events & identity propagation

Every approve, modify-and-approve, or reject writes its own row — the human is in the chain, not adjacent to it. Human approval cannot be claimed unless it is chained. All four identities (agent, operator, source, auditor) propagate into every row, making "who did what when, with what data, under what authority" answerable at every step.

§6

The lineage thread & the ledger pattern productized vs assembled

The data-accessed field threads audit row → Gold view → Silver canonical → Bronze reference → source. Microsoft: productized reference architecture (Foundry emits, Purview catches). AWS: custom build on DynamoDB or OpenSearch (QLDB flagged in maintenance mode) — six-to-twelve weeks. GCP: BigQuery or Cloud Storage, with Cloud Audit Logs tamper-evident by default.

§7

Why it matters & operational observability

Regulated industries require the chain; EU AI Act high-risk traceability and NIST AI RMF Measure/Manage attach to it; boards want "if something goes wrong, can we trace it?" The same rows serve a second consumer — operational observability: prompt failure rates, tool-call latency, drift detection, cost anomalies. One substrate, two query patterns.

⚔

The disagreement

Reid: the ledger pattern is overengineered for the ~80% of internal-use, non-regulated agents that need no cryptographic chains. Keven concedes partially — the 80% can run with simpler observability, but the 20% touching regulated, customer-facing, or board-visible workloads need the full pattern. Convergence: substrate at the foundation, enforcement intensity per-workload.

★

What to carry forward

(1) The reasoning chain, not the prediction, is the artifact — audit row not log line; hash-chained, replay-validated, identity-propagated. (2) Productized as reference architecture on Microsoft; buildable on AWS/GCP in six-to-twelve weeks. (3) One substrate serves compliance and observability — build at the foundation, tune enforcement per-workload.

Multi-Cloud Reality, Cloud Portability, and Model Portability

1

Content Breakdown

section by section
CO

Cold Open

A new CTO of a multi-national faces an architecture review with one agenda item: agentic AI across three subsidiaries, each acquired over seven years and each on a different primary cloud. "The cloud reality is not a strategy, it is a history." The losing move is "standardize on Microsoft"; the right move recognizes multi-cloud means different things at the enterprise level versus the workload level.

§1

The Acceleration Framework's cloud portability — by design

The framework is architecturally cloud-portable because the Five Principles are vendor-neutral. Keven walks each principle with a portability lens. Headline: Microsoft earns the recommendation on productized-capability density, not architecture-lock.

§2

What "primary cloud" actually means in practice

The 90-percent reality — most agentic workloads land on one cloud per scenario, driven by data gravity. Enterprise-level multi-cloud runs 70-90% at scale, but that does not mean every workload spans clouds. The costly conflation: "we are multi-cloud" usually means "we have workloads on multiple clouds," not "this workload spans clouds."

§3

Cross-cloud egress economics

Egress is an economic constraint, not just a preference. Cross-cloud data egress runs directionally 5-10 cents/GB; intra-cloud movement is often free — an order-of-magnitude asymmetry. Agentic workloads generate many small data movements per decision; cross-cloud, each is a metered event that compounds. Federation reduces egress but does not eliminate it.

§4

Identity federation patterns across clouds

Three primitives: Entra B2B (inbound federation into Entra), AWS IAM federation (SAML, OIDC, cross-account roles), and GCP Workload Identity Federation (the strongest cross-cloud primitive — short-lived, scoped, rotated credentials). Reid's push: design for federation from Wave One, or every cross-cloud scenario becomes an expensive retrofit.

§5

Multi-cloud audit chain patterns

The Episode 5 ledger composes across clouds because the audit-row schema is defined in code, not in any cloud's audit-service config — same row shape written to Fabric, DynamoDB, or BigQuery. Cross-cloud replay is the hard problem — it is a model-portability problem in disguise.

§6

Model Portability — Principle 5 deep dive

Model generations refresh faster every cycle (~9-18 months across major families). An agent built tightly to one generation gets re-architected within 12-18 months. Four disciplines produce portability: versioned prompts, tool-call abstractions, model-agnostic SDKs, evaluation harnesses. "Models are deployable artifacts; agents are durable assets."

§7

When multi-cloud is legitimate vs theatre

Legitimate: regulatory data residency, source-data gravity, model-family preference, M&A inheritance. Theatre: anti-lock-in rhetoric without portability discipline, "cloud-agnostic on principle," procurement leverage masquerading as architecture. The seller must be precise about which the client is doing.

⚔

The disagreement

Keven: multi-cloud at the agentic-stack level is rare and stays exceptional through 2026-2028. Reid: data-residency mandates will make workload-level multi-cloud "increasingly less rare." Convergence: both true at different timescales — resolved as portable-by-default, single-cloud-by-default-execution.

★

What to carry forward

(1) The framework is cloud-portable by design — defend Microsoft on productization density, the framework on architecture. (2) Primary cloud means primary-cloud-per-workload; legitimate multi-cloud vs theatre is a precision test. (3) Model portability is its own four-part discipline — models refresh, agents endure.
