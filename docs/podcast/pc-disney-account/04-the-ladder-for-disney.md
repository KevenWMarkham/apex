## The conversation

### The four-level ladder at Disney specifically

**KEVEN:** Set up where we are.

**RILEY:** Episodes 1-3 covered Disney as an account and the 16 scenarios. Episode 4 takes the Sellers Podcast's *four-level ladder* — Power BI heatmap, then Wave 1 agentic services, then B2B practice-wide, then B2C transformation — and walks it *at Disney specifically.* What each rung looks like. Who the buyer is. What the dollar range is. How the GSSC Lab supports each rung. *And* — the two build-and-deployment meta-scenarios that don't quite fit the four-rung model but matter to the Account Team.

The ladder is climbed *per segment* at Disney. Entertainment ladder. Sports ladder. Experiences ladder. Each ladder climbs independently. The Account Team can be at L1 in one segment and L3 in another *simultaneously.*

### L1 for Disney — the Power BI Opportunity Heatmap

**KEVEN:** L1. The Power BI Opportunity Heatmap.

**RILEY:** What it is at Disney specifically — *a five-day build, delivered to a segment leader (Entertainment, Sports, or Experiences), showing the segment's portfolio mapped against the 16 Disney-fit scenarios.* The .pbix file lives in the leader's workspace. They open it whenever. *They own it.*

What's in it for Disney — the heatmap rows are *the leader's business unit dimensions* (Disney+ regions, Hulu shows, ESPN sports, Parks properties, etc.). The columns are *the relevant scenarios from the 16.* Each cell is scored on three dimensions — opportunity size, Microsoft attach potential, Wave 1 readiness.

The dollar value — *twenty-five to seventy-five thousand dollars* of services revenue. *Not the point.* The point is the conversation it unlocks.

The conversation it unlocks — *"want to act on the top three?"* That's the L1-to-L2 unlock sentence we covered in the Sellers Podcast. At Disney, the top three are typically — two CX scenarios and one operations scenario — varies by segment.

**KEVEN:** Who the L1 buyer is at Disney —

**RILEY:** The buyer is *a senior person within a segment who has discretionary access to a small services budget.* Sometimes a segment president's chief of staff. Sometimes a head of operations within the segment. Sometimes a CTO-level role. *Not the CEO.* Not the segment president. Those buyers come at L2 and L3.

The Account Team's job at L1 is to *identify the right L1 buyer in each segment.* Each segment has one. Sometimes two. Not more.

### L2 for Disney — the first Wave 1 agentic services SOW

**KEVEN:** L2. The first real SOW.

**RILEY:** At Disney specifically — *the first Wave 1 SOW is most likely password-sharing detection from the CX Six*, per the disagreement we had in Episode 2. The Disney-specific reasoning — *highest strategic visibility, highest governance differentiation, net-new revenue rather than retention.*

The dollar value — *five hundred thousand to two million dollars* of services revenue. Plus Disney's Microsoft consumption — Fabric capacity, Foundry consumption, Copilot licenses, Purview, Entra — which Disney procures directly from Microsoft under their separate enterprise agreement.

The bundle shape — *Discover, Design, Build, Deploy.* Ninety days from kickoff to pilot deployment. BVA-backed. KPI envelope defined upfront.

The buyer at L2 — a step up from L1. Typically the *segment-level technology or operations leader.* For password-sharing in streaming — likely the President of Direct-to-Consumer or their senior VP. For ride-system predictive maintenance in Parks — the SVP of Engineering for the parks. *The L2 buyer can authorize a one-to-two-million-dollar pilot, but not a multi-Wave commitment.*

**KEVEN:** And the discipline at L2 —

**RILEY:** Three disciplines.

One — *Wave 1 BVA must be locked before SOW signing.* The KPI envelope is part of the SOW. The discipline of the BVA-in-5-bullets from the Sellers Podbook applies. Disney's commercial team will hold the seller to the bullets.

Two — *the GSSC Lab demo precedes the SOW.* We don't sign Wave 1 without showing the working pattern in the Lab first. That's Disney-specific posture but reflects every sophisticated client expectation today.

Three — *the L2 pilot's success criteria are agreed upfront with Disney's CCO and CIO organisations.* This is where Disney's governance sophistication shines. Don't get to Wave 2 conversation without a *governance hand-off* artifact from Wave 1.

### L3 for Disney — TMT-MED Practice CoE within Disney

**KEVEN:** L3. The B2B Practice-Wide expansion.

**RILEY:** L3 is *the move from one Wave 1 Service to a fleet of Wave 2+ Services in production.* At Disney specifically — this means *establishing a TMT-MED Practice Agentic CoE inside Disney's technology organisation.* Not just delivering more Services. *Establishing the operating model* through which Disney runs the agentic-Service portfolio across segments.

The dollar value — *five to twenty million dollars* of services revenue annually. *Expansion revenue.* Same client, growing footprint, predictable cadence.

What's in the CoE — by L3, Disney is operating typically *six to ten Services* across the segments. Several CX scenarios from the original six. Several Operations scenarios from the eight. Plus the platform and governance services that surround them. *Disney has its own internal team operating these, with Deloitte serving as advisor, deployer, and operate-partner for new Services.*

The buyer at L3 — *the steering committee.* CIO. CTO. CCO. President or COO of one or more segments. Sometimes the General Counsel. *The decision-making body for L3 is multi-person, multi-quarter.*

**KEVEN:** And the multi-Wave compounding at L3 specifically —

**RILEY:** The compounding-asset thesis from the Sellers Podcast — *each new Service costs a fraction of the prior one because the canonical-at-Silver foundation is paid for* — is what makes L3 work commercially. At Disney, by L3, the Account Team has paid the canonical investment in CX. The next Service in CX is dramatically cheaper. The first Operations Service is more expensive because operational data is messier, but the second Operations Service is again cheaper. *The compounding is real and Disney-specific.*

### L4 for Disney — B2C transformation

**KEVEN:** L4. The Dream's apex.

**RILEY:** L4 at Disney is *fully agentic consumer experience across the entire customer journey.* The Disney+ subscriber's home screen is agent-composed. The Parks guest's day is agent-orchestrated through MagicBand+. The ESPN viewer's content stream is agent-curated. The Hulu user's discovery is agent-driven. *All of this composes on the same canonical-at-Silver foundation. All of it under Disney's governance posture. All of it producing audit rows in Purview that Disney's CCO trusts.*

The dollar value — *twenty to fifty million dollars and up* annually. *Strategic / displacement* revenue. The competitor at L4 isn't another consulting firm — *the competitor is doing-it-yourself or doing-it-with-a-platform-vendor.*

The buyer at L4 — the CEO and the board. By L4, the framework has earned its keep through L1-L3, and the CEO-level conversation is about *strategic posture for the next decade of Disney's consumer experience.*

**KEVEN:** And the architectural shape at L4 —

**RILEY:** All six Growth Platforms densely composed. Fabric Real-Time Intelligence at consumer scale. Foundry Agent Service at high availability. M365 Copilot in customer-facing context. Dynamics 365 Customer Insights as the customer-data platform. Purview enforcing governance at every layer. Entra issuing workload identities for hundreds of agent instances. *And — critically — the cross-segment data composition that's Disney's structural advantage.*

The L4 narrative — *"Disney is the only major media company that can deliver agent-orchestrated experiences spanning streaming, parks, sports, and consumer products on a unified canonical foundation. APEX is the framework that builds and operates that foundation."*

### The four unlock sentences — Disney-tailored

**KEVEN:** And the unlock sentences for each transition — let me Disney-tailor them.

**RILEY:** L0 to L1 — *"I'll build your segment a Power BI Opportunity Heatmap mapping the 16 Disney-fit scenarios to your portfolio. Five days. Free. You own the .pbix."*

L1 to L2 — *"You've seen the heatmap. The top three Wave 1 candidates for your segment are X, Y, Z. Want to act on number one?"*

L2 to L3 — *"Wave 1 delivered X against Y of services spend in ninety days, with the BVA envelope landing in the upper band. Want to establish the TMT-MED CoE inside your technology organisation to scale this pattern across the segment?"*

L3 to L4 — *"Your B2B fleet is humming across segments. When do we move to the cross-segment consumer experience — fully agent-orchestrated, fully governed?"*

Four sentences. The Account Team should know all four. *Use them at the right transition. Each unlocks meaningful revenue.*

### The two build-and-deployment meta-scenarios

**KEVEN:** OK. The two scenarios in the image that sit in the "Engineering & Build / Deployment" group. Let me cover those because they sit slightly outside the four-rung ladder.

**RILEY:** Right. These are *meta-scenarios.* They augment *Disney's own engineering build and deployment workflows.* Not consumer-facing. Not operational. *Engineering-facing.* The customers are Disney's own software engineers and platform operators.

**Build scenario — agent-augmented code review and design review.** *The pain* — Disney's engineering organisation produces enormous volumes of code and design reviews. Senior engineers spend significant time on review work. The agent strategy — agentic code review that surfaces likely issues, architectural-design review that compares proposed designs against patterns, generates first-draft architecture-decision records. The senior engineer reviews and approves. *The KPI is review-cycle compression and architectural-coherence improvement.*

**Deploy scenario — agent-augmented deployment and SRE operations.** *The pain* — Disney's operations teams manage enormous deployment surface. CI/CD pipelines. Production deployments. Incident response. The agent strategy — agentic SRE assistance during incidents (the Day-Zero scenario from the Deployment Podcast), agent-augmented release coordination (which services upgrade in what order), agent-emit-and-recommend during the deployment window. *The KPI is mean-time-to-recovery and deployment-velocity improvement.*

**KEVEN:** And where these fit in the ladder —

**RILEY:** These typically *enter at L2 or L3* alongside one of the customer-or-operations Wave 1 Services. *They aren't standalone L1 deals.* The build/deploy meta-scenarios are how Disney's *engineering organisation itself* benefits from the agentic-AI work that's being delivered across the segments.

The Account Team should position them as — *"as we deliver Wave 1 against [whatever the customer scenario is], we can also augment your engineering team's build-and-deploy workflows with adjacent agents that benefit from the same canonical and governance investment we're making for Wave 1."* That framing earns the build/deploy Services additional scope without requiring separate L1 entry.

### The GSSC Lab for Disney demonstrations

**KEVEN:** Last big topic for this episode. The GSSC Lab for Disney conversations. Because the Lab is *the* demo-substrate that makes the ladder land at Disney.

**RILEY:** The GSSC Lab — covered architecturally in the Sellers Podcast — is Deloitte's pre-built Microsoft-native environment with Agent 365 and APEX fleets running. *For Disney specifically*, the Account Team has access to TMT-MED scenarios pre-loaded in the Lab. The 16 Disney-fit scenarios can be demonstrated *in the Lab* with synthetic data shaped to look like Disney's portfolio.

The Lab use cases for Disney conversations —

*L1 use case.* During the Power BI Heatmap conversation, the seller can pop into the Lab on the tablet and *show a working Disney-fit scenario.* Live. Real agent. Real audit row. *"This is what the streaming-churn agent looks like operating live; the heatmap I just showed you tells you where to deploy this pattern at Disney."*

*L2 use case.* During the Wave 1 SOW conversation, the Lab serves as *the design substrate.* The team designs the Disney Wave 1 in the Lab, with Disney's input on data shapes and KPI envelope. The L2 SOW proposes *promotion of the Lab-validated pattern into Disney's tenant for production.*

*L3 use case.* The Lab serves as *the platform for testing new Services* before they go into Disney's production tenant. Disney's CoE can use the Lab for the test-and-rehearse motion across the multi-Service fleet.

*Independence boundary.* The Lab is *Deloitte tenant, synthetic data.* No Disney data ever enters the Lab. Promotion to Disney's tenant is an L2 motion under Disney's separate Microsoft licensing. *This separation is the Independence-clean demo path.*

### One disagreement

**RILEY:** Pushback time.

**KEVEN:** Go.

**RILEY:** I want to push on the *L1 timeline.* Five days for a Power BI Heatmap is the Sellers Podbook standard. *For Disney specifically* — I think the Account Team should plan for *eight to ten days* for the L1 build. Disney's data access requires non-trivial procurement-and-data-sharing-agreement work, even for a synthetic-data-driven heatmap. *Trying to compress to five days produces a thinner heatmap.* Better to commit to eight to ten and produce something genuinely useful.

**KEVEN:** And the seller's framing —

**RILEY:** Reframe to the leader — *"I'll build you a Disney-fit heatmap mapping the 16 curated scenarios to your segment portfolio. Eight to ten days, given the data-sharing setup. Free. You own the .pbix."* Most Disney leaders won't quibble about three to five extra days. The output is better.

**KEVEN:** Agree. *Don't compress the L1 timeline if it costs quality.* Disney leaders care about quality more than they care about velocity at L1.
