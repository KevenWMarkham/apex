**KEVEN:** Set up the structure.

The ladder climbs *per studio* at Disney Studios. Marvel ladder. Pixar ladder. Walt Disney Pictures ladder. Lucasfilm ladder. 20th Century ladder. Searchlight ladder. *Six concurrent ladders.* Each climbs independently with creative-autonomy-respecting framing.

### L3 for Disney Studios — Studios-wide AXLE CoE

**EDEN:** L3. The expansion to Studios-wide Agentic CoE.

**KEVEN:** L3 is the move from one Wave 1 SOW at one studio to *a multi-Service, multi-studio fleet running across the Studios sub-business.* The framework's play here is — establishing a Studios-wide Agentic Center of Excellence inside Disney Entertainment's technology organisation. *Not just delivering more individual Services to more studios.* Establishing the operating model through which Disney runs the Studios-wide agentic portfolio.

Dollar value at L3 — *five to twenty million dollars services revenue annually.* Expansion revenue across multiple studios.

What's in the CoE by L3. *Disney is operating typically four to six Services across multiple studios* — production-pipeline plays, marketing plays, rights-and-licensing plays. Plus the cross-studio platform and governance services. *The CoE reports into Disney Entertainment's technology leadership.*

L3 buyer — *the steering committee.* Chairperson of Disney Entertainment. CTO and CIO at the parent level. Studio presidents who have Wave 1 in production. CFO involvement for cross-studio cost and ROI.

Dollar value at L4 — *twenty to fifty million dollars and up* services revenue annually at scale.

L4 buyer — Chairperson of Disney Entertainment. Plus Disney Entertainment senior leadership. Possibly parent-company executive sponsorship.

### The 30-Minute Framework tuned for Studios

**EDEN:** The 30-Min Framework at Studios.

**KEVEN:** The five questions from the Sellers Handbook — *open with the metric, reverse the timeline, surface the constraint, test the scenario, confirm the bundle* — with Studios-specific tunings.

*Open with the metric.* For Studios leaders, the metric varies. The Marvel President thinks in *franchise health* — recent title performance plus upcoming-slate quality. The Pixar Chief Creative Officer thinks in *production-cycle on-schedule rate.* The Searchlight President thinks in *awards-nomination capture rate.* The studios CFO thinks in *per-title ROI* and *slate-level margin.* *Listen for which metric the leader names; adjust the conversation.*

*Reverse the timeline.* For Studios leaders, "ahead in 90 days" often means *ahead of a specific upcoming title release or production milestone.* Greenlight committee meeting. Production-cycle phase transition. Awards-season campaign launch. Listen for the specific milestone.

*Surface the constraint.* Studios constraints are usually *cycle-time* (decisions take too long) or *coordination-quality* (alignment across stakeholders is hard) or *signal-velocity* (data arrives too late for decisions). Less often capacity in the conventional sense.

*Test the scenario.* Name a *specific Studios play*. Not "an agent." *"An agent that composes greenlight-decision substrate for your green-light committee."* Or *"an agent that surfaces trailer-performance composite within hours of vendor delivery."* Specificity earns engagement.

*Confirm the bundle shape.* Studios bundles. Plus the GSSC Lab demo scheduled.

### Independence specifics for Disney Studios

**EDEN:** Independence at Studios.

**KEVEN:** Disney is Independence-managed at the global level. Disney Studios specifically requires three additional considerations.

*Note one — talent-and-AI policy scope discipline.* Plays that touch creative-authorship workflows (writing, performance, performance-likeness, dialogue replacement) require explicit positioning around the WGA, SAG-AFTRA, and DGA AI provisions. *The Account Team's framing is operational-and-analytical augmentation, never creative replacement.*

*Note two — financial-reporting-adjacent scope avoidance.* Plays that touch box office reporting, residual calculations, talent compensation, or financial-reporting metrics require Independence review before scope finalisation. *Avoid plays that touch external financial reporting* — that's audit-adjacent.

*Note three — rights and licensing scope discipline.* Plays in the rights-and-licensing area (Plays 14-15 from Episode 4) require careful scope — Deloitte recommends what the client should build; the client makes rights-and-licensing decisions. *Deloitte does not advise on the substantive licensing decision, only on the operational composition of the licensing-status data.*

The standard discipline from the Disney Account Podbook applies — two-contract model, no co-sell, careful coordination with global Independence office.

### The 90-day calendar at Disney Studios

**KEVEN:** And the calendar.

**EDEN:** Six categories of activity.

*Days 1-15.* Relationship refresh. *Per-studio relationship-mapping.* Each of the six studios has its own relationship cadence. Confirm Tier 1 and Tier 2 relationships per studio.

*Days 15-30.* L1 wedge attempt window. Schedule the Power BI Heatmap conversation with two-or-three studios where the relationship is strongest. Schedule the GSSC Lab demo.

*Days 30-60.* Discovery and scenario-matching. Apply the 30-Min Framework with the L1 buyer at each engaged studio. *Parallel discovery across multiple studios* — each delivers its own index card. *Initiate the Independence-scope review for the leading Wave 1 candidate.*

*Days 60-75.* BVA construction for the leading Wave 1 candidate (likely one specific studio's play). Internal Deloitte review including the Studios-specific Independence review.

*Days 75-90.* Wave 1 SOW window at the chosen studio. SOW negotiation. Wave 1 kickoff scheduled.

*Days 90+.* Wave 1 kickoff. Wave 1 in production at one studio. Reference value building for the next studio's L1 conversation.

### The Account Team's hardest moments at Studios

**EDEN:** Three hardest moments at Studios specifically.

*Hardest one — getting the wrong studio sequence.* Six studios. The Account Team has bandwidth to land Wave 1 at one studio at a time. If you pick the studio with weakest data foundation, the engagement struggles. If you pick the studio with strongest relationship but weakest leadership-AI-posture, the engagement stalls at L2. *The sequencing matters enormously.* My experience — start with Marvel or Walt Disney Pictures operationally; bring Pixar carefully; Searchlight if awards-campaign management is the natural fit.

*Hardest two — the creative-authorship sensitivity.* Studios leadership is genuinely sensitive about creative-AI use post-strike. *Even with explicit operational-AI-only framing*, some Studios leaders will probe deeply. The Account Team has to be prepared for those conversations and have crisp answers. *If you can't honestly answer how the agent does not touch creative authorship for a specific play, don't propose that play.*

*Hardest three — the windowing-decision moment.* The L4 conversation involves the Chairperson of Disney Entertainment and parent-company senior leadership. *Don't pitch L4 prematurely.* L1-L3 establishes the credibility. L4 happens after multi-year operational track record. *Prematurely pitching L4 is the surest way to end the relationship.*
