## The conversation

### Why an account-specific podcast for the Studios sub-business

**KEVEN:** Set this up. We already have the Disney Account Podcast at the company-wide level. Why also do a Studios-specific podcast?

**EDEN:** Three reasons.

One — *Studios has structurally different economics from the rest of Disney.* Streaming, Parks, and Consumer Products are continuous-operations businesses. *Studios is a portfolio-of-bets business.* Each film or series is a discrete production with a specific budget, schedule, and risk profile. The Account Team has to think about this account *title-by-title* in a way that doesn't apply to the streaming or parks businesses.

Two — *the agentic-AI plays for Studios are distinct.* The Disney Account Podcast covered streaming, operations, and back-office plays. *Studios has its own play set* — content development, greenlight decisioning, production pipeline, VFX/animation coordination, marketing-and-distribution decisions, awards strategy, rights and licensing. These aren't covered cleanly in the company-wide podcast.

Three — *the talent and creative-rights dimension is unique.* Post the 2023 WGA and SAG-AFTRA settlements, AI use with respect to creative talent is heavily governed. *The Account Team needs to be precise about how APEX plays are positioned at studios* — to assist operational and analytical work, *not* to displace or threaten creative-authorship roles. Episode 5 covers this in operational detail; this episode sets up the framing.

### The six studios — name them correctly

**KEVEN:** Walk me through the studios as a portfolio.

**EDEN:** Six studios under Disney Entertainment.

*Walt Disney Pictures.* Burbank. The flagship — Disney-branded family theatrical features. Modern releases span live-action remakes of the animated classics plus original Disney-branded family theatrical content.

*Pixar Animation Studios.* Emeryville. Long-cycle animation features. Four-to-five-year production cycles per feature. Distinctive culture and pipeline.

*Marvel Studios.* Burbank. The Marvel Cinematic Universe — theatrical features plus Disney+ original series. The dominant franchise in the studios portfolio.

*Lucasfilm.* San Francisco. Star Wars theatrical features and Disney+ originals. Plus the Indiana Jones franchise. Plus ILM — Industrial Light and Magic — the VFX division that serves Lucasfilm productions and external clients.

*20th Century Studios.* Century City, LA. The former Fox studio acquired by Disney in 2019. Mass-market theatrical content broader than the Disney-branded slate.

*Searchlight Pictures.* Beverly Hills. Specialty / awards-focused theatrical. Smaller scale, higher per-title prestige. Searchlight's value proposition is awards-season performance and specialty-theatrical viability.

**KEVEN:** And the integration —

**EDEN:** *Each studio operates with significant creative autonomy* under its own president and chief creative leadership. Disney Entertainment Chairperson oversees all six. *Operational integration happens at the corporate level* — distribution, marketing, finance, business affairs, talent management. But the *creative decisions* — what gets greenlit, who directs, who stars, what the cut looks like — stay at the individual-studio level.

This matters enormously for the Account Team. *The buyer for any APEX play is studio-specific.* A Marvel production-pipeline play sells to Marvel Studios leadership. A Pixar animation play sells to Pixar leadership. *Don't pitch one play to all six studios simultaneously.* Each studio has its own decision-maker, its own operational culture, its own technology posture.

### The economics of the studios business in 2026

**EDEN:** Let me walk the economics for the Account Team member who isn't familiar.

**KEVEN:** Go.

**EDEN:** A theatrical-release film at Disney scale today has *roughly the following economics envelope.*

*Production budget* — for a Marvel tentpole, can be 200-300 million dollars production. For a Pixar feature, similar range. For a mid-tier Walt Disney Pictures release, 100-200 million. For a Searchlight specialty release, 10-25 million.

*Marketing budget* — typically 50-100 percent of production budget. So a Marvel tentpole carries 100-200 million in marketing alongside its production cost. *Total title cost approaches half a billion dollars at the tentpole level.*

*Theatrical opening* — domestic three-day opening of 70-150 million for a Marvel tentpole. International opening simultaneous. *Total theatrical run* — multiples of opening, typically 2.5-4× depending on hold and word-of-mouth.

*Post-theatrical windowing* — streaming on Disney+, eventually licensed to broadcast or third-party platforms in some cases. International theatrical sometimes lags domestic.

*Long-tail revenue* — Consumer Products licensing, merchandise, parks integration, franchise-extension feeds for Disney+ original series. *The franchise-extension revenue often exceeds the original theatrical revenue over the long tail.*

**KEVEN:** And the risk profile —

**EDEN:** *Hit-and-miss volatility at the title level.* A miss-tier theatrical performance at the Marvel level can produce a 200-million-dollar write-down. A hit can produce a billion-dollar global run. *Per-title variance is huge.* The studio's annual P&L is the result of a portfolio of these bets.

The Studios business as a whole — across all six studios — produces multi-billion-dollar revenue annually. Margin is highly sensitive to the hit rate. *Anything that improves the hit rate, compresses the cost of misses, or extracts more value from hits is structurally important to studio P&L.*

### Five strategic pressures right now

**EDEN:** OK. The five pressures that pressure every Studios conversation.

**KEVEN:** Walk them.

**EDEN:** *Pressure one — content cost inflation.* Marvel, Star Wars, and Pixar production costs have escalated across the 2010s and into the 2020s. Per-title budgets are at historic highs. *The cost of a miss is higher than ever.* Pressure on per-title ROI is real and ongoing.

*Pressure two — hit-and-miss volatility.* Recent Marvel projects have underperformed audience expectations. Some Pixar releases have been shifted from theatrical to direct-to-Disney+. *The portfolio risk is reasserting itself* after a decade of seemingly unstoppable Marvel hits and theatrical-first Pixar economics. The leadership question is *how to manage portfolio risk while still investing at the scale franchise content requires.*

*Pressure three — windowing decisions.* Theatrical-only vs theatrical-plus-streaming vs streaming-direct decisions have become *strategic, not procedural.* Each decision reshapes revenue, marketing economics, and talent relationships (talent often has theatrical-specific compensation provisions). *The decision per title is now an executive-committee discussion, not a default.*

*Pressure four — talent dynamics and AI policy.* The 2023 WGA and SAG-AFTRA settlements include AI-use provisions that are *binding* for the studios. Any AI use that touches creative work (writing, performance, performance-likeness, dialogue replacement) must be navigated within the contract framework. *Plus residuals, talent compensation structures, and union-relationship management.* The talent dimension is enormous and politically sensitive.

*Pressure five — awards strategy and brand differentiation.* For Searchlight and (selectively) 20th Century Studios, *awards-season performance is the brand.* Oscar nominations and wins drive subsequent talent attachment, distribution leverage, and specialty-theatrical viability. *Awards-campaign management is a year-round operational discipline*, not a seasonal effort.

**KEVEN:** And the strategic narrative APEX can offer Studios leadership —

**EDEN:** *"You have the data — title performance, audience response, production telemetry, marketing campaign performance. Today the data informs decisions that are still made on six-month cycles. APEX gives you agent-driven decision support that operates on six-hour cycles, with governance your audit organisation will accept, on a Microsoft platform you already substantially use."* That sentence lands.

### Why APEX now at Studios specifically

**KEVEN:** And the case for now —

**EDEN:** Three reasons.

One — *the platform is mature.* Microsoft Fabric, Foundry, Agent Framework, Purview — all GA. The infrastructure to deploy governed agentic AI exists today.

Two — *the studios' data is rich.* Audience science data, production telemetry, marketing campaign performance, distribution windowing analytics, talent and rights data. *Each studio has its own data estate — variably integrated.* APEX's canonical foundation gives the studio a way to compose across the estate without forcing premature integration.

Three — *the strategic pressure is operational.* The cost-and-volatility pressure on the Studios business is real and immediate. Leadership is investing in operational discipline. *Anything that produces measurable per-title ROI improvement is welcome and visible.* APEX produces those improvements.

### A reading I want to do

**KEVEN:** I want to read from a recent industry analysis on studios economics.

**EDEN:** Read it.

**KEVEN:** [reading, paraphrased]

*"The studios business has entered a phase where data-driven decision support is no longer optional. The economics of tentpole production demand earlier signal on audience response, tighter coordination across the production pipeline, smarter windowing decisions, and more disciplined marketing-spend optimisation. Studios that deploy AI-driven operational decision support — without compromising the creative-authorship of writers, directors, and performers — will manage portfolio risk materially better than those that don't."*

[pause]

**EDEN:** *"Without compromising the creative-authorship."* That's the line that bounds every Studios agentic-AI conversation. *Operational and analytical augmentation, not creative replacement.* The Account Team's posture has to honour that boundary explicitly.

### The 15 APEX plays — preview

**KEVEN:** And the 15 curated plays for the Studios business —

**EDEN:** Four groups.

*Content Development and Greenlight* — four plays. IP and franchise opportunity scoring. Greenlight decision support. Audience science and sentiment. Talent and casting intelligence. *Episode 2 walks these in depth.*

*Production* — five plays. Production schedule and budget intelligence. VFX pipeline optimisation. Pixar animation pipeline. Post-production workflow. Production safety and wellness. *Episode 3.*

*Marketing, Distribution, and Rights* — four plays. Trailer performance and audience testing. Marketing campaign optimisation. Theatrical-vs-streaming windowing. Awards campaign management. *Episode 4.*

### One disagreement

**KEVEN:** Pushback.

**EDEN:** Let me push on the *six-studio framing.* I think the Account Team should think about this as *three sub-portfolios* with different operational rhythms.

**KEVEN:** Go.

**EDEN:** *Sub-portfolio one — the franchise tentpole studios.* Marvel and Lucasfilm. Multi-year IP-development cycles. Marvel-Cinematic-Universe / Star-Wars-Universe interconnection. Very high-budget productions. Heavy VFX. Long-term franchise planning.

*Sub-portfolio two — the family animation studios.* Walt Disney Pictures (animation division) and Pixar. Long-cycle animation production. Distinctive creative cultures. Distinct talent pools (animators, story artists, technical directors).

*Sub-portfolio three — the broader theatrical studios.* 20th Century Studios and Searchlight. Lower-budget-per-title. Higher per-title variance. Awards strategy matters disproportionately for Searchlight.

*Each sub-portfolio has different operational pressures, different technology readiness, different leadership posture toward agentic AI.* The Account Team's conversation differs by sub-portfolio.

**KEVEN:** And the implication for play-sequencing —

**EDEN:** *Marvel and Pixar are the most mature data estates and the most receptive to operational-AI conversation.* The production-pipeline and VFX-pipeline plays land first there. *Searchlight is interested but smaller-scale* — awards-campaign-management is the right Searchlight entry. *20th Century is in transition* under the Disney umbrella — the integration with Disney systems is still working through.

The Account Team picks the sub-portfolio that matches the buyer's relationship strength.

### What to carry forward to Episode 2

**KEVEN:** Three things.

One — *Studios is six studios with creative autonomy, integrated at the corporate level.* Every play is studio-specific in execution but composed at the portfolio level.

Two — *five strategic pressures pressure every conversation.* Content cost, hit/miss volatility, windowing, talent-and-AI policy, awards strategy.

Three — *AI plays at Studios must respect creative authorship boundaries.* Operational and analytical augmentation. Never creative replacement. The Account Team's posture is unambiguous.

**EDEN:** Next episode — *Content Development and Greenlight Plays.* Four plays — IP scoring, greenlight decision support, audience science, talent and casting intelligence. The earliest-stage agentic plays in the studios pipeline.

**KEVEN:** See you there.
