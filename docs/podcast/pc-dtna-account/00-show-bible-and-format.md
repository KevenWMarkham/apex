## 2. The conversational design rules

All rules from the Trilogy and Disney podcasts apply:

- **No chapter or section number citations in dialogue.** Hosts know the framework.
- **Historical and industry context opens each episode** — 3-5 minutes setting up DTNA's own history with the segment under discussion.
- **One concept developed fully (4-6 min) before transitioning.** No topic-switching.
- **Real disagreement between hosts** in every episode — Keven and Marcus have different priors.
- **One quote-and-react moment** per episode from primary sources (DTNA / Daimler Truck Holding earnings, industry analyses, APEX framework documents).
- **No announced segments.**
- **`Further Reading` section** in each .md.

## 3. DTNA-specific style additions

### Rule 1 · Treat DTNA as a specific company with specific people

DTNA is not "a generic truck OEM." Major Class 8 product is the Freightliner Cascadia. Premium and vocational is Western Star. Buses are Thomas Built. Engines are Detroit. Use these brand names correctly.

When discussing DTNA leadership, use *titles and roles*, not invented names. The CEO, the COO, the VP of Manufacturing, the VP of Connected Vehicle, the President of Detroit, the President of Thomas Built. These are real roles even if we don't name the individuals.

### Rule 2 · Know the brands and the plants

The hosts should sound like people who have walked the Mt. Holly truck plant, watched Cascadias roll off Cleveland, NC, seen Detroit engines at Redford and Saltillo. If you have, you sound credible. If you don't, sound like you've at least read the public-facing facility list.

### Rule 3 · The three strategic themes

Episodes weave through three strategic themes that pressure the business:

1. **Electrification.** eCascadia, eM2, eEconic. The CARB Advanced Clean Trucks rule. The build-out of charging infrastructure for fleet customers. The cost-and-capacity question for the OEM.
2. **Autonomy.** Daimler Truck Holding owns Torc Robotics. Level 4 highway-trucking development. The technology bet that's been multi-year and is approaching production milestones.
3. **Freight cycle.** Class 8 orders are highly cyclical. Up cycles and down cycles affect everything downstream — dealer health, fleet customer behaviour, OEM revenue, manufacturing throughput planning.

Every episode references at least one of the three.

### Rule 4 · The DTNA Independence considerations

Daimler Truck Holding AG is a publicly listed German company; the audit relationship is in Germany. Deloitte's services posture for DTNA in North America requires careful coordination with Deloitte global structure. The Account Team should:

- Treat DTNA as Independence-managed — coordinate with the global Independence office for the Daimler Truck audit relationship
- Apply the standard two-contract model — Microsoft licensing directly with DTNA; Deloitte services SOW directly with DTNA
- *No co-sell with Microsoft.* The recommendation of Microsoft is on the merits, not on partner-channel compensation
- The DTNA-Torc-Daimler-Truck-Holding tripartite structure requires precise scope discipline — engagement scope is DTNA-specific or Torc-specific or parent-level

Episode 5 names specific Independence considerations for this account.

### Rule 5 · The AXLE Practice mapping

DTNA scenarios map almost entirely to the **AXLE Practice** — the automotive Practice in the framework. Some plays draw on adjacent Practice patterns:

- **Aftermarket parts and service** scenarios share DNA with the ICE Practice (Industrial · Construction · Equipment, which includes heavy-equipment aftermarket).
- **Fleet customer experience** scenarios touch the TH Practice patterns (Travel & Hospitality, where loyalty and customer-journey orchestration patterns developed).
- **Contact center for fleet customers** uses the cross-Practice contact-center pattern.

But the *primary* Practice anchor is AXLE.

## 5. Recurring rhythms

Each episode has these rhythms, not announced:

| Rhythm | Length |
|---|---|
| **DTNA scene** — an anonymised but specific moment (a plant, a dealer, a fleet customer's office) | 3-5 min |
| **The DTNA context** — historical / business context for the scenario under discussion | 3-5 min |
| **The plays** — each play walked: pain → strategy → Service → KPI | 12-18 min |
| **Disagreement** — a specific point Keven and Marcus diverge on | 3-5 min |
| **The takeaway** — what the Account Team should do this week | 1-2 min |

Total: 25-30 minutes per episode.
