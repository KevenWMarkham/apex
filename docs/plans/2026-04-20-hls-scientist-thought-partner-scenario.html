HLS Scientist Thought-Partner

A scenario brainstorm for the APEX HLS Practice: AI-augmented lab notebook with regulatory-grade decision capture and end-to-end product-lifecycle threading.

Status: Brainstorm — pre-design Date: 2026-04-20 Design reference: APEX_Design.md §15 HLS

What we're building

A notebook that thinks with the scientist — and leaves a regulator-defensible trail without anyone having to stop and format it that way.

The gap this closes

Scientists today keep two (or three) records in parallel: a paper lab notebook, a digital ELN, and a mental model of "what this means for the product / the regulator." The three rarely align, which costs time at submission and creates compliance risk.

The promise

One surface where the scientist narrates their work in natural language. An AI thought-partner listens, structures, flags the moments where a decision is being made, and captures that decision once — with signed provenance — so it flows automatically into the downstream regulatory submission and product record.

The three questions APEX answers — applied here

APEX questionWhat that looks like for this scientist
What should happen next? The agent proposes: "This observation looks like a protocol deviation — do you want to log a PD and notify QA?"
Who must approve, and under what rules? Deterministic gate: protocol deviations → HITL to QA; hypothesis refinements → ACK_ONLY; typos → ZERO_TOUCH.
What audit trail does the decision leave? 21 CFR Part 11-compliant signed record: who, when, why, what-was-known, model version, prompt version, policy version. Immutable.
APEX positioning: This is not an ELN replacement. It's a thought-partner that sits alongside ELN/LIMS systems, consuming their canonical data via hlscml-mcp and writing structured decisions back to them through the Silver layer.

Scientist personas

Different scientists, different regulatory surfaces, different interaction shapes. Picking an anchor persona determines the first increment; others become extensions in Wave 2+.

Discovery chemist / bench scientist Earliest

Runs wet-lab experiments, captures hypotheses and results, tracks IP-relevant observations. Mostly GLP-adjacent; heavy IP notebook requirements.

AI earns its keep by
  • Structuring free-form observations into experiment records
  • Suggesting next experiment based on the current result
  • Flagging when an observation changes the IP claim
Regulatory surface
  • GLP, 21 CFR Part 58, IP/patent disclosure timing

CMC scientist Chemistry · Manufacturing · Controls

Owns process development, transitions from lab-scale to GMP manufacturing, authors CMC sections of regulatory submissions (IND, NDA, BLA).

AI earns its keep by
  • Watching process-parameter drift and flagging deviations before they become PDs
  • Assembling submission-ready CMC narrative from bench notes
  • Cross-referencing ICH Q8/Q9/Q10 requirements
Regulatory surface
  • GMP, ICH Q8–Q11, 21 CFR 210/211, CMC sections of IND/NDA

Clinical researcher / investigator Clinical

Executes clinical protocols at a site or sponsor level, captures subject-level events, writes sections of the Clinical Study Report.

AI earns its keep by
  • Catching protocol deviations at the moment the scientist describes them
  • Linking adverse events to subject FHIR records and safety signal queries
  • Drafting CSR sections from structured notes
Regulatory surface
  • GCP, ICH E6(R2), 21 CFR 50/56/312, CDISC SDTM/ADaM

Biostatistician / data scientist Analysis

Owns Statistical Analysis Plans, runs pre-specified and exploratory analyses, answers regulator questions during review.

AI earns its keep by
  • Capturing the rationale for every SAP change (the part regulators grill)
  • Tracking pre-specified vs post-hoc analyses
  • Drafting response narratives to regulator info-requests
Regulatory surface
  • ICH E9, CDISC ADaM, regulatory Q&A traceability

Medical device engineer Device

Runs design-control activities, produces Design History File, manages risk analysis through the product lifecycle.

AI earns its keep by
  • Keeping the DHF live instead of assembled-at-the-end
  • Linking risk-analysis updates to design changes
  • Drafting 510(k) / PMA sections from design-control artefacts
Regulatory surface
  • 21 CFR 820 (QSR), ISO 13485, ISO 14971 (risk), IEC 62304 (SaMD), EU MDR

Regulatory writer / reviewer Adjacent

Not a "scientist" in the bench sense, but a heavy consumer of whatever the thought-partner captures. Benefits when upstream capture is structured.

AI earns its keep by
  • Surfacing all open regulatory decisions across a product line
  • Diffing the current submission draft against the captured decision record

One moment, illustrated

A CMC scientist at the bench, 10:34 AM on a Tuesday. This is the shape of the interaction — not a script, but the kind of thing the AI thought-partner enables.

Scientist

(dictating into Teams while working) "Running batch APX-2026-044. pH came in at 7.8 instead of the 7.4 target. Temperature was 2 degrees high on the start — I think the heating element is drifting. Product looks fine visually. Going to proceed but flag it."

AI

Two things caught my attention. (1) The pH deviation is outside the ±0.2 window in the process validation record for APX-2026 — that's a protocol deviation by the CMC definition, not just a note. (2) You said "temperature was 2 degrees high" but didn't specify units or duration — can you confirm so I log it precisely?

Scientist

"2 degrees C, for about 15 minutes at the start. Confirmed."

AI

Thanks. I'm proposing this as a Protocol Deviation record against APX-2026 PV-3.1. It's material to the CMC section. Before I write it, two decisions are yours: (a) is the batch salvageable per §5.2 of the validation plan, or does this invalidate it? (b) Do you want to proceed or halt?

If it's salvageable and you proceed, this needs QA concurrence before batch release. I can ping QA now so the review is parallel to your continuing work.

Gate

HITL: batch-disposition — Protocol Deviation + proceed/halt. Options: Salvageable, proceed, QA concur · Invalidate batch · Halt pending QA. Scientist's choice is the decision; QA concur is the second signature.

Scientist

"Salvageable, proceed, flag for QA."

Audit

Signed decision row written to ledger-mcp: decision_id, agent_id, invoking_identity (scientist's Entra ID), three version SHAs pinned, reasoning trace captured (DLP-scrubbed), PD linked to batch record, QA notification sent. Total scientist friction added: 45 seconds.

What's different from today: Without the thought-partner, this deviation might end up as a line in the notebook, recognised as a PD weeks later during batch-record review, and reconstructed imperfectly for the submission. Here it's captured as a structured, signed, linked decision at the moment it's made, with zero additional forms to fill.

How the AI participates

Same underlying agent infrastructure, four distinct interaction modes. Most sessions blend them.

Mode 1 — Passive structurer

Scientist dictates or types freely. AI listens silently, structures the narrative into an ELN-ready record (experiment, observation, result, hypothesis). Surfaces the structured version for a thumbs-up.

HITL
  • ACK_ONLY on structured record acceptance

Mode 2 — Clarity prompter

When the scientist says something ambiguous or contradicts an earlier note, the AI asks a single targeted question. Not about the science — about the record. "You said the temperature was high but didn't specify duration — was this sustained or transient?"

HITL
  • Conversational; no formal gate

Mode 3 — Regulatory sentinel

Watches for patterns that map to regulatory triggers (PD, AE, SAE, OOS, deviation, change control). Raises these explicitly. Never silent-files a regulatory event.

HITL
  • HITL on every regulatory-grade decision
  • ESCALATION when safety signal thresholds cross

Mode 4 — Lifecycle threader

Keeps an internal graph: decision → affected-record → downstream-artefact (CMC section, CSR section, DHF entry, submission module). When the scientist makes a decision today, the lifecycle threader knows which submission section it will flow into tomorrow.

HITL
  • Per-downstream-artefact, at assembly time

MVP recommendation: Pick one persona × one regime beyond 21 CFR Part 11 × one product-lifecycle stage. Expand from there. Trying to cover all regimes from day one is how these projects die.

How a decision threads through

Example: A CMC scientist decides during preclinical that the acceptable range for a critical quality attribute (CQA) is ±5%.

  • Preclinical: Decision row written, linked to CQA specification.
  • Clinical Phase 1: Batch-release specs pull the ±5% from the decision row; any batch outside triggers a PD; scientist who set the ±5% is referenced in the PD record.
  • Clinical Phase 3: Process validation uses the same spec; if a validation batch exceeds ±5%, the lifecycle threader surfaces the original rationale to the investigator.
  • Submission: The CMC section of the NDA is drafted referencing the original decision row and every downstream batch-record event that tested against it.
  • Manufacturing: Commercial-scale deviation from ±5% triggers a change-control evaluation; the decision row is the starting reference.
  • Post-market: If an adverse event cluster correlates with lot-level CQA values, the threader surfaces the decision chain back to the original rationale.
Every stage is the same underlying decision row, referenced forward. Nobody retypes anything. Nobody reconciles notebooks. The auditor walks trace_id forward or backward.

Agents composed

Note-capture agent

Ambient dictation / typing ingest; structures into HLSCML records; zero-touch unless ambiguous.

Clarity agent

Targeted single-question probes when the record has a gap that will bite later.

Regulatory sentinel

Classifies every utterance against a regulatory-trigger taxonomy (PD, AE, SAE, OOS, deviation, change-control).

Decision recorder

Takes a classified trigger + the scientist's choice; produces the signed decision row; fires HITL to the right approver group.

Lifecycle threader

Maintains the decision → downstream-artefact graph. Surfaces upstream decisions when a downstream artefact is touched.

Submission assembler

At submission time, assembles CMC/CSR/DHF sections from decision rows + source artefacts. Never starts from scratch.

Experience surface

Microsoft 365 Copilot / Teams channel

Scientist narrates in Teams (voice or text); Copilot shows structured record, classifications, and HITL cards. Lowest friction for knowledge-worker scientists (CMC, biostat, regulatory). Uses Copilot Studio for the HITL surface.

Native ELN plugin (Benchling / LabWare)

Embedded UI inside the existing ELN. Right for bench scientists who already live in the ELN. Requires a plugin per ELN vendor — higher up-front cost.

OneNote / Microsoft Loop

Loop components embedded in pages. Good for teams who already standardise on OneNote. Less opinionated about structure.

Power App canvas app

Purpose-built app. Most opinionated; hardest sell if the scientist already has an ELN they prefer.

Open questions to resolve before design

Each row below is a decision that materially changes what we build. Answer any you have strong views on; I'll default the rest.

Q1 — Anchor persona

Which scientist persona do we build for first? CMC is a strong default: clear regulatory surface, strong ROI, heavy decision volume.

Q2 — Lifecycle scope for MVP

End-to-end is the North Star, but MVP needs a smaller bite. Preclinical + Phase 1? CMC-only (process dev through validation)? One clinical phase?

Q3 — Passive vs active AI

Passive structurer + clarity prompter is a safer MVP. Active (proposing next experiments, drafting sections) is more ambitious and carries more risk of hallucination / scientist rejection.

Q4 — Experience surface

Microsoft 365 Copilot / Teams channel is the fastest to ship and leverages identity + DLP already. ELN plugin is most native but slow to ship.

Q5 — Decision taxonomy

What counts as a "regulatory decision"? A fixed taxonomy (PD / AE / SAE / OOS / deviation / change-control) is defensible but may miss edge cases. An extensible taxonomy is flexible but harder to govern.

Q6 — 21 CFR Part 11 validation

APEX can produce the signed-record shape by design, but end-to-end Part-11 validation of the system is a separate commitment. Is that in MVP scope or a follow-on?

Q7 — Integration depth

One ELN vendor (e.g. Benchling) or ELN-agnostic via HLSCML canonical layer? Agnostic is more work up front but compounds faster.

Q8 — Privacy + PHI

How much PHI is in scope? Scientist notes often brush PHI (subject IDs, adverse-event narratives). Default: tokenise at the boundary per APEX standard; surface only tokens to the AI.

My recommended MVP (pending your feedback): CMC scientist persona · GMP + 21 CFR Part 11 + ICH Q8/Q10 · preclinical process dev through Phase 1 batch release · passive structurer + clarity + regulatory sentinel (modes 1–3) · Microsoft 365 Copilot / Teams as experience surface · HLSCML + StudyML canonical layer · decision taxonomy fixed to the six canonical triggers.
