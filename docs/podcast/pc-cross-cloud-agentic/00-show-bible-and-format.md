# Show Bible · The Cross-Cloud Agentic Podcast

Format, voice, Independence, and house-style rules for the eight-episode Cross-Cloud Agentic Podcast. Adds to (does not replace) the Trilogy, Disney, DTNA, and Toyota show bibles. **The defining constraint: this is the first podcast in the family written for Microsoft sellers as a class, and the substance is genuinely cross-cloud.**

---

This series speaks to the seller carrying a Microsoft quota into a CIO conversation where AWS and GCP are equally on the table.

| **Keven** | `en-US-AndrewNeural` | rate +0%, pitch +0 Hz | Trilogy continuity host. The practitioner. 22+ years on the Microsoft platform. Warm, confident, plain-spoken. Has been in the room when the CIO asked the hard cross-cloud question. |

**The both-Andrew situation.** Andrew Neural and Andrew Multilingual share a base timbre. The personas are differentiated three ways:

1. **Rate and pitch tuning** — Keven at +0%/+0Hz, Reid at -3%/-2Hz. Reid lands slower, slightly lower, more deliberate.
2. **Vocabulary** — Keven uses *"here's the thing"* and *"so what I tell sellers is"*; Reid uses *"let me push back on that"* and *"on AWS this works differently…"*
3. **Sentence shape** — Keven leads with the conclusion and walks back; Reid leads with the constraint and walks forward.

The tuning is the safety net; the personas are the differentiation.

**Structural-disagreement dynamic.** Every episode has at least one substantive disagreement-and-convergence moment. Reid is empowered — by design — to enforce cross-cloud honesty. When Keven overclaims for Microsoft, Reid pushes back. The disagreement is real, never performative. The convergence is the point: two practitioners who have built on all three clouds arrive at the architectural answer together, not by Microsoft-loyalty.

## 3. The Acceleration Framework — naming convention

The architectural approach the series teaches is referred to on tape as:

- *"the Acceleration Framework"* (preferred)
- *"an Agentic AI Acceleration Framework"* (variant on first introduction)
- *"the framework"* (when context is established)

**Never named "APEX" on tape.** The framework name is descriptive, not branded. This is the generic series — the framework is platform-agnostic in its substance and its naming.

---

## 4. Independence rules

The rule that protects everything. Spoken on tape:

- **Deloitte recommends.** Microsoft is the platform Deloitte recommends — on the technical and economic merits. The client contracts with Microsoft directly.
- **Two contracts.** Microsoft licensing on Microsoft paper. Deloitte services on Deloitte paper. Said exactly that way in Episode 1 and again in Episode 8.
- **Three contracts when NVIDIA is in scope.** Client-NVIDIA, client-Microsoft, client-Deloitte — three separate paper paths.
- **No compensation flows from Microsoft to Deloitte** for influencing the client's cloud choice. Said at least once per series, plainly.

### Forbidden vocabulary — absent from this podcast on purpose

These words never appear on tape as endorsements: *co-sell* · *alliance* · *strategic partnership* · *channel partner* · *our partnership with Microsoft*. If a take drifts into partner-channel language, it is a re-record.

## 6. Production rules

All rules from prior show bibles apply, with these emphases:

- **Cold-open scenes** — every episode opens with 3-5 minutes of lived experience. A CIO meeting where the cross-cloud question came up. A FinOps review where token spend went sideways. A late-night call where the auditor asked for replay evidence. Not abstract framing.
- **Stair-step pedagogy.** No concept used in episode N+1 that was not introduced in episode N. The five principles are named in Episode 1 and reinforced in every subsequent episode.
- **Real disagreement per episode.** Reid and Keven have different priors. The disagreement is substantive, not performative. At least one moment per episode where Reid pushes back on a Microsoft claim and Keven engages with the constraint.
- **Quote-and-react from primary sources** — Microsoft Learn, AWS Well-Architected, Google Cloud Architecture Center, NIST AI RMF, EU AI Act text, ISO 42001, Gartner / Forrester / IDC analysis. **Paraphrased; never fabricate verbatim quotes.** Always with attribution.
- **What-to-carry-forward closer.** 1-2 minutes naming what the seller should take into their next conversation.
- **Further Reading section** in each `.md` — primary sources only.
- **No announced segments.** Structure is felt, not narrated.

## 7. What NOT to do

- **Do not name specific clients on tape.** This is the GENERIC series. Reference archetypes only — *a global retailer · a large auto manufacturer · a regulated bank · a hyperscale media company.* If a take names a specific client, it is a re-record.
- **Do not use internal codenames on tape.** APEX, APEX-M, ORCH-01, BRML, CVML, QEML, AAML, SB06, AXLE Practice, LEDGER-all-caps — none of these on tape.
- **Do not use forbidden vocabulary.** *co-sell · alliance · strategic partnership · channel partner · our partnership with Microsoft* — re-record.
- **Do not overclaim Microsoft.** When Microsoft is genuinely differentiated, say so plainly. When it is a tie, say it is a tie. When AWS or GCP is the better answer for a given constraint, say that — and explain how Microsoft composes with the other answer.
- **Do not dismiss AWS or GCP capabilities.** Bedrock, Vertex AI, Athena Federated Query, BigQuery Omni, Dataplex, Lake Formation — these are mature, well-engineered services. The seller who dismisses them loses the room.
- **Do not dismiss Deloitte AI team pushback.** The series is safe for AI team listeners; when an AI team architect would push back on a claim, the script preempts the pushback honestly.
- **Do not fabricate metrics.** Reference Gartner / Forrester / McKinsey / IDC patterns in spirit — *"industry analysts cite 20-40% QoQ growth in AI consumption"* — without inventing specific dollar figures, percentages, or named-account results.
- **Do not use emojis** in the markdown files.

## 8. Numbers discipline

No fabricated specific numbers on tape. Reference patterns, not figures. *"Industry-cited 20-40% QoQ AI consumption growth"* is fine. *"A 38% improvement at a major retailer"* is not — not in a generic series.

When the script references a model-card limit, a token price, or a context window, it cites vendor documentation. When it references a market trend, it cites the analyst firm by name (Gartner / Forrester / McKinsey / IDC), paraphrased. Never invent verbatim quotes.

---

## 9. Cross-cloud honesty

This is the substance of the series. Specific places where the honest answer points away from Microsoft:

- **Regulatory data residency** in jurisdictions where AWS or GCP has more mature region coverage.
- **AWS-resident data gravity** — when the client's data of record is already in S3 at petabyte scale, the honest first move is often Athena Federated Query, not a Fabric Mirror that re-ingests.
- **GCP-strategic posture** — when the client has committed strategically to GCP and the agentic workload is Vertex AI-native, the right Microsoft posture is *compose, do not displace.*
- **Anthropic-on-Bedrock preference** — when the client has a strong Claude preference and AWS-resident data, Bedrock is the cleaner runtime.

Reid names these explicitly. Keven engages, does not deflect.
