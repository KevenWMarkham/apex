## The conversation

### Why an account-specific podcast for DTNA

**KEVEN:** Set this up. The Trilogy podcasts cover the framework. We've also done a Disney Account Podcast. What does a DTNA-specific podcast add?

**MARCUS:** Three things.

One — *DTNA is the AXLE Practice's most important Account Team conversation in North America.* The Sellers Guide names a global OEM as an anchor account archetype; DTNA is the named instantiation. The Account Team needs the named version, with the actual brands, the actual plants, the actual leadership structure.

Two — *the AXLE-Practice scenarios that map to DTNA aren't generic.* They're specifically tuned for Class 8 commercial truck. Warranty cost spirals look different in commercial trucking than in passenger vehicles — the fleet-customer relationship is different, the dealer dynamics are different, the regulatory bar is different. The Account Team needs the trucking-flavoured version of the framework.

Three — *DTNA's strategic position right now is exceptionally interesting.* The company is navigating *three simultaneous transitions* — electrification, autonomy, and the freight cycle — at the same time. Each one alone would be a multi-year strategic effort. All three together are the defining business challenge of the decade. *APEX has a role in each.* The Account Team needs the picture of how the framework lands across all three.

### Who DTNA is — the structure

**KEVEN:** Walk me through DTNA as a company. Brands, scale, where the manufacturing happens.

**MARCUS:** DTNA is the North American subsidiary of *Daimler Truck Holding AG* — the publicly listed German parent that was *spun off from Daimler AG in 2021.* That spinoff matters strategically because it separated the truck business from Mercedes-Benz passenger cars. Daimler Truck is now a focused commercial-vehicle company globally; DTNA is its largest single market.

Four brands operate under DTNA.

*Freightliner.* The flagship. Class 8 on-highway trucks. Cascadia is the volume product. The single best-selling Class 8 truck in North America. Massive dealer network. Most fleet customers know Freightliner.

*Western Star.* Premium and vocational. Heavier-duty applications — vocational construction, mining, specialty hauling. Lower volume than Freightliner but higher per-unit revenue.

*Thomas Built Buses.* School buses, transit buses. Gastonia, NC. Different end-customer base — school districts, transit authorities, municipalities. Different cycle dynamics than freight.

*Detroit.* The powertrain brand. Engines (DD13, DD15, DD16). Axles. eAxle for electrification. Detroit Connect — the telematics platform. *Detroit is the technology-rich part of the company* and gets disproportionate engineering and digital investment.

**KEVEN:** And the manufacturing footprint —

**MARCUS:** Major North American sites — *Portland, Oregon* for Freightliner HQ and engineering. *Mt. Holly, North Carolina* for Cascadia truck assembly. *Cleveland, North Carolina* for additional Freightliner production. *Saltillo, Mexico* for engine assembly. *Redford, Michigan* for additional Detroit engine production. *Gastonia, North Carolina* for Thomas Built Buses. Plus parts and service operations across the dealer network.

*The Account Team should be aware of every site geographically — at minimum at the "I know what they make there" level.* Some of the Account Team's leadership conversations will reference these sites specifically. The seller who's actually walked Mt. Holly is more credible than the seller who hasn't.

### The market structure

**KEVEN:** And the market context. Class 8 NA is —

**MARCUS:** Class 8 North America is a *highly cyclical, highly consolidated market.* About 250-280 thousand units in a normal cycle year. DTNA carries roughly 40 percent market share — *largest among five major players.* The other major OEMs — PACCAR (which makes Kenworth and Peterbilt), Volvo Trucks NA, Navistar International (now part of TRATON / Volkswagen), Mack (part of Volvo Group). *Five-firm market. DTNA is the market leader.*

The customer base — three tiers.

*Large national fleets.* UPS, FedEx, J.B. Hunt, Schneider, Werner, Walmart Transportation, the major LTL carriers, the major dedicated-contract carriers. Hundreds to thousands of trucks per fleet. Sophisticated purchasing organisations. *These customers buy in negotiated frame agreements with multi-year commitments.*

*Mid-size and regional fleets.* Hundreds of carriers in the 50-500 truck range. Significant aggregate volume. Tend to buy through dealer networks rather than direct from OEM. Detroit Connect telematics adoption is a competitive differentiator.

*Owner-operators and small fleets.* The "long tail." Single-truck owner-operators and small fleets up to 50 trucks. Buy through dealers. Highly price-sensitive. Loyalty to brand, dealer, and product family.

The dealer network is critical. *DTNA's Class 8 dealer network is one of the largest competitive moats in the business.* Service capability, parts availability, geographic coverage. The dealer story is where the aftermarket plays we'll cover in Episode 3 live.

### The three strategic themes

**MARCUS:** OK. The three themes that pressure the business and that any Account Team conversation needs to be situated within.

**KEVEN:** Walk them.

**MARCUS:** *Theme one — electrification.* DTNA has launched the eCascadia (Class 8 electric), the eM2 (medium-duty electric), and the eEconic (specialty vocational electric). The trucks are real, in customer hands, accumulating real-world data. The strategic question — *when does electrification cross over from "limited deployment in specific use cases" to "majority of new orders"?* That timing affects manufacturing capacity planning, supplier relationships, dealer training investment, and capital allocation.

The regulatory pressure is real — *California Air Resources Board Advanced Clean Trucks rule* and *EPA 2027 NOx standards* are pushing aggressive electrification targets. Fleet customers in California are under regulatory pressure to adopt zero-emission trucks. DTNA has to be ready to supply.

*Theme two — autonomy.* Daimler Truck Holding owns *Torc Robotics*, the Blacksburg, Virginia autonomy developer working on Level 4 highway trucking. Multi-year program, approaching production milestones. The strategic question — *when does Level 4 reach commercial deployment, and what's the business model* — driverless trucks on dedicated routes operating as freight-as-a-service, or autonomous-capable trucks sold to fleets with driver-monitored deployment?

The autonomy investment is enormous. The technology bet has been building for years. DTNA and Torc are coordinated; the engineering work is intensive. *APEX has a role in supporting both the engineering and the eventual operational deployment.*

*Theme three — the freight cycle.* Class 8 orders are *highly cyclical.* Up cycles (2018, 2021-2022) produce strained capacity, long order backlogs, premium pricing. Down cycles (2019-2020, 2024-2025) produce excess capacity, order cancellations, dealer inventory pressure. *The freight cycle drives everything downstream.* Manufacturing throughput planning, capital investment timing, dealer profitability, fleet customer purchasing power, even electrification adoption (which slows when freight margins are tight).

The Account Team has to understand where DTNA *currently is* in the cycle when planning any pursuit. *Different waves of the cycle motivate different agentic plays.*

### Why APEX *now* at DTNA

**KEVEN:** And the case for *now* — why is 2026 the moment for the Account Team to push agentic AI conversations at DTNA?

**MARCUS:** Three reasons aligned right now.

One — *the platform is mature.* Microsoft Fabric, Foundry, Agent Framework, Purview — all GA. The infrastructure to deploy governed agentic AI at DTNA's scale exists today.

Two — *DTNA's data foundation has been built.* Detroit Connect has been collecting telematics data for years. The manufacturing systems are digitised. The dealer-network systems are integrated. *The data exists; it's not been composed agentically yet.* APEX is the framework that composes.

Three — *the strategic transitions are the forcing function.* Electrification, autonomy, freight cycle — these aren't problems agentic AI alone solves, but agentic AI is the *acceleration framework* for the strategic decisions DTNA's leadership is making across all three. *APEX is a force multiplier* for the strategic agenda, not a parallel initiative.

The seller's frame — *"You have the data. You have the strategic agenda. APEX is how you compress the time between strategy and execution."* That sentence lands.

### Where the 16 scenarios from the AXLE Practice land at DTNA

**KEVEN:** And the framework's scenarios specifically. The AXLE Practice has roughly seven catalogued Services. Which ones map cleanest to DTNA?

**MARCUS:** Six of the seven map directly. Let me name them with the scenario codes the Account Team should know.

*AXLE-WRTY-01 — Warranty Traceability and Cost Avoidance.* The Zero Day Warranty story. Class 8 warranty cost is materially higher per unit than passenger-car warranty. Multi-million dollar claims are common. *This is the highest-fit play for DTNA.* Episode 2 walks it in depth.

*AXLE-ASSET-01 — Predictive Maintenance, Stamping Press OEE.* Equipment-level reliability on the manufacturing line. Mt. Holly, Cleveland, Saltillo, Redford all have applicable use cases. Episode 2.

*AXLE-QUAL-01 — Quality Escape Detection and Root Cause.* Quality events on the line correlated to build records and field-claim outcomes. Episode 2.

*AXLE-CONNVEH-02 — Connected Vehicle Diagnostic Acceleration.* Detroit Connect is the telematics platform; diagnostic agent assistance for dealers and fleet customers is the AXLE-CONNVEH play. Episode 3.

*AXLE-DEALER-03 — Dealer Network Performance Analytics.* DTNA's dealer network is large and diverse. Dealer performance optimisation is the AXLE-DEALER play. Episode 3.

*ICE-AFTERMKT-05 — Predictive Parts Demand.* While ICE Practice technically, the pattern applies to DTNA's massive parts and service aftermarket. Episode 3.

Six of seven. The seventh — generative-engineering-as-the-wedge — is more relevant in passenger-car design contexts than in commercial-truck design where the engineering cycles look different. *It's not zero-relevance — DTNA does invest in design tools — but it's not the lead Wave 1 conversation.*

### One disagreement

**KEVEN:** Pushback time.

**MARCUS:** OK. Let me push on the *primacy of Zero Day Warranty* as the lead Wave 1 play at DTNA.

**KEVEN:** Go.

**MARCUS:** Conventional wisdom — and the framework's reference scenario — points to AXLE-WRTY-01 as the flagship play and the natural Wave 1 entry. *I want to argue that for DTNA specifically, the right Wave 1 entry might be Connected Vehicle Diagnostic Acceleration — AXLE-CONNVEH-02.*

**KEVEN:** Why?

**MARCUS:** Three reasons.

One — *Detroit Connect has more mature data foundations than the warranty data estate.* Telematics has been digital-first since launch. Warranty data is improving but still has legacy claim-processing dependencies. The first APEX Service at DTNA goes faster on cleaner data.

Two — *the Connected Vehicle play touches both fleet customers and dealers.* It's a customer-facing play, not an internal-back-office play. *Customer-facing wins build relationship momentum* faster than back-office wins.

Three — *the eCascadia adoption is driving renewed Detroit Connect investment.* The strategic frame is naturally aligned. DTNA leadership is already investing in Detroit Connect; agentic AI augmentation slots into existing momentum rather than creating a new initiative from scratch.

**KEVEN:** And the counter —

**MARCUS:** The counter is — Zero Day Warranty has the *biggest dollar number* attached to it. Warranty costs in commercial trucking are measured in hundreds of millions per OEM per year. The CFO conversation is more compelling. *If you can get the CFO into Wave 1, the dollars are bigger.*

So the right answer depends on *which buyer the Account Team has the strongest current relationship with.* CFO-relationship strength → lead with Warranty. CTO or VP Connected Vehicle relationship strength → lead with Connected Diagnostic. *Match the play to the relationship.*

**KEVEN:** Good frame. *Match the play to the relationship.* That's the disciplined version.
