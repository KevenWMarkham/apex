## 2. The conversational design rules (inherited from Services v2 + Deployment + Sellers Eps 8-10)

All rules from the Trilogy podcasts' conversational style apply here. Briefly:

- **No chapter or section number citations in dialogue.** Hosts know the framework.
- **Historical and industry context opens each episode** — 3-5 minutes setting up Disney's own history with the segment under discussion.
- **One concept developed fully (4-6 min) before transitioning.** No topic-switching.
- **Real disagreement between hosts** in every episode — Keven and Riley have different priors.
- **One quote-and-react moment** per episode from primary sources (Disney earnings calls, industry analyses, etc.) or from the APEX framework documents.
- **No announced segments.** No "OK, time for APEX Facts." Rhythms emerge organically.
- **`Further Reading` section** in each .md — Microsoft Learn, industry analyses, Disney public materials where appropriate.

## 3. Disney-specific style additions

### Rule 1 · Treat Disney as a specific company with a specific history

Disney is not "a generic media client." Disney has 100+ years of history, a specific corporate-segment structure, a specific competitive landscape, and a specific recent strategic posture (Iger return, segment reorganisation, streaming-profitability focus, $60B Parks investment). Episode 1 sets this up. Later episodes reference it specifically.

When hosts discuss Disney scenarios, they should sound like *people who know Disney as a company* — not like consultants reading a deck.

### Rule 2 · Name the segments correctly

Disney reorganised into three segments in 2023:
- **Entertainment** — Studios (Walt Disney Studios, Pixar, Marvel, Lucasfilm, 20th Century, Searchlight), Networks (ABC, FX, Nat Geo), Direct-to-Consumer (Disney+, Hulu)
- **Sports** — ESPN linear, ESPN+, ESPN-direct-to-consumer (forthcoming)
- **Experiences** — Parks (Disneyland, Walt Disney World, Disneyland Paris, Tokyo, Hong Kong, Shanghai), Cruise Line, Consumer Products, Disney Vacation Club

Use these segment names. Use the brand names within them. Avoid generic "their streaming business" when specifically discussing Disney+, Hulu, or ESPN+.

### Rule 3 · Specific named pressure points

The Disney conversations the Account Team will have are about *specific* business pressures. The series names them:

- **Streaming profitability** — Disney+ has historically lost money. Iger's commitment is to consolidated DTC profitability. The pressure is real and ongoing.
- **Linear TV decline** — ABC, FX, ESPN linear all face cord-cutting. The transition to DTC is structural.
- **Content cost inflation** — Marvel, Star Wars, and Pixar content production costs have escalated. ROI per title is under scrutiny.
- **Parks operational complexity at scale** — six parks worldwide, 100,000+ employees in Experiences. Operational efficiency matters.
- **Password sharing** — Netflix demonstrated paid-sharing economics. Disney is implementing.
- **Successor planning** — Iger's eventual successor matters strategically.
- **The 2024 proxy fight (Trian)** — public history; relevant context for governance discussions.

Hosts reference these naturally as part of conversation, not as a list.

### Rule 4 · Account-team-specific Independence

The Independence posture for Disney specifically:

- Deloitte serves Disney across multiple service lines. The Account Team understands the audit-vs-advisory boundary at this account specifically.
- Recommendations of Microsoft platforms are on the merits — APEX is delivered as services, no Microsoft compensation flows back to Deloitte.
- Disney's procurement is sophisticated and will probe Independence. Sellers must be precise.
- Episode 5 names specific Independence considerations for this account.

### Rule 5 · Names without false specificity

We can name Disney publicly. We *cannot* invent specific names of individuals, internal projects, or non-public financial data. When discussing roles, use *titles* and segments — not names.

✅ "The Chief Operating Officer of Experiences"
✅ "The President of Disney+"
✅ "The Chief Technology Officer at Disney"
❌ A made-up name for the CTO

When citing financials, use *publicly disclosed* numbers from earnings calls or annual reports. Cite the source where appropriate.
