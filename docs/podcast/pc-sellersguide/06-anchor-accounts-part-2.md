## The Story

### Chapter 23 — the global media and entertainment conglomerate (TMT-MED)

**KEVEN:** Chapter Twenty-Three. *A global media and entertainment conglomerate.* Theme parks, studios, streaming, broadcast, consumer products. The business is structurally unique — there is *no* generic playbook for a media conglomerate of this scale. The TMT-MED Practice in APEX is the most account-specific Practice in the guide.

**APEX thesis** — section twenty-three point three — *content rights and royalty reconciliation, plus park-and-experience operations as a second front.* Two fronts because the business is two businesses. The rights-and-royalty front is the higher-Purview, higher-margin path. The parks front is the higher-volume, more visible path. Pick one.

**Microsoft pillar lead** — section twenty-three point four — *Purview-first for rights-and-royalty. Foundry-first for parks and experience.* Different entry per front.

**The opening demo** — section twenty-three point eight — *content rights conflict detection.* The agent reads through a proposed content reuse, identifies all rights-holders affected, surfaces conflicts and required clearances. Or — for parks — *guest-experience anomaly detection at scale.*

**Commercial envelope** — section twenty-three point nine — Wave One typically 3.5 to 5 million dollars Deloitte services. This is a multi-Wave relationship from day one.

**JORDAN:** Observation — *this account values craft.* The buyer in media is unusually attuned to *how things are presented.* Your decks have to be beautiful. Your demos have to be precise. Bring craft.

### Chapter 24 — the Tier-1 North American telecommunications operator (TMT-TEL)

**KEVEN:** Chapter Twenty-Four. *A Tier-1 North American telecommunications operator.* This is a long-time Deloitte and Microsoft relationship and the buyer profile is well-known.

**APEX thesis** — section twenty-four point three — *contact-center transformation as Wave One, network-operations augmentation as Wave Two.* The order matters. Contact-center is the visible value. Network ops is the structural value. You don't lead with network ops because it's an architectural conversation. You lead with contact-center because it's a *customer-pain* conversation.

**Microsoft pillar lead** — section twenty-four point four — *Copilot for contact-center transformation. Foundry plus Fabric for network ops.* Two different leads for two different Waves.

**The opening demo** — section twenty-four point eight — *the agent-assisted contact-center call.* The agent listens to the customer call in real time, surfaces account context, recommends next actions to the human agent, and emits the audit record automatically. This is one of the most directly *demonstrable* APEX scenarios.

**Commercial envelope** — section twenty-four point nine — Wave One typically 4 to 6 million dollars services. Telco contact-center transformations are large.

**JORDAN:** Observation — *the buyer here knows the technology better than the seller usually does.* Don't oversell. Be technical. Be precise.

### Chapter 25 — the global enterprise infrastructure technology firm (TMT-TEC)

**KEVEN:** Chapter Twenty-Five. *A global enterprise infrastructure technology firm.* Hardware, hybrid cloud, AI infrastructure. The fun thing about this account is — *they sell AI infrastructure to other people for a living.* So the bar for an AI proposal is unusually high.

**APEX thesis** — section twenty-five point three — *internal productivity-and-engineering acceleration first.* This account is most interested in APEX for *their own* engineering workforce — code generation, design assist, technical-marketing acceleration. They want to be a reference customer for what they sell.

**Microsoft pillar lead** — section twenty-five point four — *Foundry plus Copilot.* Heavy Copilot Studio. Heavy developer-productivity tooling.

**The opening demo** — section twenty-five point eight — *engineering velocity dashboard.* Show how an agent accelerates code review, technical writing, and customer-facing technical content. The buyer cares about their engineers shipping faster.

**Commercial envelope** — section twenty-five point nine — Wave One typically 2.5 to 4 million dollars services. Smaller envelope, faster Wave Two follow-on.

**JORDAN:** Observation — *this is a reference-deal account.* You're not just selling them APEX. You're co-developing a story they can tell to their customers. Plan the joint case study.

### Chapter 26 — the North American legacy carrier, hub-and-spoke (TH)

**KEVEN:** Chapter Twenty-Six. *A North American legacy carrier — hub-and-spoke operations model.* This is one of the two airline anchors and the operations profile is hub-and-spoke. Which matters because the cascade dynamics of an irregular-operations event are different at a hub-and-spoke carrier than at a point-to-point.

**APEX thesis** — section twenty-six point three — *IROPs recovery orchestration as Wave One.* When weather or mechanical disruption hits a hub, the cascade is catastrophic. The agent re-plans the rebooking, the crew, the equipment, the customer comms — all in minutes.

**Microsoft pillar lead** — section twenty-six point four — *Foundry.* Heavy agent orchestration. Fabric matters but it's not the lead.

**The opening demo** — section twenty-six point eight — *A Day in IROPs.* The Sellers Guide reference deployment we mentioned in Episode Four. A thunderstorm closes the hub. The agent has eight minutes to rebook 12,000 affected passengers. The narrative is pacy and the room leans in. This is one of the best demos in APEX.

**Commercial envelope** — section twenty-six point nine — Wave One typically 3 to 4 million dollars services. Airline IT environments are complex; integration is non-trivial.

**JORDAN:** Observation — *airlines decide fast when they decide.* Their procurement is sophisticated, their timing is brutal. If you get past discovery and into a paid pilot, the speed picks up.

### Chapter 27 — the North American legacy carrier, mileage-program differentiated (TH)

**KEVEN:** Chapter Twenty-Seven. *A North American legacy carrier — mileage-program differentiated.* Second airline anchor. The differentiator here is the customer-loyalty franchise. The airline's mileage program is a separate business unit, several billion dollars of revenue.

**APEX thesis** — section twenty-seven point three — *customer-loyalty-and-experience-driven IROPs.* Same IROPs recovery problem, but with a different priority — preserving high-value loyalty-member experience first.

**Microsoft pillar lead** — section twenty-seven point four — *Foundry plus Copilot.* The Copilot integration matters because the loyalty-program agents need to be in the hands of customer-service representatives, not just operations dispatchers.

**The opening demo** — section twenty-seven point eight — *high-value-member IROPs experience.* The agent identifies all top-tier loyalty members affected by a disruption, generates personalised rebooking offers, drafts the proactive communication for each member — all before the customer even calls.

**Commercial envelope** — section twenty-seven point nine — Wave One typically 3 to 4.5 million dollars services. Loyalty-program integration adds scope.

**JORDAN:** Observation — *the same Practice, the same flagship Service, but a different angle of attack.* The two airline anchors look identical from outside; the buyer's framing is different. Match the framing.

### Chapter 28 — the corporate relocation and mobility services provider (TH-adjacent)

**KEVEN:** Chapter Twenty-Eight. The last anchor account. *A corporate relocation and mobility services provider.* TH-adjacent because it's travel and mobility, services-adjacent because it's a B2B services business — not a B2C consumer brand. Smaller in pure dollar size than the other anchors but strategically interesting because it's the gateway to the broader Deloitte mobility-services portfolio.

**APEX thesis** — section twenty-eight point three — *traveller-experience and assignment-management orchestration.* Corporate relocation is a high-touch, high-stakes service. Agents help manage the dozens of vendor handoffs, regulatory checks, and traveller comms.

**Microsoft pillar lead** — section twenty-eight point four — *Copilot, with Fabric for the unified traveller profile.* Front-door is the relocation consultant's workflow.

**The opening demo** — section twenty-eight point eight — *traveller-assignment status orchestration.* The agent watches all open relocations, surfaces the ones at risk, recommends interventions, drafts the traveller and corporate-client communication.

**Commercial envelope** — section twenty-eight point nine — Wave One typically 1.5 to 2.5 million dollars services. Smaller than the other anchors but with strategic significance for the wider mobility-services portfolio.

**JORDAN:** Observation — *the smallest anchor account is the most over-looked.* It's the one where a single Wave One win produces a disproportionate effect on the Deloitte mobility-services book. Don't dismiss it for size.

### The thirteen-account picture

**JORDAN:** OK. Across Episode Five and Six we've covered all thirteen anchor accounts. What does the *whole* portfolio tell us?

**KEVEN:** Three things.

One — **the Practice mix.** Five RC-adjacent, three AXLE-or-ICE, three TMT, three TH-adjacent (two airlines plus the mobility provider). Two HLS-adjacent (the conglomerate touches HLS through pharma and medical devices). The mix tells you where Microsoft and Deloitte are betting jointly — and it skews toward RC and TMT.

Two — **the Microsoft pillar lead mix.** Roughly half lead with Fabric. About a third lead with Foundry. The rest lead with Copilot or Purview. Notable: *no anchor account leads with all four pillars.* The Sellers Guide is disciplined about this — *lead with one, compose with all four.*

Three — **the Wave One commercial envelope range.** Smallest is the mobility provider — 1.5 to 2.5 million. Largest is the OEM and the telco — 4 to 6 million plus. Average is about 3.5 million. The aggregate Wave One commercial opportunity across the thirteen accounts is in the 45 to 55 million dollar range *just for Deloitte services* — not counting Microsoft consumption or Wave Two and Wave Three expansion.

## Adopt / Hold

**KEVEN:** Adopt versus Hold on the TMT-and-TH portfolio specifically. Jordan, Adopt.

**JORDAN:** Adopt — **invest in the airline accounts now.** Both airline anchors plus the mobility-provider anchor together form a coherent TH-services franchise inside Deloitte. The IROPs demo travels across all three. The traveller-profile canonical schema works for all three. A seller who invests in TH this year has three anchor accounts to draw from. That's significant.

**KEVEN:** Hold. When is the TMT-and-TH portfolio *not* the right focus?

**JORDAN:** Two cases.

Case one — *if you live outside the geographic concentration.* The TMT and TH anchors skew toward specific U.S. metro areas — Manhattan, DFW, Chicago, LA. A seller in Atlanta or Seattle has different geographic logic.

Case two — *if your firm's delivery capacity is already booked on RC and AXLE.* The constraint isn't pipeline. It's *deliverable pipeline.* Don't open a TMT pursuit if you can't staff Wave One.

**KEVEN:** Synthesis?

**JORDAN:** Geography and capacity gate the pursuit selection. Within those gates, the TMT-and-TH portfolio is a coherent franchise. Build to it.

## Lessons

**KEVEN:** Monday-morning lessons.

One — **map the thirteen anchor accounts against the seller's actual book.** Which anchor is closest to one of your current accounts? Which sub-segment in the anchor chapter matches your buyer profile? Read that chapter.

Two — **practice the IROPs demo before the airline meeting.** Section twenty-six point eight or twenty-seven point eight. Rehearse the pacing. The thunderstorm-rebooking narrative is one of the strongest pieces of audio in this entire Practice — *if you deliver it well.* Practise.

Three — **for the media-conglomerate-style account — bring craft.** Beautiful slides. Precise demos. The buyer in media reads the *presentation* as a proxy for *delivery quality.*

Four — **for the telco-style account — be technical.** They will not respect imprecision. Memorise the Foundry-Copilot composition.

Five — **for the smaller TH-services anchor — don't dismiss it.** The dollar size is misleading. The strategic leverage on the broader mobility book is substantial.
