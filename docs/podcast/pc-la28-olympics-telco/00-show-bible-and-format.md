# Show Bible · The LA28 Telco-Agentic Anchor Podcast

Internal Deloitte sellers and pursuit content for the DMTSP Account Teams running the 24-month build-to-LA28 motion: Denver kickoff in June 2026, through the LA28 Closing Ceremony in July 2028, into Wave-2 syndication in late 2028. The design rules below add to (don't replace) the Trilogy, Disney, and DTNA show bibles.

---

## 1. Series identity

**Title.** *The LA28 Telco-Agentic Anchor Podcast.*

**One-line pitch.** Nine episodes, one milestone each, that walk a TMT-led pursuit team through the 24 months between Denver kickoff and the Closing Ceremony — using AT&T as the Wave-1 Telco anchor, LA28 as the Summer Olympics anchor, and the Microsoft Azure platform as the technology spine.

**Audience.** DMTSP Account Teams plus the cross-Practice leads who get pulled into this motion: TMT (lead), TH, RC, AXLE, ER, HLS, ICE, FSI, GPS, with Risk Advisory riding the overlay. Internal sellers and pursuit voices only — this is not customer-facing content. Hosts speak the way pursuit leads speak to other pursuit leads in a hallway: candid, specific, naming titles and roles.

## 3. The 9-milestone arc

Each episode is one milestone from the `APEX-Agentic-Telco-Olympics-Tracker` HTML. The arc is the show; the milestones are the chapters.

- **01 · The pursuit.** DMTSP Denver kickoff. *M0, M-25 → M-22, Denver 10 Jun 2026.* The pursuit-team charter is signed; the 24-month roadmap-as-instrument starts ticking.
- **02 · The anchor.** AT&T and LA28 commit. *M1, M-24 → M-22.* The Wave-1 anchor and the Summer Olympics anchor lock; the endorsement chain forms.
- **03 · The default channel.** Home goes live in shadow. *M2, M-21 → M-15.* The Home Channel ships in shadow mode behind the AT&T NOC; the first Adaptive Cards land on operator screens.
- **04 · The first plug-ins.** Travel + Retail + Media signed. *M3, M-15 → M-12.* American Airlines, Marriott, Expedia, Walmart, NBCUniversal — the first three plug-in Channels commit.
- **05 · The second wave.** Mobility + Auto + CPG. *M4, M-12 → M-9.* Uber, Hertz, Toyota Connected, AutoNation, Sazerac, CVS — the second wave of plug-in Channels lands.
- **06 · The dress rehearsal.** Super Bowl LXII. *M5, M-6 → M-5, February 2028.* The Super Bowl is the production load test for LA28; what breaks here gets fixed before July.
- **07 · The home stretch.** California + venue + transit. *M6, M-4 → M-1.* The CA Governor's office, the LA Mayor's office, venue operators, and transit authorities come on-stream.
- **08 · Sixteen days.** LA28 Games live. *M7, July 14 → July 30, 2028.* The Games themselves. Sixteen days of live operations, with the Closing Ceremony as the final cue.
- **09 · The wrap.** Case study and Wave-2 syndication. *M8, post-Games.* The case study lands; Wave-2 syndication begins late 2028 — the next Telco anchor, the next sporting anchor, the next Microsoft Azure platform engagement.

## 5. Episode shape

Every episode follows this skeleton. Hosts do not announce the segments; the listener feels the rhythm because the rhythm is consistent.

```
# Episode NN · <Title>
Builds-on: <prior milestone hand-offs>

## Cold Open
<300–400 word vignette, one moment in the pursuit>

## The conversation
### <sub-section 1>
### <sub-section 2>
### <sub-section 3>
### <sub-section 4>
### <sub-section 5>
(5–7 sub-sections total)

### A reading I want to do
### One disagreement
### The quote-and-react
### What to carry forward
1. <seller-actionable>
2. <seller-actionable>
3. <seller-actionable, optional — minimum 2, maximum 3>

## Further reading
```

The four post-conversation `###` headings (*A reading I want to do*, *One disagreement*, *The quote-and-react*, *What to carry forward*) sit as peers inside the `## The conversation` H2 block — they are not a separate H2 section. The conversation closes when *What to carry forward* closes; `## Further reading` is the only H2 that follows.

## 6. Cold-open style

The cold open is always a moment-in-the-pursuit vignette, 300 to 400 words, written cinematically. It compresses the milestone's stakes into one concrete moment before any framework term enters the conversation.

Example seeds, one per arc-band, to anchor what "a moment" looks like:

- A pursuit-team-lead's inbox at 6:47 in the morning the week of Denver kickoff, three emails deep, the Practice Principal cc'd on all three.
- An AT&T NOC engineer on a Wednesday night at 11:47pm watching the first Home-Channel Adaptive Card surface on the operator console next to her.
- An LA28 operations desk on Super Bowl Friday — the dress-rehearsal cue sheet on one screen, the production playbook on the other.
- A Super Bowl-weekend pilot household where the Home Channel quietly mediates the doorbell, the streaming app, and the rideshare arrival, and nobody in the room notices because nothing breaks.
- A Game-day Adaptive Card landing on the AT&T CEO's screen at 8am Pacific on Day Three of the Games.
- A Casey Wasserman BD call on a Tuesday afternoon, the LA Mayor's office staffer on speaker, the TMT VP taking notes.

The cold open never opens with a framework term, never with a section number, and never with the host saying "today we're going to talk about." It opens with a moment, and the conversation joins it.

## 7. Content discipline

These are non-negotiable. They are the carry-forward from the CFMP re-tone work and the DTNA Rule 1.

**Independence language.** The canonical phrase for the Microsoft relationship is *Microsoft platform engagement*. Hosts use it every time. The four legacy phrases that imply commercial bundling between Deloitte and Microsoft — the ones that suggest joint selling, a formal vendor relationship, a named programme tie, or a designated reseller posture — are off the table. Hosts do not use them, do not paraphrase around them, do not flirt with them. The underlying reason is simple and Rashmi names it once in episode one: **Deloitte audits Microsoft.** The auditor-independent posture is the reason the language has to be clean.

**Microsoft commercial instruments — off-tape.** Hosts do not name, describe, or reference Microsoft's funding programs or consumption-commitment vehicles anywhere on tape. Not the funded-engagement program. Not the consumption-commitment vehicle. Not the partner-investment vehicle. No framing that implies Deloitte and Microsoft are running a coordinated commercial play. Those instruments are Microsoft's to handle directly with the client; they are not Deloitte's to describe. The instruments exist in the world; they do not exist in this podcast. *Microsoft platform engagement* is the only language Deloitte uses for the platform relationship, and the platform is recommended on the merits — full stop. The auditor-independent posture requires that the Deloitte page stays clean of Microsoft commercial-instrument language.

**LA28 and IOC brand discipline.** The TOP-program scope is a separate conversation from platform-vendor positioning — hosts respect that line and do not blur it. NBCUniversal has exclusive U.S. broadcast rights for the Games; the Media Channel is ride-along orchestration (watch-party suggestions, Peacock cross-promo with disclosure, second-screen Adaptive Cards) and never competitive distribution. Olympic trademarks — *LA28*, *the Games*, *Opening Ceremony*, *Closing Ceremony*, *the Olympic Rings* — are named with care and never used decoratively.

**Anchor-partner naming.** Real entities are named as themselves: AT&T, LA28, NBCUniversal, Walmart, American Airlines, Marriott, Expedia, Airbnb, Hertz, Uber, OpenTable, Viator, Toyota Connected, AutoNation, Progressive, Sazerac, CVS, the Casey Wasserman BD organisation, the LA Mayor's office, the CA Governor's office.

**Internal C-suite by title and role — DTNA Rule 1 carries forward.** The TMT VP. The M&P Leader. The AT&T CCO and CTO. The AT&T CEO. The Account SE. The Delivery Architect. The Practice Principal. The GPS Account Lead. The AXLE VP. The Risk Advisory Partner. Never *Sarah*, never *Mike*, never an invented first name.

**Narrative tone.** Every sub-section opens with a concrete pursuit moment before any framework term enters. Technical vocabulary tucks into asides. Every sub-section closes on what the seller does Monday morning.

**No section or chapter number citations on tape.** Hosts know the framework. They do not cite themselves.

## 9. Recurring elements

Four recurring beats land in every episode, none announced. The first three live as peer `###` headings inside `## The conversation` after the numbered sub-sections close; the fourth — *The quote-and-react* — is its own `###` heading in the same peer position.

**A reading I want to do.** One to two paragraphs. Rashmi or Keven recommends a primary-source reading: an AT&T earnings transcript, an NBCUniversal Olympics commitments statement, an LA28 organising-committee public release, a Microsoft Azure architecture-center reference pattern, a Walmart investor-day deck, a Toyota Connected developer post.

**One disagreement.** A real tension between Rashmi and Keven, named. Sometimes it converges by the end of the episode and sometimes it doesn't. When it doesn't, the show ends with the tension still open — that is the honest record of how pursuit teams actually work.

**The quote-and-react.** One per episode, drawn from a primary source. Hosts read the quote, then react. The source is named; the host says where the quote came from before reading it. Lives as its own `### The quote-and-react` heading, ordered between *One disagreement* and *What to carry forward*.

**What to carry forward.** Two or three numbered items, every one of them something a seller can do this week. Not aspirations — actions. Two is the minimum; three is the cap.

## 11. Pacing rules (carried from the DTNA sibling show bible)

- No chapter or section number citations on tape.
- Three to five minutes of historical or industry context to open each episode.
- One concept developed fully — four to six minutes — before any transition.
- One real disagreement per episode, named.
- No announced segments.
- A `Further reading` section in each `.md`.

---

## 12. Pre / During / Post / Handoff within each episode

Every episode internally follows a milestone-arc structure that hosts use implicitly and never announce.

- **Pre — the milestone runway.** What the prior milestone set up. The hand-offs the pursuit team received as they entered this milestone.
- **During — the moment.** What *this* milestone delivers. The signatures, the production cut-overs, the anchor commits, the Adaptive Cards landing.
- **Post — the handoff.** What unlocks the next milestone. What the pursuit team must close before the runway lights flip for the next chapter.

Hosts do not say "now we'll talk about the handoff." The handoff is in the rhythm of the conversation.

---

## 13. Length target

**Spoken-word target per episode: 5,200 to 6,500 words.** Verification band: 4,800 to 7,500. Spoken-only count excludes `[stage directions]`, headings, and speaker tags. Nine episodes at this band yields approximately fifty thousand spoken words across the series — roughly the length of a short book, broken into nine sittings, walked milestone by milestone.
