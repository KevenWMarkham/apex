## The conversation

### The four-level ladder at DTNA specifically

**MARCUS:** Set up the architecture.

**KEVEN:** Episodes 1-3 set the strategic context, the manufacturing-quality plays, and the connected-vehicle plays. Episode 4 takes the Sellers Podcast's *four-level ladder* — Power BI heatmap, then Wave 1 agentic services, then B2B practice-wide CoE, then customer transformation — and walks it *at DTNA specifically.*

The ladder climbs *per brand and per business segment* at DTNA. Freightliner ladder. Western Star ladder. Thomas Built ladder. Detroit ladder. *Each ladder climbs independently.* Plus the cross-segment plays (Connected Vehicle, Dealer Network) that span multiple brands.

The Account Team can be at L1 in one segment and L3 in another *simultaneously.* That's normal at a multi-brand industrial OEM.

### L1 for DTNA — the Power BI Opportunity Heatmap

**KEVEN:** L1. The wedge.

**MARCUS:** Adapted for DTNA — *eight to ten days to build*, not five. We covered the timing discipline in the Disney podcast — Disney has data-sharing-agreement work; DTNA has *plant-floor-data-access* work and *connected-vehicle-data-access* work. Both require structured procurement engagement before any data flows to the heatmap.

What's in the heatmap. *Rows are DTNA's product portfolio dimensions* — by brand (Freightliner, Western Star, Thomas Built, Detroit), by plant (Mt. Holly, Cleveland NC, Portland engineering, Redford, Saltillo, Gastonia), by dealer region (East, Central, West), by fleet-customer tier (large national fleets, mid-size, owner-operators). *Columns are the AXLE scenarios* — the 6+ plays from Episodes 2 and 3.

Each cell is scored on the three dimensions — *opportunity size, Microsoft attach, Wave 1 readiness.* The leader looking at the heatmap sees, on a single page, *where the most-valuable Wave 1 candidates are in their portfolio.*

The dollar value at L1 — *25-75 thousand dollars services revenue.* Wedge revenue, not the point.

The conversation the heatmap unlocks — *"want to act on the top three?"*

**KEVEN:** Who's the L1 buyer at DTNA —

**MARCUS:** Typically *a senior person within a segment with discretionary access to a small services budget.* The VP of Manufacturing Engineering for Freightliner. The President of Detroit. The VP of Connected Vehicle. Sometimes a chief of staff to one of the senior executives. *Rarely the CEO or the segment president.* Those buyers come at L2 and L3.

*Each segment has one or two L1 buyers.* The Account Team's job is to identify them.

### L2 for DTNA — the first Wave 1 SOW

**KEVEN:** L2.

**MARCUS:** At DTNA specifically — *the right Wave 1 SOW is almost certainly one of three plays.* From the disagreements in Episodes 1 and 2 —

Option A — *Zero Day Warranty Traceability (AXLE-WRTY-01).* The flagship. CFO conversation. Highest dollar visibility.

Option B — *Connected Vehicle Diagnostic Acceleration (AXLE-CONNVEH-02).* Customer-facing. Strategic-momentum alignment. Cleanest data foundation.

Option C — *Quality Escape Detection (AXLE-QUAL-01).* Quality executive conversation. Reduces downstream warranty feed; complementary to Option A.

The Account Team picks *one* based on which buyer relationship is strongest. *Match the play to the relationship.*

The dollar value at L2 — *one to two million dollars* services revenue. Plus DTNA's Microsoft consumption — Fabric, Foundry, Copilot, Purview, Entra. Two-contract model.

The bundle shape — *Discover, Design, Build, Deploy. Ninety days.* BVA-backed. KPI envelope defined upfront with the buyer's organisation.

**KEVEN:** And the discipline at L2 —

**MARCUS:** Three disciplines, all from the Sellers Handbook applied at DTNA.

One — *Wave 1 BVA must be locked before SOW signing.* KPI envelope is part of the SOW.

Two — *the GSSC Lab demo precedes the SOW.* DTNA leadership won't commit without seeing a working AXLE-pattern agent in the Lab.

Three — *the Daimler Truck global Independence structure must be coordinated before the SOW signs.* This is *DTNA-specific.* Episode 5 covers it in detail.

### L3 for DTNA — AXLE Practice CoE inside DTNA

**KEVEN:** L3. The expansion.

**MARCUS:** L3 is the move from one Wave 1 Service to *a fleet of Services running across DTNA.* The framework's structural play here is — *establish an AXLE Practice Agentic Center of Excellence inside DTNA's engineering organisation.* Not just delivering more individual Services. *Establishing the operating model* through which DTNA runs the agentic-Service portfolio across the segments.

Dollar value at L3 — *five to twenty million dollars* services revenue annually. Expansion revenue. Same client, growing footprint, predictable cadence.

What's in the CoE by L3. *DTNA is operating typically four to six Services in production* — possibly Zero Day Warranty, Connected Diagnostic, Dealer Performance, Quality Escape, and one or two of the operations plays. *Plus the platform-and-governance services.* DTNA has *internal capability* operating these, with Deloitte as advisor, deployer for new Services, and operate-partner.

The buyer at L3 — *the steering committee.* CTO. CIO. COO. CFO. President of one or more segments. The decision-making body for L3 is multi-person, multi-quarter.

**KEVEN:** And the cross-segment story at L3 —

**MARCUS:** *Cross-segment becomes the strategic narrative.* By L3, DTNA has Services running in Freightliner, in Detroit, in Western Star, in Thomas Built. The cross-segment data composition — *VINs joining across brands*, *dealers serving multiple brands*, *fleet customers buying across brands* — becomes a unique DTNA asset. *The compounding-asset thesis from the framework lands enormously well at this point.*

### L4 for DTNA — Fleet Customer Transformation

**KEVEN:** L4. The transformation tier.

**MARCUS:** *The L4 framing at DTNA is different from the L4 framing at Disney.* At Disney, L4 was direct-to-consumer transformation across streaming brands. *At DTNA, L4 is fleet-customer-transformation* — the *commercial customer-relationship* equivalent of B2C.

What it means concretely. *The major fleet customers — UPS, FedEx, J.B. Hunt, Schneider, Walmart Transportation — become deeply integrated with DTNA's agentic Services.* The fleet manager's daily workflow includes DTNA-agent-driven insights. The fleet's maintenance planning is informed by DTNA's predictive-maintenance agents. *DTNA becomes the operational-intelligence platform for the fleet*, not just a truck supplier.

That's *Detroit Connect plus agentic Services* delivered to the customer at fleet-scale. *Multi-year, multi-million-dollar engagement per major fleet customer.*

The dollar value at L4 — *twenty to fifty million dollars and up* services revenue annually at scale. *Strategic/displacement revenue.* The competitor isn't another consulting firm — *the competitor is the fleet customer doing it themselves or with a non-OEM technology vendor.*

The buyer at L4 — *the CEO of DTNA and the board.* Possibly with sponsorship from Daimler Truck Holding parent leadership. The investment is strategic. The timeline is multi-year. The competitive frame is — *which OEM's agentic platform are the major fleets going to standardise on?*

**KEVEN:** And the architectural shape at L4 —

**MARCUS:** All six Microsoft Growth Platforms densely composed at scale. Fabric Real-Time Intelligence operating at fleet scale. Foundry Agent Service at high availability. M365 Copilot in fleet-manager and dispatcher hands. Dynamics 365 — possibly — as the fleet-customer-relationship surface. Purview enforcing governance with multi-tenant boundary discipline (DTNA's data + the fleet customer's data, separately governed). Entra workload identities at scale across multiple-fleet-customer engagements.

*And — critically — the cross-segment data composition.* DTNA's Freightliner, Western Star, Thomas Built, Detroit data combined with the fleet customer's operational data. *L4 is where DTNA's structural cross-segment advantage becomes a customer-facing asset.*

**KEVEN:** Let me Disney-style-tailor the four unlock sentences for DTNA.

**MARCUS:** L0 to L1 — *"I'll build your segment a Power BI Opportunity Heatmap mapping the AXLE Practice scenarios across your brand portfolio. Eight to ten days. Free. You own the .pbix."*

L1 to L2 — *"You've seen the heatmap. The top three Wave 1 candidates for your segment are X, Y, Z. Want to act on number one?"*

Four sentences. The Account Team should know all four. *Use them at the right transition.*

### The strategic frames — electrification and autonomy

**MARCUS:** And the strategic frames that thread through the ladder.

**KEVEN:** Two themes from Episode 1 — *electrification* and *autonomy* — show up at every rung of the ladder differently.

*At L1 — heatmap inclusion.* Electrification-related scenarios (battery-quality traceability for eCascadia, charging-infrastructure optimisation, range-prediction) appear in the heatmap as overlay cells. Autonomy-related scenarios (Torc-engineering-acceleration, sensor-validation-pipelines) appear as Wave 3+ strategic frames in the heatmap commentary, *not* as Wave 1 candidate cells.

*At L2 — Wave 1 scope includes electrification-aware components where applicable.* The Zero Day Warranty agent, deployed for the Cascadia, *also* serves the eCascadia warranty-traceability story. Same architecture; the agent extends to the electric variant. *Electrification doesn't require a separate Wave 1; it extends the conventional Wave 1.*

*At L3 — the AXLE CoE explicitly covers both ICE and electric powertrains.* The CoE is the operational substrate for *DTNA's powertrain transition.* The framework supports both conventional warranty traceability and electric-vehicle-specific warranty traceability (battery, charging-related, eAxle-related) under the same canonical foundation.

*At L4 — fleet-customer transformation is largely about electrification readiness.* Helping the major fleets transition their operations to electric trucks. *DTNA's L4 agentic services become the fleet-customer's transition-management capability.* That's where the strategic value compounds.

**MARCUS:** And the autonomy frame —

**KEVEN:** Autonomy is *primarily a Wave 3+ frame* with one specific Wave-2 connection. The connection — *Torc Robotics's engineering organisation can be an APEX customer.* Torc is a separate subsidiary of Daimler Truck Holding, but a related-party entity that benefits from the same architectural patterns. *The autonomy-engineering acceleration play* (sensor validation, simulation infrastructure, ADAS data composition) is a credible Wave 2/3 conversation for DTNA-via-Torc.

Account Team should be aware. *Don't propose autonomy plays at Wave 1 to DTNA leadership.* Position autonomy as the strategic-future-state frame at the CEO and Board level, and as a specific Wave 2-3 play through the Torc relationship.

### The GSSC Lab for DTNA demonstrations

**MARCUS:** And the GSSC Lab.

**KEVEN:** Covered architecturally in Episodes 1 and 5 of the Sellers Podcast. *For DTNA specifically*, the Account Team has access to AXLE Practice scenarios pre-loaded in the Lab. The 6+ plays from this podcast can be demonstrated *in the Lab* with synthetic data shaped to look like DTNA's portfolio.

Lab use cases for DTNA conversations —

*L1 use case.* During the Power BI Heatmap walk-through, the seller pops into the Lab and shows the Zero Day Warranty agent running against a synthetic Cascadia warranty cluster. Live. Real agent. Real audit row. *"This is what your warranty-traceability agent looks like operating live; the heatmap I just showed you tells you where to deploy this pattern at DTNA."*

*L2 use case.* During the Wave 1 SOW conversation, the Lab is the *design substrate.* The team designs DTNA Wave 1 in the Lab with DTNA's input on data shapes and KPI envelope. *The L2 SOW proposes promotion of the Lab-validated pattern into DTNA's tenant.*

*L3 use case.* The Lab is the test-and-rehearse platform for new Services before they reach DTNA's production tenant.

*Independence boundary.* The Lab is *Deloitte tenant, synthetic data.* No DTNA data enters the Lab. Promotion to DTNA's tenant is an L2 motion under DTNA's separate Microsoft licensing.

### One disagreement

**KEVEN:** Pushback.

**MARCUS:** Let me push on the *L4 fleet-customer-transformation* framing. Specifically — I think the *L4 timeline at DTNA is genuinely 5-10 years* because the fleet-customer integration depth required is multi-year by nature. *Account Team shouldn't pitch L4 to current DTNA leadership as a near-term outcome.*

**KEVEN:** Go.

**MARCUS:** Three reasons.

One — *fleet-customer data sovereignty is structural.* J.B. Hunt's operational data is J.B. Hunt's. Multi-tenant deep-integration architecture requires the customer's CIO and CCO to sign off on each integration. *That's multi-quarter work per major fleet customer.*

Two — *DTNA's product roadmap for the connected platform is the rate-limiting factor.* L4 depth requires Detroit Connect platform capabilities that are still on the product roadmap. The agent layer can't move faster than the underlying platform supports.

Three — *the competitive dynamics aren't yet at L4-pressure.* No competitor OEM is currently operating at L4-equivalent depth with the major fleets. So DTNA leadership doesn't have a "we'll lose if we don't move now" forcing function. *The strategic case is real; the urgency case isn't yet.*

So the *honest framing* — *"L4 is the multi-year strategic horizon."* Account Team should mention it but not lead with it. *L2 and L3 are where the next-three-year conversation lives.*

**KEVEN:** Agree. *Lead with L2 and L3 in conversation. Position L4 as the strategic horizon, not the near-term ask.*
