## The conversation

### Why marketing economics rival production economics

**KEVEN:** Frame the why.

**EDEN:** A theatrical-tentpole marketing budget can equal or exceed production budget. *Marketing is half the title's investment.* Yet marketing decisions historically operate on shorter cycles than production decisions — campaigns develop in weeks, trailer cuts iterate in days, premiere strategy adapts based on competitive landscape. *Decision velocity matters enormously in marketing.*

Plus distribution decisions — windowing, international release sequencing, awards strategy — shape revenue capture per title. *Bad windowing leaves money on the table. Strong awards-campaign management can extend a specialty release's life by months.*

Plus rights and licensing — clearance work for archive content reuse, music synchronisation, talent-likeness use — is high-volume legal-and-creative work that has been historically labour-intensive.

Six plays this episode address each of these areas. Four plays on marketing-and-distribution; two on rights-and-licensing.

### Play one — Trailer Performance & Audience Testing Agent

**EDEN:** Play one.

**KEVEN:** Trailer testing is the practice of putting trailer cuts through audience-research panels and tracking response. Comparable trailer performance, audience-segment-response, predicted-opening-weekend-correlation. Studios invest heavily in trailer testing because trailer effectiveness shapes opening weekend.

The pain point. *Trailer-test results arrive in batches — 48-hour turnaround from a research vendor.* By the time results are reviewed, the trailer-house has often started next-cut iteration. *Composition of trailer-test results with social-signal, competitive-trailer-performance, and audience-segment context* is human work today.

The agent strategy. Trailer-performance agent composes test-vendor results, social-signal once trailers launch, comparable-trailer-performance benchmarks, and competitive-landscape positioning. *Surfaces composed-view to the marketing executive within hours of results landing.* Marketing leadership decides on cut iteration and placement strategy.

The architectural shape. Cross-vendor canonical for trailer-test data. Real-Time Intelligence for social-signal post-launch. Gold per-title trailer-performance composite.

The KPI signal. *Time-to-composed-trailer-insight compression 70-80 percent.* *Decision-velocity on cut iteration.* *Trailer-effectiveness improvement* measured as opening-weekend-correlation.

### Play two — Marketing Campaign Optimisation Agent

**KEVEN:** Play two.

**EDEN:** Marketing campaign optimisation across channels. *Theatrical-tentpole campaigns run across* — broadcast TV, digital video, social platforms, programmatic display, OOH (out-of-home), influencer activations, partnership marketing, talent press, premiere strategy, in-cinema trailer placement. *Per-channel attribution is partial; cross-channel optimisation is more partial.*

The pain point. *Marketing budgets allocated at campaign-launch* often don't get reallocated based on performance signal until late in the campaign cycle. Cross-channel attribution is sufficient to justify spend post-campaign but not sufficient to optimise during-campaign.

The agent strategy. Campaign-optimisation agent reads continuously across channel-performance signal, audience-segment response, competitive landscape, and creative-effectiveness data. *Recommends within-campaign reallocations.* Marketing-operations team reviews and approves spend shifts.

The architectural shape. Cross-channel canonical at Silver. Gold per-campaign performance-and-trajectory view. MCP tools span media-buying systems and audience-research platforms.

The KPI signal. *Within-campaign reallocation velocity.* *Marketing ROI improvement 8-15 percent* on covered campaigns. *Cross-channel attribution maturity.*

### Play three — Theatrical-vs-Streaming Windowing Decision Agent

**EDEN:** Play three.

**KEVEN:** Windowing decisions have become *strategic, not procedural.* Theatrical-only vs theatrical-plus-streaming-30-days vs streaming-direct decisions reshape revenue, marketing economics, and talent relationships. *Each decision is now an executive-committee discussion per title.*

The pain point. *Windowing-decision substrate composes across* — title-specific factors (genre, audience, budget, talent), market factors (competitive landscape, theatrical environment), strategic factors (Disney+ subscriber-acquisition value of a tentpole release), talent-contract factors (theatrical-vs-streaming compensation provisions). *Composing these into a structured decision-support view is largely manual.*

The agent strategy. Windowing-decision-support agent composes the factors across title and market dimensions. Surfaces composite-decision view to the windowing committee. *The committee decides;* the agent does not recommend the decision.

The architectural shape. Cross-segment canonical — title performance, audience signal, competitive landscape, talent commitments. Gold per-title windowing-decision composite. MCP tools span distribution, marketing, talent-business-affairs, and strategy systems.

The KPI signal. *Windowing-decision cycle compression.* *Decision-quality variance reduction across the windowing committee.* *Post-decision execution quality* — marketing and distribution alignment.

**EDEN:** And the strategic frame —

**KEVEN:** *Windowing is the most strategically sensitive marketing-and-distribution decision Studios leadership makes.* The agent supports the committee; the committee decides. *Critical: the agent doesn't pretend to know the strategic context the committee brings.* It composes data; the committee adds strategic judgement.

### Play four — Awards Campaign Management Agent

**KEVEN:** Play four.

**EDEN:** Awards campaign management is *year-round operational work*, peaking during Oscar and Emmy seasons. Searchlight Pictures' specialty-theatrical business *depends* on awards-season performance for theatrical viability. *Awards campaigns coordinate* — academy-screening events, talent press, FYC (For Your Consideration) advertising, industry-relationship management, festival positioning, eligibility tracking.

The pain point. *Awards campaign coordination spans many touchpoints* and has tight calendar windows. Eligibility deadlines, screening windows, talent-availability for events, competitive campaigns from other studios. *Composition across these dimensions* is the awards-campaign team's daily work.

The agent strategy. Awards-campaign agent reads campaign-event calendar, eligibility-tracking systems, talent-availability, FYC-advertising performance, social-and-industry sentiment, competitive-campaign signal. *Composes campaign-status view continuously.* Awards-team approves campaign-decision recommendations.

The architectural shape. Awards-system canonical — campaign events, eligibility tracking, talent calendar, FYC advertising. Gold per-campaign trajectory view.

The KPI signal. *Awards-campaign coordination velocity.* *Nomination-and-win conversion rate.* *Specialty-theatrical viability for Searchlight* — measured via per-title theatrical extension months.

**KEVEN:** And the Searchlight-specific note —

**EDEN:** *Searchlight is the most natural early-adopter for this play* because awards strategy is structurally critical to the Searchlight brand. *The President of Searchlight* and the Awards Campaign Director are the buyers. Other studios (Walt Disney Pictures awards work, 20th Century awards work) follow.

### Play five — Rights Compliance & Content Reuse Agent

**EDEN:** Play five. Rights and licensing.

**KEVEN:** Disney's content library is enormous. *Reusing archive content* — for trailers, marketing materials, behind-the-scenes content, anniversary releases, theme-park integrations — requires rights clearance work. Music rights, talent rights, footage rights, image rights. *Each reuse is a clearance project.*

The pain point. *Rights clearance work is labour-intensive and slow.* Disney's business-affairs and legal teams handle clearances title-by-title. The composition of rights status across a library is partial; clearance work happens reactively as projects need it.

The agent strategy. Rights-compliance agent reads continuously across rights-management systems, talent-contract repositories, music-licensing systems, and footage-rights databases. *Maintains continuous rights-status view of the library.* Surfaces rights-availability for any candidate reuse project.

The architectural shape. Rights-system canonical at Silver with heavy Purview governance for sensitive contract data. Gold per-title rights-status view. *PII propagation discipline* is critical for talent-contract data.

The KPI signal. *Rights-clearance cycle compression 50-70 percent.* *Reuse-opportunity surfaced rate.* *Compliance-risk reduction* — earlier detection of expired or restricted-use rights.

### Play six — Music Synchronisation Clearance Agent

**EDEN:** Play six. Music sync clearance.

**KEVEN:** Music synchronisation licensing is a specific subset of rights work. *Every musical cue in a film or trailer requires clearance* — from the music publisher (for the composition) and from the record label (for the recording). *Disney's music-clearance work is high-volume* — soundtrack assembly, trailer scoring, marketing-content scoring all require sync clearance.

The pain point. *Music-clearance turnaround time* affects creative-and-marketing flexibility. Music supervisors, business-affairs leadership, and external licensing agencies coordinate per-cue. The cycle from "we want this song" to "cleared and licensed" can run weeks.

The agent strategy. Music-sync-clearance agent reads continuously across music-publisher and record-label clearance systems, internal-licensing trackers, and clearance-history databases. *Surfaces clearance-availability and indicative pricing for candidate-cue use.* Music supervisor decides; business-affairs approves.

The architectural shape. Music-rights canonical. Gold per-project music-clearance view.

The KPI signal. *Music-clearance cycle compression.* *Music-supervisor productivity.* *Reduced clearance-related schedule risk* on tight-deadline projects (trailers, marketing materials).

### Cross-cutting observations

**KEVEN:** Step back.

**EDEN:** Three things.

One — *Plays 1, 2, and 4 share the marketing-data foundation.* Trailer testing, campaign optimisation, awards campaign management all use audience-research and campaign-performance data. *Wave 1 in one play substantially pays for Wave 2 in adjacent marketing plays.*

Two — *Plays 5 and 6 share the rights-data foundation.* Rights compliance and music sync are both rights-management work. Compounding asset thesis applies.

Three — *Play 3 (windowing) is the most strategically visible play but slowest to greenlight.* The windowing committee is the most senior decision-making body in the studios. *Don't lead with Play 3.* Lead with Play 1 or Play 2 to build operational credibility; bring Play 3 to leadership once credibility is established.

### One disagreement

**KEVEN:** Pushback.

**EDEN:** The *windowing-decision play.* I think this play is *Wave 3 minimum* — not Wave 2 even. *Windowing is too strategically sensitive and too senior* to be an early-stage APEX engagement at Studios. The executive committee that makes windowing decisions includes the Disney Entertainment Chairperson and the parent-company senior leadership. *They will not engage with an agentic-AI play they haven't seen working elsewhere first.*

**KEVEN:** And the alternative —

**EDEN:** Wave 1 = Plays 1 and 2 (trailer testing and campaign optimisation). Wave 2 = Plays 4, 5, 6 (awards, rights, music). *Wave 3 = Play 3 (windowing)* — once the operational track record is established. Don't lead with windowing.

**KEVEN:** Agree. *Pace the strategic plays behind the operational plays.*
