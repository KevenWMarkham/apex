### The Sellers Dream — what it is

**JORDAN:** OK. Start with the Dream. What is it?

**KEVEN:** The Sellers Dream is — *one diagram, four levels of revenue, climbed by one seller, against one Deloitte industry practice, over multiple years.* That's the whole thing.

Picture it as a ladder. Bottom rung — *Level One.* A Power BI Opportunity Heatmap, given to the practice leader as a free gift. Five days of effort. Twenty-five to seventy-five thousand dollars of wedge revenue. The practice leader sees, on their own .pbix, *every place in their portfolio where APEX would compress delivery.* That's the cold-open.

Second rung — *Level Two.* The first Wave 1 agent deployment. Five hundred thousand to two million dollars of services revenue. One use case. One agent. One HITL gate. Ninety days. BVA-backed. *The first real SOW.* That's the wedge converting to a deal.

Third rung — *Level Three.* The B2B Practice-Wide expansion. Five to twenty million dollars. A practice-level Agentic Services Center of Excellence. The seller's footprint has gone from one Service to a fleet — supplier agents, partner agents, channel agents, contract agents — across the practice.

Fourth rung — *Level Four.* B2C Transformation. Twenty to fifty million dollars and up. Consumer-facing agents. Loyalty. Service. One-to-one personalisation. The practice has become a *Microsoft-native, APEX-native, multi-wave annuity.* That's the Dream.

**JORDAN:** And the four transitions —

**KEVEN:** The four transitions — and this is the part I want every seller to internalise — each transition is *unlocked by a single sentence* the seller delivers to the practice leader.

Transition one — L0 to L1 — *the cold open.* *"I'll build you a Power BI Opportunity Heatmap for your portfolio. Five days. Free. You own the .pbix."*

Transition two — L1 to L2 — *the wedge unlock.* *"Now you can see every place APEX would compress your delivery — want to act on the top three?"*

Transition three — L2 to L3 — *the Wave-1-proved unlock.* *"Wave 1 returned X in ninety days against Y of services spend. Want to scale this pattern across the practice?"*

Transition four — L3 to L4 — *the consumer unlock.* *"Your B2B is humming. When do we go to your clients' consumers?"*

**JORDAN:** Memorise those four sentences.

**KEVEN:** Memorise those four sentences. They're how the ladder climbs itself.

### Why this changes the seller's job

**JORDAN:** And the implication for the seller —

**KEVEN:** The implication is — *the seller's mental model of an engagement changes.* It's no longer *"win the deal, deliver the deal, finish the deal."* It's — *"climb the ladder.* Cold-open with a gift. Convert to Wave 1. Scale to B2B fleet. Open the B2C door. Each transition unlocked by a single sentence. Each rung worth more than the prior."

That's a different selling motion than the one most consulting sellers have ever been taught. The closest analog is the SaaS land-and-expand motion — start small, prove value, expand. But the APEX version is *enterprise-services-flavoured* — with a Deloitte SOW at each rung, Microsoft platforms attaching from L2 upward, and Independence preserved structurally throughout.

**JORDAN:** And the connection to the Sellers Guide content from Episodes One through Seven —

**KEVEN:** The Sellers Guide content is *what fills each rung.* The four Microsoft pillars from Episode Three — those are the *technical* substance of what you're selling at L2 and above. The seven industry Practices from Episode Four — those are *where* the ladder is climbed (within RC, HLS, ER, AXLE, TMT, TH, ICE). The commercial arc from Episode Two — wave envelope, value chain, oversight spectrum, ROI math — that's the *commercial substance* inside each rung.

The Dream is the *outer frame.* The Sellers Guide content is *what's inside the frame.* You need both.

### The Six Growth Platforms — Pillars expanded

**JORDAN:** OK. Move to the platforms. We covered four pillars in Episode Three — Fabric, Foundry, Copilot, Purview. The Handbook says *six.* What did we miss?

**KEVEN:** Good question. The Sellers Guide Four Pillars model was — Fabric, Foundry, Copilot, Purview. It worked. It was useful for the architectural conversation. The Sellers Handbook's Six Growth Platforms model *extends* the four with two more — *Microsoft Entra* and *Dynamics 365.*

Let me explain why those two became first-class platforms in the seller's vocabulary.

**Entra.** In the Four Pillars model, identity was *implicit* — assumed to be solved by the platform. In the Six Growth Platforms model, *identity is its own selling motion.* The reason — Microsoft has invested heavily in Entra ID Governance, in Entra Verified ID, in Conditional Access, and increasingly in *workload identity for agents.* These are *revenue-influence opportunities* for the seller — the client buys Entra capacity for these capabilities, and the seller's APEX recommendation drives that demand. Treating Entra as a *platform* rather than as *plumbing* gives the seller leverage in conversations with the CISO and the IT-Identity organisation.

**Dynamics 365.** In the Four Pillars model, Dynamics was *adjacent* — outside the agentic AI conversation. In the Six Growth Platforms model, *Dynamics is the platform where consumer-facing and customer-service agents are surfaced.* Sales Copilot. Service Copilot. Customer Insights. *The L4 B2C Transformation* — the top rung of the Sellers Dream — *largely happens through Dynamics 365.* If you don't position Dynamics as a platform, you don't have the L4 conversation.

**JORDAN:** So the move from four to six is *commercially* motivated.

**KEVEN:** Commercially motivated. The Four Pillars covered the *architecture* of Wave 1 agentic deployment. The Six Growth Platforms covers the *full revenue arc* from wedge to L4 annuity.

### Walking the six platforms in seller terms

**JORDAN:** Walk me through all six in plain seller language.

**KEVEN:** Fast tour. Each one in two sentences.

**Fabric — the data plane.** Where the client's data lands, gets canonicalised, and is shaped for agent consumption. Every APEX engagement attaches Fabric capacity from L2 onward — usually F64 for Wave 1, scaling to F128 or F256 in later waves.

**Foundry — the agent plane.** Where APEX agents run in production. Hosts the Agent Framework SDK, the Agent Service runtime, the safety planes, the evaluation planes. Foundry consumption attaches alongside Fabric from L2 onward.

**Copilot — the experience plane.** Where end users *meet* the agents. Microsoft 365 Copilot for knowledge workers. Copilot Studio for citizen-developer agent extensions. Sales/Service Copilot for Dynamics-front-doored workflows. Attaches from L2 with M365 Copilot licenses; deepens at L3 and L4.

**Purview — the trust plane.** Where audit, classification, lineage, and DLP enforcement live. Required from L2 onward — the CCO conversation depends on Purview being present. Often attached even earlier — sometimes at L1 if the heatmap requires sensitivity classification.

**Entra — the identity plane.** Where workload and human identities live and the governance posture for them gets enforced. Conditional access. Verified ID. Agent identity. Attaches from L2 onward and increasingly central as the practice climbs.

**Dynamics 365 — the consumer plane.** Where consumer-facing and customer-service workflows live. Sales Copilot. Service Copilot. Customer Insights. The platform where L4 B2C Transformation primarily lands.

**JORDAN:** And the composition —

**KEVEN:** The composition — for L2 onwards, all six attach. The dollar weight shifts as the practice climbs. At L2, Fabric and Foundry dominate consumption. At L3, Logic Apps and the Azure AI Agent Service grow. At L4, Dynamics 365 and the M365 Copilot per-seat licenses become the largest line items.

The single sentence the seller carries — *"From L2 onward, the client licenses six Microsoft platforms directly. Deloitte's services SOW is independent of those licenses. Two contracts. Always."*

### A reading I want to do

**KEVEN:** I want to read a passage from the Sellers Handbook. It's the framing of *why* the Six Growth Platforms matter to a seller.

**JORDAN:** Read it.

**KEVEN:** [reading]

*"The seller's job is not to position any one platform. The seller's job is to position the composition. A seller who pitches Fabric in isolation has Fabric envy from the client's data team. A seller who pitches the composition has the practice leader nodding because the composition is what delivers the outcome the leader wants. Composition is the asset. The six platforms are the instrument set."*

[pause]

**JORDAN:** *Composition is the asset. The six platforms are the instrument set.* Good frame.

**KEVEN:** Good frame. It also distinguishes APEX selling from pure-Microsoft selling. *Microsoft AEs sell platforms.* The seller who's positioning APEX sells *the composition.* The two are complementary, not competitive — but they're different motions.

### One disagreement

**JORDAN:** OK. Push back time.

**KEVEN:** Go.

**JORDAN:** The expansion from four to six pillars — *I'm not entirely persuaded.* In a thirty-minute first meeting with a CIO, naming six platforms is *too many.* The CIO will glaze. The four-pillar pitch from Episode Three is *more landable* in the room. I think the six-platform model is right for the *seller's internal mental model* and the seller's pursuit-planning, but in the actual client meeting, *lead with four. Bring up Entra and Dynamics when they're relevant.*

**KEVEN:** I think that's the right pragmatic point. The *internal* model is six platforms. The *external* opening is four. You introduce Entra when the CISO joins the meeting. You introduce Dynamics when the customer-experience conversation opens. You don't pitch all six at minute one of meeting one.

**JORDAN:** Good. *Six platforms in your head, four pillars in the room — let the additional two earn their introduction.*

**KEVEN:** Yes.

Two — *the Six Growth Platforms extend the Four Pillars to cover the full revenue arc.* Fabric. Foundry. Copilot. Purview. Entra. Dynamics 365. Lead with four; let the other two earn their introduction.
