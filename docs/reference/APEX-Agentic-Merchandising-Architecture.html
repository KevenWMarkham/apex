§ 1 · Channel topology

One marketplace. One Default. Five plug-in Channels at launch.

The marketplace is structured the way the Telco's existing content bundle is structured. Default Telco Services — the Home Channel — ship with every subscriber and anchor the household state machine. Every other Channel is plug-in: a brand-operated set of sub-agents bundled or à-la-carte on the same monthly Telco bill, settled with the partner brand on the back end. New Channels (Health, Finance, additional verticals) onboard without changing the substrate.

§ 2 · APEX five planes — Marketplace view

The five planes seen from the Channel Store.

The plane model is constant across Practices and across the new Channel-marketplace construct. What changes is the data shapes (Channel + ChannelSubscription added to TMTML), the persona cast (the household replaces the enterprise as primary persona), and the surface patterns (mobile-first HITL replaces wallboard-first dashboards).

Plane 1Experience

What the plane does. Surfaces named-persona household decision moments. Mobile push, "My Channels" surface in the Telco app, voice-intent on smart speakers, bill line-items, contact-center CSR views (consent-bounded).

Marketplace specialization "My Channels" view (subscribe / pause / cancel any Channel in one tap); HITL Adaptive Cards for purchase / rebooking / allocation-hold approvals; orchestrator-routing transparency ("Routing to Walmart — change to Costco?"). Primary persona is the Household Primary Account Holder, not an enterprise role.
Plane 2Decision

What the plane does. Hosts the agents and HITL gates. Each Channel publishes sub-agents (orchestrator + 4–8 specialists). The Home Orchestrator (HOM-99) sits above all Channels and routes household intents to the appropriate Channel's sub-agent.

Marketplace specialization Cross-Channel intent routing is first-class. Default Telco sub-agents (HOM-01..08) coordinate posture with plug-in Channels (e.g., Travel sets home to vacation-mode; Automobile triggers HOM-07 telematics for UBI quotes). HITL gates declared per-Channel; consent grants gate per-scope.
Plane 3Runtime

What the plane does. The orchestration engine. Reads the per-Channel manifests, dispatches agents in the declared archetype pattern, routes through the policy + consent + state-rules layer.

Marketplace specialization Per-Channel latency tiers: Default Telco runs sub-minute for IoT events; Travel runs sub-second for IROPS alerts; Retail runs near-real-time for inventory + pricing; CPG runs second-scale for allocation drops. Same archetype, different SLO. CAF Dynamic Orchestrator + A2A Swarm + MCP partner endpoints carry the dispatch.
Plane 4Integration

What the plane does. Open MCP partner protocol (apex.tmt.mcp.partner.v1) + vertical extensions. Connectors to household IoT (Samsung, LG, Nest, Ring), to in-vehicle telematics (Toyota, Ford, GM), to brand APIs (AA, Marriott, Walmart, AutoNation, Progressive, Sazerac).

Marketplace specialization Partner-MCP onboarding is the only integration footprint per Channel partner. Every brand implements the open spec once; lists across every participating Telco. Bilateral integration tax = zero. Schema canonicalization to TMTML extended for Channel + Trip + Booking + RetailOrder + Vehicle + CellarItem + ChannelSubscription.
Plane 5Ledger

What the plane does. 14-field audit row + per-household vault + consent ledger + Channel-billing settlement records. Composes the audit row from CAF LangFuse traces + Channel context.

Marketplace specialization Household vault is the per-tenant ledger root (customer-held KMS key). Channel-subscription + per-Channel-action audit rows feed Telco billing settlement to partner brands. Regulatory evidence per Channel: HOM (CPNI/PHI/PII), TRV (loyalty CPNI), RTL (PHI for RTL-02), MOB (CPNI for embedded SIM), AUT (FCRA + FTC CARS Rule), BEV (state ABC compliance).
§ 3 · Canonical schema — TMTML + Channel extensions

One Practice schema family, one set of Channel extensions.

TMTML — the APEX canonical schema family for TMT — extends with marketplace primitives (Household, Vault, Channel, ChannelSubscription) plus per-Channel reference and event entities. Every Channel agent reads and writes against TMTML, not the underlying partner SoR — that is what makes Channels portable across Telcos and Channel partnerships substitutable without manifest rewrites.

CAF substrate (unchanged)

The marketplace runs on the same CAF substrate APEX-TMT runs on today — AEF LLM Gateway, A2A Swarm, Dynamic Orchestrator, Guardrails (GSO + Prompt Eval), 301-tier agents, Margin Maker blueprint pattern, LangFuse + Registry. No CAF capability is reinvented for Channels. Each Channel manifest references CAF capabilities by tag (cap:inventory-stream, cap:irops-disruption-stream, cap:credit-pull-soft, cap:age-verification); CAF Registry resolves to the live tier-301 agent.

A2A is the cross-Channel bridge

Cross-Channel scenarios are A2A-native — a Travel-x-Automobile insurance scenario (rental coverage during a trip), a Mobility-x-Retail service-routing decision (Walmart TLE vs Toyota dealer), an Automobile-x-Home charging-coordination decision are all A2A sessions. One APEX scenario manifest dispatches across two Channel agent fleets, CAF A2A routes, the audit row composes from both Channels' traces. APEX never re-implements that primitive.

Why brands accept the marketplace

Each plug-in partner gets four things they cannot build alone: (1) direct billing on the Telco invoice — no card-on-file friction, ~2% payment-fail rate vs ~8% direct; (2) verified-context household signal (calendar, eldercare, vehicle telematics) brand-direct can never see; (3) multi-Telco distribution from a single MCP integration — build once, list across every Telco partner; (4) regulated-utility brand-permission posture — the Telco's privacy-regulation envelope protects the partner from headline risk. None of these is replicable by any single brand acting alone.

§ 10 · Next steps

From reference architecture to engagement.

  1. Pick the W1 Telco partner. AT&T or T-Mobile is the highest-leverage Default-Telco anchor — broadest household reach + most mature billing+bundle infrastructure. Charter / Verizon FIOS as W2 candidates given prior FIOS-of-digital-content precedent.
  2. Stand up the Default Home Channel. 9 agents (HOM-01..08 + HOM-99), vault + customer-held KMS, "My Channels" surface, Adaptive Card HITL framework, shadow-run on partner IoT data (Samsung / LG / Whirlpool / Nest / Ring / Dexcom).
  3. Land the first plug-in. Travel Channel with AA + Marriott + Expedia + Airbnb is the recommended W2 wedge — high-emotional-stakes IROPS recovery + bundle muscle parallel to streaming.
  4. Open the partner-MCP onboarding lane. Publish apex.tmt.mcp.partner.v1 as an open spec. Walk Walmart, Toyota, AutoNation, Cox, Progressive, Sazerac through the channel-design workshop + MCP-implementation plan + commercial-terms negotiation.
  5. Walk the cross-Channel scenarios. Home-x-Travel vacation continuity, Mobility-x-Retail service routing, Automobile-x-Home EV-charging are the proof points that demonstrate the marketplace is more than the sum of its Channels.
  6. Preserve independence posture. All marketplace commercial materials use "Deloitte's Microsoft practice," "DMTSP," "Microsoft platform capabilities," "Microsoft-native deployment." The terms "partner," "alliance," and "strategic alliance" do not appear.
Independence note

Several anchor partner brands have audit-firm relationships that affect engagement structuring (notably some Telco accounts, AutoNation, and the OEMs). Before any W1 Default-Telco contract or W2 plug-in-partner-onboarding contract goes to drafting, confirm Deloitte's audit posture relative to each named brand and route the SOW through the Independence office. The APEX marketplace pattern is independence-safe by design; specific engagements are not assumed — they are designed.
