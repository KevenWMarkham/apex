# Show Bible · The Disney Studios Account Podcast

Internal Deloitte Account Team content specifically about The Walt Disney Studios business and its six component studios. Design rules below add to (don't replace) the Trilogy and Disney Account show bibles.

---

## 1. The conversational design rules

All rules from the Trilogy and prior Account podcasts apply:

- **No chapter or section number citations in dialogue.** Hosts know the framework.
- **Historical and industry context opens each episode** — 3-5 minutes setting up the studios-specific context for the topic.
- **One concept developed fully (4-6 min) before transitioning.** No topic-switching.
- **Real disagreement between hosts** in every episode — Keven and Eden have different priors.
- **One quote-and-react moment** per episode from primary sources (Disney IR materials, industry trade press, academic film/production research, or APEX framework documents).
- **No announced segments.**
- **`Further Reading` section** in each .md.

## 2. Studios-specific style additions

### Rule 1 · Use studio brand names correctly

The six studios — Walt Disney Pictures, Pixar, Marvel Studios, Lucasfilm, 20th Century Studios, Searchlight — are real, distinct brands with distinct leadership, distinct cultures, distinct production rhythms. Use the brand name when discussing the studio-specific operation. *"Pixar's animation pipeline"* is different from *"Marvel's VFX pipeline"* even though both are content production at scale.

### Rule 2 · Roles, not invented names

When discussing studio leadership, use **titles and roles**, not invented individual names. *"The President of Marvel Studios."* *"The Chief Creative Officer of Pixar."* *"The Chairperson of Walt Disney Studios."* These are real roles that the Account Team can identify with publicly available information; we don't need to put words in named individuals' mouths.

### Rule 3 · The studios are a portfolio of bets

Studios economics are project-by-project. Each greenlit film or series is a discrete production with its own budget, schedule, and risk profile. Hosts should *speak in title-level terms* — "a Marvel tentpole," "a Pixar feature," "a Lucasfilm Disney+ original," "a Searchlight specialty release." This reflects how the business actually operates.

### Rule 4 · The five strategic themes

Episodes weave through five strategic themes that pressure the studios business:

1. **Content cost inflation.** Marvel, Star Wars, and Pixar production costs have escalated. Per-title ROI is under scrutiny.
2. **Hit/miss volatility.** Some Marvel projects have underperformed audience expectations. Pixar releases have shifted between theatrical and streaming. The portfolio risk is real.
3. **Windowing decisions.** Theatrical-only vs theatrical-plus-streaming vs streaming-direct decisions reshape revenue and marketing.
4. **Talent dynamics and post-strike AI policy.** WGA, SAG-AFTRA, DGA settlements include AI-use provisions. Talent compensation and residuals shape the cost structure. AI policy with respect to creative talent is a sensitive area.
5. **Awards strategy and brand differentiation.** Awards-season performance affects subsequent talent attachment, distribution leverage, and theatrical viability for specialty content (Searchlight especially).

Every episode references at least one.

### Rule 5 · AI policy with respect to creative talent

The post-2023 strike settlements (WGA, SAG-AFTRA) include specific AI-use provisions. The Account Team should:

- Be deeply aware of the current AI-talent provisions (writing-credit, performance-likeness, dialogue replacement)
- Position APEX plays in ways that **clearly preserve human creative authorship** at the production-execution level
- Never propose plays that displace creative-talent roles (writers, actors, directors, animators) — propose plays that augment **operational and analytical** roles around them
- Be precise about the distinction between *creative work* (always human-authored) and *operational coordination work* (where the agent assists)

This is the most sensitive Independence-and-positioning dimension at this account. Episode 5 covers it in operational detail.

### Rule 6 · The Independence considerations

Disney is Independence-managed at the global level. Disney Studios specifically:

- Apply the two-contract model — Microsoft directly with Disney Studios; Deloitte services SOW directly
- No co-sell with Microsoft
- Coordinate with Disney audit-Independence structure
- Avoid scope that touches Disney's external financial reporting (10-K, earnings, investor communications) — that's audit-adjacent
- Plays that touch box office reporting, residual calculations, talent compensation, or financial-reporting metrics require additional Independence review
